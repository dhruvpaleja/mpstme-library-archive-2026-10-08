B   Traditional legal concepts, built on tangible notions of geography, [10]
territory, and physical evidence, face significant challenges when applied
to cyberspace.
Jurisdictional dilemmas
• The borderless nature of cyberspace: A traditional court's
jurisdiction is tied to physical boundaries. A cybercrime, however,
can be committed by a person in one country, using a server in
CO 2,   another, to harm a victim in a third. This complicates the
BL 2   determination of which court has the authority to hear the case and
which nation's laws should apply.
•   Conflict of laws: Different countries have different laws regarding
issues like online speech, data privacy, and intellectual property.
The application of a "country of origin" or "country of destination"
rule can lead to conflicting legal outcomes, creating an
environment of legal uncertainty.
Challenges to legal concepts

•   Digital evidence: Traditional rules of evidence focus on the
authenticity and integrity of physical documents. In cyberspace,
digital evidence (like emails and chat logs) can be easily altered,

•   Property and ownership: The traditional concepts of tangible
