Advantages of Alternative Investments
Diversification:
Including alternative investments in a portfolio can reduce risk by providing exposure
to assets that don’t correlate directly with the performance of traditional stocks and
bonds. This helps to smooth returns and reduce volatility.
Hedge Against Inflation:
Certain alternatives, such as real estate, commodities, and infrastructure, can provide
protection against inflation, as their prices tend to rise with inflationary pressures.
Higher Return Potential:
Alternatives like private equity, venture capital, and hedge funds often offer higher
return potential compared to traditional investments, although they come with higher
risk and illiquidity.
Access to Unique Opportunities:
Many alternative investments offer access to unique opportunities that cannot be found
in public markets. For example, private equity allows investors to support innovative
startups or buyout established companies, while art and collectibles can offer the
chance to invest in items with appreciating value over time.
Less Market Volatility:
Since alternative investments are less correlated with stock market movements, they
can provide a buffer during market downturns, offering stability in a portfolio.

1.   High Return Potential: These investments can yield higher returns than traditional

diversification, as these assets often behave differently from public equities.
3.   Access to Exclusive Investments: These investments are typically available only to
institutional investors or high-net-worth individuals (HNWI), providing access to exclusive
opportunities.

Advantages of Derivatives
1.   Risk Management: Derivatives help manage exposure to price movements in
underlying assets, reducing the financial impact of adverse events.

1.   Phishing Attacks
Cybercriminals use fraudulent emails and websites to deceive investors into revealing login
credentials or financial information. Attackers often impersonate banks, brokerage firms, or
regulatory authorities.
In India, phishing scams have targeted customers of leading banks like SBI and HDFC, with

Financial institutions handling large sums of money are lucrative targets for ransomware

and customer data management.

unauthorized data access.

Term Insurance
The term insurance plan is one of the most sought-after types of life insurance policies in
India. This is one of the types of life insurance policy in India that you can buy for a specific
period of 10, 20, 30 or more years, hence the name.
While some other types of life insurance policy offer maturity benefits, term insurance does
not. It is one reason why term insurance, being the best insurance policy in India, is
comparatively cheaper than other types of life insurance schemes.
Term insurance is pure life cover, unlike other types of life insurance policies which have a
saving component. You can also opt for a significant life cover at a lower premium as
compared to other types of life insurance policy which are costlier but have built-in saving
components.
1. Term Insurance with return of premium
A term insurance plan is amongst the types of life insurance policies that provides a death
benefit but no maturity benefit.
Term Insurance with return of premium is one of the types of term insurance plans that give
back the premiums you pay on surviving the policy period.
2.   Unit Linked Insurance Plan (ULIP)
A ULIP is one of the types of life insurance policies in India that fulfill both insurance and
investment aspects. Amongst different types of life insurance, it is the one that offers life
cover along with investment opportunities. Being one of the types of life insurance, it has
a lock-in period of five years, which makes it a long-term investment instrument that comes
with risk protection. ULIPs also allow you to balance your funds as per market dynamics.
3. Endowment Policy
Endowment policies are one of the types of life insurance policies that provide you with the
combined benefit of life insurance and savings. Along with giving you the life cover, these
types of life insurance help you save money regularly over a period to get a lump sum at
maturity.
What makes them one of the most useful types of life insurance policies is that they help
fulfill long-term goals in life. You will also get the maturity amount if you survive the policy
tenure.
Endowment policies, being one of the most appropriate types of life insurance plans, also help
you create a financial cushion for your family to meet various financial objectives in life.
4. Moneyback Policy

wealth over an extended period. However, most of the types of life insurance do not provide

Whole Life Insurance Policy
As a life insurance policyholder, you get the benefits depending on the types of life insurance
policies you have chosen. What distinguishes a whole life insurance plan from other life
insurance types is that it provides insurance coverage to the insured for the entire life, up to
100 years of age.
Typically, the death benefit, under a whole life insurance, is payable to the beneficiary in the
case of the untimely demise of the policyholder. On the other hand, you are eligible to receive
a maturity benefit under a whole life insurance policy if you cross 100 years of age.
Another significant feature of such whole life insurance plans is that some offer the option to
pay premium for the first 10-15 years while you get the benefits for the entire life.
Group Life Insurance
Group life insurance is one of the types of life insurance policies that covers a group of people
under one master policy. Such life insurance types are generally provided as part of an
employment benefit.
A unique feature of these types of life insurance products is that you will get the insurance
cover if you remain a part of the group. It is different from the individual types of life
insurance plans in which the coverage continues throughout the chosen policy tenure.
Child Insurance Policies
When it comes to life insurance types, a child plan is an investment+insurance plan that helps
you meet your child’s financial needs. A child insurance plan will help you create wealth for
your child’s future needs like education.
You can start investing in these plans from the birth of your child. You get the flexibility of
investing your hard-earned money into several funds on the basis of your financial condition
and goals in mind.

The policyholder must provide accurate information about the risk to be insured, and the
insurer must explain the terms and conditions clearly. Any misrepresentation or non-

2.   Principle of Insurable Interest:
The insured must have a financial or other kind of interest in the subject matter of the
insurance. This means they would suffer a loss if the insured event occurs.
For example, a person can insure their own property or life but cannot insure the property or
life of a stranger.
3.   Principle of Indemnity:
Insurance aims to restore the insured to the same financial position they were in before the
loss occurred, without allowing them to profit from the insurance claim.
If a car worth Rs 10,00,000 is insured and gets damaged, the insurer will cover the repair
costs or pay the current market value, not more than that amount.
4.   Principle of Contribution:
If multiple insurers cover the same risk, they should share the claim payout proportionately.
If a property is insured by two companies, both insurers will contribute to the compensation
amount in proportion to their coverage.
5.   Principle of Subrogation:
After compensating the insured for a loss, the insurer gains the right to pursue any third party
that caused the loss to recover the paid amount.
If an insured car is damaged due to another driver's negligence, the insurer can seek
reimbursement from the at-fault driver's insurer after compensating the policyholder.
6.   Principle of Loss Minimization:
The insured must take reasonable steps to minimize the loss or damage to the insured
property.
If a fire breaks out in an insured building, the policyholder should try to extinguish it if
possible, rather than letting it burn completely to claim the insurance.
7.   Principle of Proximate Cause:
The proximate cause is the primary cause of the loss. To claim insurance, the loss must be
directly linked to an insured peril.
If a policy covers fire damage but excludes flood damage, and a flood causes a fire, the
insurer must determine if the fire was directly caused by the flood to decide on the claim.
