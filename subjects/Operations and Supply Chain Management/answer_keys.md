CO-5; BL-2   b.   • Work sampling is a statistical work measurement technique that
estimates the percentage of time workers or machines spend on
specific activities by making numerous random, instantaneous
observations.
•   It determines utilization rates, sets performance standards, and   [7]
identifies bottlenecks without continuous observation.
Key Aspects of Work Sampling
• Random Observations: Observations are taken at random, not
fixed, intervals to ensure unbiased data.
********** 8 **********
--- page 9 ---
•   Large Sample Size: A high number of observations (often 500-
3,000+) are needed over a long period to ensure high confidence
levels and accuracy.
•   Applications: Used to measure productive vs. non-productive time,
establish allowances for jobs, and calculate machine utilization
(LUR - Labor Utilization Rate).
•   Advantages: Cost-effective, requires fewer man-hours than time
studies, does not require continuous observation, and is less
disruptive to workers.
•   Limitations: Not ideal for short-cycle, repetitive jobs (where time
study is better); results are less accurate if the sample size is too
small.
Steps in Work Sampling
•   Define Activities: Clearly define the tasks/activities (e.g., working,
idle, maintenance).
•   Plan the Study: Determine the required number of observations
and their random times.
• Perform Observations: Make random observations and record the
status of the worker/machine.
•   Analyze Data: Calculate the percentage of time for each activity
and apply statistical analysis.
CO-5; BL-2   c.   • Synchronous manufacturing is a production management strategy
that aligns all business functions—production, inventory, and
sales—to move at the same pace, typically guided by the Theory of
Constraints (TOC). It focuses on maximizing system-wide
throughput while minimizing inventory and operating expenses,
often using demand-driven, real-time data to create a continuous
flow. Key principles include managing bottlenecks and balancing
flow rather than capacity.
• Key Aspects of Synchronous Manufacturing:
•   Bottleneck Focus: Identifies the slowest resource
(bottleneck) and schedules it to match customer demand,
[7]
as bottleneck capacity dictates the overall system's output.
• Reduced Inventory: By using smaller batch sizes for non-
bottleneck processes, manufacturers reduce work-in-
process (WIP) inventory, while sometimes using larger
batches on bottleneck resources to maintain efficiency.
• Demand-Driven (Pull System): Unlike traditional
forecasting, synchronous manufacturing often operates on
real-time orders, reducing overstocking and improving,
faster response times.

1. Understanding Six Sigma in Supply Chain

* Six Sigma is a methodology aimed at reducing process variation and defects using the DMAIC
framework (Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, Control).

* In supply chains, it focuses on streamlining operations, improving quality, and eliminating

inefficiencies.

2. How Six Sigma Reduces Lead Times

a) Process Standardization
* Six Sigma identifies non-value-added activities and standardizes processes across procurement,
production, and distribution.

* Impact: Reduces delays caused by inconsistent procedures and manual errors.

b) Defect and Error Reduction
* By minimizing errors in orders, production, or logistics, Six Sigma prevents rework, returns, and
reprocessing.

* Impact: Speeds up the flow of goods, reducing overall lead time.

c) Streamlined Inventory Management
* Six Sigma helps optimize inventory levels and reorder points using data-driven analysis.

* Impact: Reduces waiting time for raw materials or components and avoids production stoppages.

d) Improved Supplier Performance
* Six Sigma can be extended to suppliers (e.g., Supplier Six Sigma) to enhance reliability and consistency
of incoming materials.

© Impact: Reduces delays caused by late or defective deliveries.

e) Data-Driven Decision Making
* Six Sigma emphasizes using statistical analysis to identify bottlenecks.
* Impact: Enables targeted improvements that shorten cycle times in procurement, production, and
