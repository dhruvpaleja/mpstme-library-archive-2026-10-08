Course Objective
This course aims to develop awareness and foundational skills in precision measurement
techniques essential for mechanical engineering. As industrial processes and manufacturing
technologies become more complex, precise and accurate measurements play a critical role in
ensuring quality and efficiency. This course equips students with fundamental knowledge of
measurement principles and their application in modern industry operations.
Course Outcomes
After completion of the course, students would be able to -
1. Understand the fundamental concepts of Metrology and Measurement Systems.
2. Describe the working principles and operation of various measuring instruments.
3. Apply measurement tools and techniques to assess linear and geometric forms.
4. Assess the performance of various sensors and transducers in practical applications.
5. Analyze different types of measurement errors and their impact on precision.
6. Evaluate the importance of limits, fits, and tolerances in manufacturing and their
application in design.

Measurement of Straightness, Flatness, Squareness, Parallelism, roundness,
cylindricity, coaxility, eccentricity and concentricity.

3.   Limits, Fits and Tolerances   7
Introduction, concept of interchangeability, terminologies, limits and

System response, Time delay. Errors in measurement, Classification of errors.

Introduction, mechanical detector-transducer elements, classification of
transducer, selection of transducer. Resistive transducer: strain gauges, linear
and angular motion potentiometers. Variable inductance transducer: LVDT,
capacitive transducer, Piezoelectric Transducer, hall effect transducer,
thermoelectric transducer, photoelectric transducer.
6.   Flow, Force and Vision based Measurements   10
Types and classification of flowmeters, Rotameter, Ultrasonic Flow meter,
Electromagnetic flow meter, selection of flowmeters.
SI units of force, hydraulic load cell, pneumatic load cell, elastic force devices,
and electromechanical methods of force measurement, vision-based
Measurement systems.
