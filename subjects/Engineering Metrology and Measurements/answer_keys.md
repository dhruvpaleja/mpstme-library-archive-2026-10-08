Dynamic characteristics of a measurement system describe how an instrument responds
when the measurand changes with time. Unlike static characteristics, these indicate the
performance of the system under varying or time-dependent conditions.
Speed of Response: It is the rate at which the instrument responds to changes in input. A
faster response means the instrument quickly follows variations in the measurand.
Fidelity: Fidelity is the ability of a measurement system to reproduce the changes in the

Lag (Time Delay): Lag is the delay between the input change and the corresponding output

Dynamic Error: Dynamic error is the difference between the true value of the varying

final value after a sudden change in input (step input). A smaller time constant indicates a
faster system.

Standards of length are classified based on their level of accuracy and application into
international, primary, secondary, and working standards. The international standard is

b A generalized measurement system is an arrangement of different functional elements
used to measure a physical quantity (measurand) and convert it into a form that can be
easily observed and interpreted. It typically consists of stages such as sensing,
conversion, manipulation, transmission, and presentation, all working together to provide
an accurate measurement.
The process begins with the measurand, which is the physical quantity to be measured,
such as temperature, pressure, or displacement. This is detected by the primary sensing
element, which directly responds to the measurand and produces an initial signal. This
signal is then passed to the variable conversion element, which converts it into a more
suitable form, often an electrical signal. Next, the variable manipulation element modifies
the signal by amplifying or filtering it to make it more useful. The processed signal is
then carried by the data transmission element, such as wires or cables, to the final stage.
Finally, the data presentation element displays the measured value in a readable form,
such as on a digital screen or meter.

b   Errors in measurement are the differences between the true value and the measured value
of a quantity. These errors affect the accuracy of measurements and are broadly classified
into gross errors, systematic errors, and random errors.
Gross errors are mainly caused by human mistakes such as incorrect reading of instruments,
wrong recording of data, or improper handling of equipment. These errors are usually large
and can be avoided by taking proper care, repeating measurements, and following correct
procedures. For example, noting down a wrong value from a scale or using an unsuitable
measuring instrument leads to gross error.
CO-3;   Systematic errors occur due to consistent and identifiable causes, and they affect all
SO-1   [10]
BL-3   measurements in a similar manner. These errors may arise from faulty instruments
(instrumental   errors), environmental   conditions   like temperature   or humidity
(environmental errors), or mistakes made by the observer such as parallax (observational
errors). For instance, a zero error in a Vernier caliper or expansion of a measuring scale
due to temperature changes are examples of systematic errors.
Random errors are caused by unpredictable and uncontrollable variations in the
measurement process. These errors vary in magnitude and direction and cannot be
completely eliminated. However, they can be minimized by taking multiple readings and
calculating the average value. Examples include slight fluctuations in readings due to
electrical noise or minor environmental disturbances.

A Vernier calliper consists of a main scale (fixed scale) and a vernier scale (sliding scale).

b   Explain the Hall effect transducer with principle, working, and industrial applications.
Hall Effect Transducer is a device used to measure magnetic field, current, position, or
speed based on the Hall Effect.
Principle:
The Hall effect states that when a current-carrying conductor or semiconductor is placed
in a magnetic field perpendicular to the direction of current, a voltage (called Hall
voltage) is developed across it. This voltage is proportional to the strength of the
magnetic field.
Working:
In a Hall effect transducer, a thin semiconductor material is used. When electric current
CO-2;   flows through it and a magnetic field is applied perpendicular to the current, the charge
SO-2   [10]
carriers are deflected to one side. This creates a potential difference across the material,
BL-2
known as Hall voltage. The magnitude of this voltage is proportional to the magnetic
field strength, and it is measured and converted into a usable output signal. This output
can then be used to determine quantities like current, displacement, or speed.
Industrial Applications:
Hall effect transducers are widely used in industries for various purposes. They are used
for current measurement in electrical systems without direct contact. They are also used
in position sensing in automation and robotics, speed detection in motors and rotating
machines, and proximity sensing in industrial equipment. Additionally, they are used for
magnetic field measurement and in devices like brushless DC motors and automotive
sensors.

a   Explain the working principle and applications of strain gauge and LVDT.
A strain gauge works on the principle that the electrical resistance of a conductor changes
when it is stretched or compressed. This is based on the piezoresistive effect. When a
strain gauge is bonded to a surface and that surface is subjected to force, the gauge
deforms along with it, causing a change in its length and cross-sectional area. This results
in a change in electrical resistance, which is measured using a bridge circuit (usually
Wheatstone bridge) and related to the amount of strain.
Applications: Measurement of force, stress, pressure, and weight; used in load cells,
structural testing, and mechanical components.

Construction:
It consists of a non-conductive pipe section lined with insulating material,

fluid. This voltage is detected by the electrodes and is proportional to the velocity of the
fluid. The flow rate is then calculated and displayed.

Pneumatic Load Cell:
A pneumatic load cell operates on the principle of balancing an applied force with air

pressure is adjusted until it balances the applied force, and the corresponding air pressure
is measured to determine the load. The output pressure is directly proportional to the
applied load. These load cells are highly accurate and safe in explosive environments.
