c.   Introduction
Surface roughness is made up of different types of irregularities formed during machining. These are
mainly classified as primary texture and secondary texture.
Primary Texture
•   Also called surface roughness or micro-irregularities.
•   Consists of fine, closely spaced marks produced by the cutting tool or abrasive grains.
• Caused by tool feed, tool wear, or vibration during machining.
• Measured by parameters such as Ra and Rz.
•   Example: fine tool marks on a turned surface.
Secondary Texture
•   Also known as waviness or macro-irregularities.
• Made up of larger, widely spaced irregularities superimposed on primary texture.
• Caused by machine vibration, tool chatter, or misalignment.
• Measured by parameters like Wa and Wt.
•   Example: wavy pattern caused by vibration during turning

Why Sine Bar is Not Preferred Above 45°
Sine bars are generally not preferred for measuring angles greater than 45°, due to the following
reasons:
• At high angles, the height of the slip gauge stack (h) becomes large, making the setup unstable
and prone to tipping.
•   Slight errors in height measurement cause large angular errors because the sine curve becomes
very steep beyond 45°.
•   Difficulty in maintaining firm contact between the component and sine bar.
•   Increased chance of slip gauge slippage or deformation under heavy components

b   Nano metrology is the science of measurement at the nanometer scale (1–100 nm). It deals with
accurately measuring physical quantities like length, surface roughness, and material properties at
extremely small dimensions.
Importance of Nano Dimensions
• At the nanoscale, material properties such as strength, conductivity, and reactivity change
significantly.
•   Precise measurement is essential for developing nanotechnology-based devices like sensors,
semiconductors, and MEMS/NEMS.
•   It ensures quality control and standardization in nanomanufacturing processes.
•   Accurate nano measurement supports research in materials science, biotechnology, and
quantum engineering.
Measurement Techniques
Common techniques used in nano metrology include:
• Atomic Force Microscopy (AFM): Measures surface topography at atomic resolution.
• Scanning Tunneling Microscopy (STM): Used to study atomic structures by detecting
tunneling current.
• Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM): Provides detailed surface images.
•   Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM): Used for internal structure analysis at the
nanometer level.
•   Optical and Laser Interferometry: Measures nano-scale displacements and film thickness
accurately.
Applications
•   Semiconductor and microchip fabrication.
•   Nanomaterial and thin-film characterization.
•   Quality assurance in nanomanufacturing.
•   Biomedical applications such as drug delivery systems and biosensors.

b   Principle
When monochromatic light reflects from two surfaces, the path difference between them produces
interference fringes.
•   If both surfaces are perfectly flat and parallel, straight and equally spaced fringes appear.
• Any deviation from flatness causes curvature or distortion in the fringes.
Types of fringe patterns and their indications
1. Straight and parallel fringes
o   Indicate that the test surface is perfectly flat and parallel to the reference surface.
2. Equally spaced, tilted fringes
o   Indicate that the surface is flat but inclined with respect to the reference plane.
3. Curved or circular fringes (convex surface)
o   Fringes bulge outward, indicating the surface is convex (raised at the center).
4. Curved or circular fringes (concave surface)
7   o   Fringes curve inward, indicating the surface is concave (depressed at the center).
5. Uneven or distorted fringes
o   Indicate that the surface has irregularities or waviness, showing local deviations from
flatness.

b   Interferometry is a precise optical measurement technique that uses the interference of light waves to
measure very small displacements, surface irregularities, refractive index changes, or wavelength
differences.
Basic Principle of Interference of Light
When two or more coherent light waves (having the same wavelength and a constant phase difference)
overlap, they combine to form a new light pattern called an interference pattern.
The resulting intensity at any point depends on the phase difference between the waves.
Constructive Interference:
When the crests and troughs of the two waves coincide (phase difference = 0 or multiple of 2π), their
amplitudes add up, producing bright fringes.
Destructive Interference:
When the crest of one wave coincides with the trough of another (phase difference = π), the amplitudes
cancel, producing dark fringes.
Emerging Technologies in Nano-Scale Measurement Systems:

5. Optical and Laser Interferometry

b   Interferometry is a precise optical measurement technique that uses the interference of light waves to
measure very small displacements, surface irregularities, refractive index changes, or wavelength
differences.
Basic Principle of Interference of Light
When two or more coherent light waves (having the same wavelength and a constant phase difference)
overlap, they combine to form a new light pattern called an interference pattern.
The resulting intensity at any point depends on the phase difference between the waves.
Constructive Interference:
When the crests and troughs of the two waves coincide (phase difference = 0 or multiple of 2π), their
amplitudes add up, producing bright fringes.
Destructive Interference:
When the crest of one wave coincides with the trough of another (phase difference = π), the amplitudes
cancel, producing dark fringes.
