A. Stages of Money Laundering   10
Placement
Layering
Integration
Money laundering is disguising the identity of illegally obtained money so that it would
appear to have originated from legitimate sources. Money laundering is generally used by
criminals to disguise their illicit funds, terrorists to dodge tracking or tax evaders.
Money laundering typically occurs in three key stages:
Placement: This is the initial stage where illicit funds are introduced into the financial system.
Methods may include depositing cash into banks, purchasing assets, or using gambling
facilities.
Layering: In this stage, the goal is to obscure the origins of the money through complex
layers of financial transactions. This can involve transferring funds between accounts,
converting money into different currencies, or making investments to disguise the source.
Integration: The final stage involves reintroducing the laundered money into the economy in
a way that appears legitimate. This can include using the funds to purchase property, invest in
businesses, or fund legitimate expenses.
