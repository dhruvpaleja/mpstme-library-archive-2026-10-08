Pre-requisite: Geology, Soil Mechanics, Foundation Engineering
Course Objective
This course will allow students to evaluate the importance of geology in rock mechanics practice,
classify rocks and predict their mechanical behaviour and integrate laboratory and field techniques to
determine stress-strain and strength of rocks to assess rock failure and its importance in civil
engineering.
Course Outcomes
After completion of the course, students will be able to -
1. Assess importance of geology in rock mechanics practice
2. Classify rocks based on various classification methods and predict their mechanical behavior
3. Interpret laboratory and field techniques to determine stress –strain, modulus and strength
behaviour of rocks
Detailed Syllabus
Unit   Description   Duration
Elements of Structural Geology

1   theory, geological site investigations in rocks and engineering characteristics of   03
rock masses, mapping basics; Introduction to MOVE, FieldClino MOVE and
FieldMOVE softwares (Petex).

2   rock structural   rating, geomechanics   classification (RMR)}, strength and   08

Geoengineering, classification, Deere and Miller’s Engineering classification.
In-situ stress measurement in Rocks and Rock testing
a) In-situ stress measurement in rocks
Flat Jack method, Bore-hole deformation method, Core discing method, Hydraulic
Fracturing method.
3   b) Rock testing
09
1. Laboratory testing: Compression test, Effect of L/D ratio, Determination of
Modulus of Elasticity and Poisson's Ratio, Direct and Indirect (Brazilian) Tensile
tests, Shear tests (including hollow cylinder test).
2. In-situ testing: Plate load test, Block shear test, Cable Jacking test,
Pressuremeter test.
