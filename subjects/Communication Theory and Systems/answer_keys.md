Frequency Shift Keying is a digital modulation technique in which the frequency of the
carrier signal is varied according to the digital data to be transmitted. In FSK, the binary
data stream is converted into a waveform by changing the frequency of the carrier
signal. A logic 1 is represented by one frequency, while a logic 0 is represented by
another frequency. The two frequencies used in FSK are usually separated by a fixed
frequency called the carrier frequency.
FSK can be further divided into two types: coherent and non-coherent FSK. In coherent
FSK, the carrier signal is modulated with the digital data, whereas in non-coherent
FSK, the carrier signal’s frequency is varied, but the carrier signal itself is not
modulated.

Phase Shift Keying is a digital modulation technique in which the phase of the carrier
signal is varied according to the digital data to be transmitted. In PSK, the binary data
stream is converted into a waveform by changing the phase of the carrier signal. A logic
1 is represented by one phase, while a logic 0 is represented by another phase. The two
phases used in PSK are usually separated by 180 degrees.
PSK can be further divided into three types: Binary Phase Shift Keying (BPSK),
Quadrature Phase Shift Keying (QPSK), and Differential Phase Shift Keying (DPSK).
In BPSK, the phase of the carrier signal is shifted by 0 or 180 degrees. In QPSK, the
phase of the carrier signal is shifted by 0, 90, 180, or 270 degrees. In DPSK, the phase
difference between adjacent symbols is used to represent the digital data.

Demodulation of PAM Signal

that samples a signal at symbol rate and compares it to threshold values; decision
feedback, which combines sampling with feedback from previously observed

Minimum shift keying, MSK, is a form frequency modulation based on a system
called continuous-phase frequency-shift keying.
Minimum shift keying, MSK offers advantages in terms of spectral efficiency when
compared to other similar modes, and it also enables power amplifiers to operate in
saturation enabling them to provide high levels of efficiency.
Reason for Minimum Shift Keying, MSK
It is found that binary data consisting of sharp transitions between "one" and "zero"
states and vice versa potentially creates signals that have sidebands extending out a
long way from the carrier, and this creates problems for many radio communications
systems, as any sidebands outside the allowed bandwidth cause interference to
adjacent channels and any radio communications links that may be using them.
MSK, minimum shift keying has the feature that there are no phase discontinuities
and this significantly reduces the bandwidth needed over other forms of phase and
frequency shift keying.
Minimum Shift Keying, MSK basics
The problem can be overcome in part by filtering the signal, but is found that the
transitions in the data become progressively less sharp as the level of filtering is
increased and the bandwidth reduced. To overcome this problem GMSK is often used
and this is based on Minimum Shift Keying, MSK modulation. The advantage of
which is what is known as a continuous phase scheme. Here there are no phase
discontinuities because the frequency changes occur at the carrier zero crossing
points.
When looking at a plot of a signal using MSK modulation, it can be seen that the
modulating data signal changes the frequency of the signal and there are no phase
discontinuities. This arises as a result of the unique factor of MSK that the frequency

Frequency Shift Keying is a digital modulation technique in which the
frequency of the carrier signal is varied according to the digital data to be
transmitted. In FSK, the binary data stream is converted into a waveform by
changing the frequency of the carrier signal. A logic 1 is represented by one
frequency, while a logic 0 is represented by another frequency. The two
frequencies used in FSK are usually separated by a fixed frequency called
the carrier frequency.
********** 14 **********
--- page 15 ---
FSK can be further divided into two types: coherent and non-coherent FSK.
In coherent FSK, the carrier signal is modulated with the digital data,
whereas in non-coherent FSK, the carrier signal’s frequency is varied, but
the carrier signal itself is not modulated.
Phase Shift Keying is a digital modulation technique in which the phase of
the carrier signal is varied according to the digital data to be transmitted. In
PSK, the binary data stream is converted into a waveform by changing the
phase of the carrier signal. A logic 1 is represented by one phase, while a
logic 0 is represented by another phase. The two phases used in PSK are
usually separated by 180 degrees.
PSK can be further divided into three types: Binary Phase Shift Keying
(BPSK), Quadrature Phase Shift Keying (QPSK), and Differential Phase
Shift Keying (DPSK). In BPSK, the phase of the carrier signal is shifted by
0 or 180 degrees. In QPSK, the phase of the carrier signal is shifted by 0,
90, 180, or 270 degrees. In DPSK, the phase difference between adjacent
symbols is used to represent the digital data.
