1. High Coefficient of Friction (μ): The material needs a high μ with the
pulley surface to maximize the tractive effort (grip) and minimize slip,
which is crucial for efficient power transfer.
2. High Tensile Strength: The material must withstand the high tension
developed in the tight side of the belt without failure.
3. High Flexibility and Fatigue Resistance: The belt must be highly
flexible to bend repeatedly around small pulleys at high speeds without
cracking or premature fatigue failure.
4. Low Density (Lightweight): Lighter belts minimize centrifugal tension
at high speeds, allowing more of the available tension to be used for
transmitting power.
5. Durability and Environmental Resistance: The material must resist
wear, heat, moisture, and oil to maintain performance and extend
service life.
V-Belt Structural Advantage
V-belts leverage the material's properties—especially its high coefficient of
friction and durability—through their unique wedge shape to provide
significant advantages over flat belts:
1. Enhanced Torque Capacity (Wedge Action): The primary advantage is
the wedging action. When a V-belt is tensioned, the sidewalls press into
the groove of the V-pulley. This increases the normal force between the
belt and the pulley significantly beyond what is achievable in a flat belt
(where the normal force is just the tension force). Since frictional force
is proportional to the normal force (Ff=μN), this geometric increase in
normal force results in a much greater frictional grip and thus higher
torque capacity for the same applied tension.
2. Higher Drive Efficiency (No Alignment Issues): The V-belt's structure
naturally guides the belt within the pulley groove, eliminating the need
for crowned pulleys and minimizing the risk of the belt running off.
This self-aligning feature reduces operational wear and energy lost to
constant alignment correction, contributing to higher drive efficiency
and making the drives shorter and more compact.
3. Operation at Shorter Centre Distances: Because the V-belt provides
superior grip, it can operate effectively at much shorter centre distances
and higher speed ratios than a flat belt, making the drive more compact
for a given power requirement.

(each 2 marks) Five concepts in the context of dimensional management and machine design:
Mechanical Design Concepts
i. Design Synthesis
Design Synthesis is the creative, generative phase where the designer formulates a preliminary solution.
It involves combining various technical principles and constraints (like load, function, and
environment) to conceive and shape the physical components (dimensions, material, structure) that are
intended to fulfill the required function. It answers the question, "How should the part be made?"
ii. Fits
A Fit defines the mechanical relationship between two mating parts (e.g., a shaft and a hole) based on
Q2   the difference between their sizes before assembly. It dictates the tightness or looseness of the
CO-1;
(a)   assembled joint. Fits are classified into Clearance (always loose), Transition (may be loose or tight), [10]
SO-1;
BL-2   and Interference (always tight) to control the functional objective of the joint (e.g., sliding, fixed, or
rotating).
iii. Design Analysis
Design Analysis is the systematic, quantitative evaluation of the preliminary design (the result of
synthesis). It uses engineering calculations, stress analysis, and simulation (like FEA) to verify that the
component's geometry and material will withstand the expected operational loads (force, temperature,
fatigue) and safely meet the required functional performance (strength, rigidity) without premature
failure.
