Ans   Explain how RNNs help in processing sequential language data. 5 marks
Also discuss the benefits and limitations of using deep learning models in

Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) are well-suited for processing sequential
language data because they are designed to capture dependencies between
words in a sequence. Unlike traditional models, RNNs maintain a hidden state
that carries information from previous time steps, allowing the network to
“remember” earlier words while processing the current word. This is
particularly useful in understanding the context of a sentence, where the
meaning of a word often depends on preceding words. For example, in a
customer query, the intent of the sentence can be better understood when the
sequence of words is analyzed rather than treating each word independently.
Variants like LSTM and GRU further improve this capability by handling
long-term dependencies and reducing issues like vanishing gradients. Thus,
RNNs enable chatbots to generate more context-aware and coherent
responses.
Using deep learning models in conversational AI systems offers several
benefits. They provide high accuracy and better context understanding,
enabling more natural and human-like interactions. They can handle large
volumes of diverse queries and continuously improve with more data.
Additionally, they reduce the need for manual rule-based programming,
making the system more scalable and adaptable. However, there are also
limitations. Deep learning models require large amounts of training data and
high computational resources, which can be costly. They may sometimes
produce incorrect or irrelevant responses due to lack of true understanding.
Moreover, they can struggle with rare queries or domain-specific knowledge
if not properly trained. There are also concerns related to bias, lack of
transparency, and difficulty in interpreting model decisions. Therefore, while
RNN-based models enhance chatbot performance, they must be carefully
designed and monitored for reliable use.

Q5,   A healthcare provider has collected thousands of patient feedback comments 10
(CO2,BL4,PO3) from hospital surveys and online platforms and wants to identify key themes in
patient experiences. As a Data Analyst, explain how Non-Negative Matrix
Factorization (NMF) can be used for topic modelling on this data. What
challenges might arise in implementing NMF?
Ans   Explain how Non-Negative Matrix Factorization (NMF) can be used for topic
modelling on this data. 5 marks
What challenges might arise in implementing NMF? 5 marks
Non-Negative Matrix Factorization (NMF) is an effective technique for topic
modelling in textual data such as patient feedback comments because it
decomposes a large document-term matrix into two lower-dimensional non-
negative matrices. In this context, the healthcare provider would first
preprocess the feedback data by cleaning the text, removing stop words, and
converting it into a numerical representation such as a Term Frequency–Inverse
Document Frequency (TF-IDF) matrix. NMF then factorizes this matrix into
two matrices: one representing the relationship between documents and topics,
and the other representing the relationship between topics and words. Each
topic is characterized by a set of frequently co-occurring words, allowing the
analyst to interpret themes such as “staff behavior,” “waiting time,”
“cleanliness,” or “treatment quality.” Because NMF enforces non-negativity,
the resulting topics are more interpretable compared to some other methods,
making it particularly suitable for understanding patient sentiments and
experiences.
However, several challenges may arise while implementing NMF. One major
issue is selecting the optimal number of topics, as too few topics may merge
distinct themes while too many may produce redundant or meaningless topics.
Additionally, NMF is sensitive to the quality of preprocessing; noisy, sparse,
or inconsistent text data can reduce the quality of the extracted topics. The
algorithm may also converge to local minima depending on initialization,
leading to variability in results. Another challenge is computational cost when
dealing with very large datasets, as matrix factorization can be resource-
intensive. Furthermore, interpreting topics still requires human judgment, as
some topics may not be clearly distinguishable or may overlap. Despite these
challenges, with proper preprocessing, parameter tuning, and validation, NMF

can provide valuable insights into patient feedback by uncovering hidden
thematic structures in the data.

Combining CNN models for image analysis and RNN models for text analysis
enables the digital marketing company to achieve a more comprehensive
understanding of customer feedback by leveraging both visual and textual
information. CNNs can extract visual features from product images, such as
design, color, quality, and defects, helping to understand how customers
visually perceive products. At the same time, RNNs process sequential text
data from customer reviews to capture sentiment, opinions, and contextual
meaning. When these two models are integrated, the system can correlate
what customers see with what they say. For example, a product image
showing a damaged item can be linked with negative reviews, providing
stronger evidence of quality issues. This multimodal approach improves
prediction accuracy, enhances sentiment analysis, and provides richer insights
into customer preferences, behavior, and satisfaction.

challenges. One major issue is the heterogeneity of data, as images and text
require different preprocessing techniques, model architectures, and
computational resources. Combining outputs from CNNs and RNNs into a
unified framework can be complex and may require advanced fusion
techniques. Another challenge is the high computational cost and need for
large-scale datasets to train both models effectively. Additionally, aligning
image data with corresponding text reviews is not always straightforward,
especially when the data is noisy or unstructured. There are also risks of
overfitting, increased model complexity, and difficulty in interpreting results.
Ensuring proper synchronization, scalability, and efficient deployment further
adds to the complexity.

To analyze health-related textual data effectively, HealthWatch must first
apply appropriate text preprocessing techniques to clean and standardize the
data. This begins with text cleaning, where unnecessary elements such as
HTML tags, special characters, URLs, and extra spaces are removed. Next,
tokenization is performed to split the text into words or sentences, followed
by lowercasing to ensure uniformity. Stop-word removal helps eliminate
common but less meaningful words, while stemming or lemmatization
reduces words to their base forms (e.g., “infected”, “infection” → “infect”).
Since health data often contains domain-specific terms, normalization of
medical terminology, handling abbreviations (e.g., “BP” for blood pressure),
and correcting spelling errors are also important. Additionally, techniques like
n-grams and TF-IDF can be used to convert text into numerical
representations for further analysis. These preprocessing steps improve data
quality, reduce noise, and prepare the dataset for accurate extraction of
meaningful insights.
To identify diseases, symptoms, and geographic locations, HealthWatch can
apply advanced NLP techniques such as Named Entity Recognition (NER).
NER models automatically detect and classify entities into categories like
diseases (e.g., “COVID-19”, “malaria”), symptoms (e.g., “fever”, “cough”),
and locations (e.g., cities, countries). Domain-specific NER models,
especially those trained on medical datasets (such as BioBERT or clinical
NLP models), can improve accuracy in recognizing complex medical terms.
After identifying entities, entity linking can be used to map them to
standardized medical vocabularies or databases (e.g., ICD codes or public
health registries), ensuring consistency and resolving ambiguities. This
enables HealthWatch to track the spread of diseases across regions, detect
emerging health trends, and support timely decision-making. Overall,
combining preprocessing with advanced NLP techniques allows efficient
extraction of critical information from large-scale unstructured health data.

Non-Negative Matrix Factorization (NMF) is an effective technique for topic
modelling in textual data such as patient feedback comments because it
decomposes a large document-term matrix into two lower-dimensional non-
negative matrices. In this context, the healthcare provider would first
preprocess the feedback data by cleaning the text, removing stop words, and
converting it into a numerical representation such as a Term Frequency–Inverse
Document Frequency (TF-IDF) matrix. NMF then factorizes this matrix into
two matrices: one representing the relationship between documents and topics,
and the other representing the relationship between topics and words. Each
topic is characterized by a set of frequently co-occurring words, allowing the
analyst to interpret themes such as “staff behavior,” “waiting time,”
“cleanliness,” or “treatment quality.” Because NMF enforces non-negativity,
the resulting topics are more interpretable compared to some other methods,
making it particularly suitable for understanding patient sentiments and
experiences.
However, several challenges may arise while implementing NMF. One major
issue is selecting the optimal number of topics, as too few topics may merge
distinct themes while too many may produce redundant or meaningless topics.
Additionally, NMF is sensitive to the quality of preprocessing; noisy, sparse,
or inconsistent text data can reduce the quality of the extracted topics. The
algorithm may also converge to local minima depending on initialization,
leading to variability in results. Another challenge is computational cost when
dealing with very large datasets, as matrix factorization can be resource-
intensive. Furthermore, interpreting topics still requires human judgment, as
some topics may not be clearly distinguishable or may overlap. Despite these
challenges, with proper preprocessing, parameter tuning, and validation, NMF
********** 4 **********
--- page 5 ---
can provide valuable insights into patient feedback by uncovering hidden
thematic structures in the data.

To help InfoHive evaluate the different topics they generally address in their news articles over the past

1. Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) for Topic Identification
What is LDA?
LDA (Latent Dirichlet Allocation) is an unsupervised machine learning technique for topic modeling. It
assumes that each document (in this case, each news article) is a mixture of topics, and each topic is a
mixture of words. LDA helps in discovering the underlying thematic structure of a large collection of
text data by uncovering hidden topics.
Steps for Applying LDA for Topic Identification:
1. Preprocessing the Data:
o Text Cleaning: Remove stop words, punctuation, and perform stemming or
lemmatization.
o Vectorization: Convert the news articles into a Bag of Words (BoW) or TF-IDF matrix
(term-frequency inverse document frequency).
2. Apply LDA:

What is NMF?
Non-Negative Matrix Factorization (NMF) is a dimensionality reduction technique, also used for topic
modelling. It factors a document-term matrix into two lower-rank non-negative matrices: one
representing topics and the other representing the relationship between documents and topics. NMF can
reveal topics based on the TF-IDF representation of documents and is generally easier to interpret than
LDA.
Steps for Applying NMF for Topic Identification:

o As with LDA, the articles need to be cleaned (removing stopwords, punctuation, and
applying stemming/lemmatization).
o TF-IDF Vectorization: Convert the corpus into a TF-IDF matrix, which helps capture
the importance of words in each article relative to the entire corpus.
2. Apply NMF:
o Choose the number of topics (like LDA, you need to specify the number of topics in
advance).
o Apply the NMF algorithm, which decomposes the TF-IDF matrix into two non-negative
matrices: one for topics and one for documents. Each topic is represented by a set of
important terms, and each document is represented by the topics it covers.
3. Evaluate the Topics:
o NMF will output the top words for each topic, which can be interpreted to label the
topics.
o You can also examine the document-topic matrix, which will show how much each
article relates to the different topics.
4. Interpretation:

Response to Q3:
To help InfoHive evaluate the different topics they generally address in their news articles over the past
two months, we can opt two powerful techniques for topic identification
1. Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) for Topic Identification
What is LDA?
LDA (Latent Dirichlet Allocation) is an unsupervised machine learning technique for topic modeling. It
assumes that each document (in this case, each news article) is a mixture of topics, and each topic is a
mixture of words. LDA helps in discovering the underlying thematic structure of a large collection of
text data by uncovering hidden topics.
Steps for Applying LDA for Topic Identification:
1. Preprocessing the Data:
o Text Cleaning: Remove stop words, punctuation, and perform stemming or
lemmatization.
o Vectorization: Convert the news articles into a Bag of Words (BoW) or TF-IDF matrix
(term-frequency inverse document frequency).
2. Apply LDA:
o Choose the number of topics. This can be done by testing different values and evaluating
the coherence of topics.
o Run the LDA algorithm to infer the distribution of topics for each article and the
distribution of words for each topic.
3. Evaluate the Topics:
o LDA will output a topic-term matrix where each topic is represented as a distribution
over words.
o For each article, LDA will return a topic distribution, indicating which topics the article
covers and in what proportion.
o By reviewing the top words for each topic, you can interpret and label the topics (e.g.,
“Politics,” “Technology,” “Sports”).
4. Interpretation:
o Based on the words most associated with each topic, InfoHive can identify which themes
are prevalent in the articles. For example, a topic may have words like "election," "vote,"
and "government," which would be labeled “Politics”.
********** 11 **********
--- page 12 ---
Challenges of LDA:
• Choosing the Number of Topics: The number of topics must be specified in advance, and
selecting the right number can be tricky. Too many topics may lead to overfitting, while too few
might miss important themes.
•   Interpretability: LDA generates topics as a distribution of words, which requires human
inspection to label and interpret correctly.
Use Case for InfoHive:
• Objective: If InfoHive wants to discover hidden themes in their news articles without having a
predefined set of topics, LDA is ideal.
• Example: InfoHive can apply LDA to their collection of articles and find emerging topics like

2. Non-Negative Matrix Factorization (NMF) for Topic Identification
What is NMF?
Non-Negative Matrix Factorization (NMF) is a dimensionality reduction technique, also used for topic
modelling. It factors a document-term matrix into two lower-rank non-negative matrices: one
representing topics and the other representing the relationship between documents and topics. NMF can
reveal topics based on the TF-IDF representation of documents and is generally easier to interpret than
LDA.
Steps for Applying NMF for Topic Identification:
1. Preprocessing the Data:
o As with LDA, the articles need to be cleaned (removing stopwords, punctuation, and
applying stemming/lemmatization).
o TF-IDF Vectorization: Convert the corpus into a TF-IDF matrix, which helps capture
the importance of words in each article relative to the entire corpus.
2. Apply NMF:
o Choose the number of topics (like LDA, you need to specify the number of topics in
advance).
o Apply the NMF algorithm, which decomposes the TF-IDF matrix into two non-negative
matrices: one for topics and one for documents. Each topic is represented by a set of
important terms, and each document is represented by the topics it covers.
3. Evaluate the Topics:
o NMF will output the top words for each topic, which can be interpreted to label the
topics.
o You can also examine the document-topic matrix, which will show how much each
article relates to the different topics.
4. Interpretation:
o The top words for each topic will help InfoHive interpret and label the topics. For
example, a topic that includes words like “stock,” “market,” and “economy” might be
labeled “Economy”.
Challenges of NMF:

Your task is to suggest two techniques that InfoHive can use to identify and analyze the different topics

Response to Q3:
To help InfoHive evaluate the different topics they generally address in their news articles over the past
two months, we can opt two powerful techniques for topic identification
1. Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) for Topic Identification
What is LDA?
LDA (Latent Dirichlet Allocation) is an unsupervised machine learning technique for topic modeling. It
assumes that each document (in this case, each news article) is a mixture of topics, and each topic is a
mixture of words. LDA helps in discovering the underlying thematic structure of a large collection of
text data by uncovering hidden topics.
Steps for Applying LDA for Topic Identification:
1. Preprocessing the Data:
o Text Cleaning: Remove stop words, punctuation, and perform stemming or
lemmatization.
o Vectorization: Convert the news articles into a Bag of Words (BoW) or TF-IDF matrix
(term-frequency inverse document frequency).
2. Apply LDA:
o Choose the number of topics. This can be done by testing different values and evaluating
the coherence of topics.
o Run the LDA algorithm to infer the distribution of topics for each article and the
distribution of words for each topic.
3. Evaluate the Topics:
o LDA will output a topic-term matrix where each topic is represented as a distribution
over words.
o For each article, LDA will return a topic distribution, indicating which topics the article
covers and in what proportion.
o By reviewing the top words for each topic, you can interpret and label the topics (e.g.,
“Politics,” “Technology,” “Sports”).
4. Interpretation:
o Based on the words most associated with each topic, InfoHive can identify which themes
are prevalent in the articles. For example, a topic may have words like "election," "vote,"
and "government," which would be labeled “Politics”.
********** 11 **********
--- page 12 ---
Challenges of LDA:
• Choosing the Number of Topics: The number of topics must be specified in advance, and
selecting the right number can be tricky. Too many topics may lead to overfitting, while too few
might miss important themes.
•   Interpretability: LDA generates topics as a distribution of words, which requires human
inspection to label and interpret correctly.
Use Case for InfoHive:
• Objective: If InfoHive wants to discover hidden themes in their news articles without having a
predefined set of topics, LDA is ideal.
• Example: InfoHive can apply LDA to their collection of articles and find emerging topics like
“new technology,” “pandemic updates,” or “government policies,” helping them understand what
themes they’ve been covering most in recent months.
2. Non-Negative Matrix Factorization (NMF) for Topic Identification
What is NMF?
Non-Negative Matrix Factorization (NMF) is a dimensionality reduction technique, also used for topic
modelling. It factors a document-term matrix into two lower-rank non-negative matrices: one
representing topics and the other representing the relationship between documents and topics. NMF can
reveal topics based on the TF-IDF representation of documents and is generally easier to interpret than
LDA.
Steps for Applying NMF for Topic Identification:
1. Preprocessing the Data:
o As with LDA, the articles need to be cleaned (removing stopwords, punctuation, and
applying stemming/lemmatization).
o TF-IDF Vectorization: Convert the corpus into a TF-IDF matrix, which helps capture
the importance of words in each article relative to the entire corpus.
2. Apply NMF:
o Choose the number of topics (like LDA, you need to specify the number of topics in
advance).
o Apply the NMF algorithm, which decomposes the TF-IDF matrix into two non-negative
matrices: one for topics and one for documents. Each topic is represented by a set of
important terms, and each document is represented by the topics it covers.
3. Evaluate the Topics:
o NMF will output the top words for each topic, which can be interpreted to label the
topics.
o You can also examine the document-topic matrix, which will show how much each
article relates to the different topics.

Response to Q3:
To help InfoHive evaluate the different topics they generally address in their news articles over the past
two months, we can opt two powerful techniques for topic identification
1. Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) for Topic Identification
What is LDA?
LDA (Latent Dirichlet Allocation) is an unsupervised machine learning technique for topic modeling. It
assumes that each document (in this case, each news article) is a mixture of topics, and each topic is a
mixture of words. LDA helps in discovering the underlying thematic structure of a large collection of
text data by uncovering hidden topics.
Steps for Applying LDA for Topic Identification:
1. Preprocessing the Data:
o Text Cleaning: Remove stop words, punctuation, and perform stemming or
lemmatization.
o Vectorization: Convert the news articles into a Bag of Words (BoW) or TF-IDF matrix
(term-frequency inverse document frequency).
2. Apply LDA:
o Choose the number of topics. This can be done by testing different values and evaluating
the coherence of topics.
o Run the LDA algorithm to infer the distribution of topics for each article and the
distribution of words for each topic.
3. Evaluate the Topics:
o LDA will output a topic-term matrix where each topic is represented as a distribution
over words.
o For each article, LDA will return a topic distribution, indicating which topics the article
covers and in what proportion.
o By reviewing the top words for each topic, you can interpret and label the topics (e.g.,
“Politics,” “Technology,” “Sports”).
4. Interpretation:
o Based on the words most associated with each topic, InfoHive can identify which themes
are prevalent in the articles. For example, a topic may have words like "election," "vote,"
and "government," which would be labeled “Politics”.

Challenges of LDA:
• Choosing the Number of Topics: The number of topics must be specified in advance, and
selecting the right number can be tricky. Too many topics may lead to overfitting, while too few
might miss important themes.
•   Interpretability: LDA generates topics as a distribution of words, which requires human
inspection to label and interpret correctly.
Use Case for InfoHive:
• Objective: If InfoHive wants to discover hidden themes in their news articles without having a

• Example: InfoHive can apply LDA to their collection of articles and find emerging topics like

2. Non-Negative Matrix Factorization (NMF) for Topic Identification
What is NMF?
Non-Negative Matrix Factorization (NMF) is a dimensionality reduction technique, also used for topic
modelling. It factors a document-term matrix into two lower-rank non-negative matrices: one
representing topics and the other representing the relationship between documents and topics. NMF can
reveal topics based on the TF-IDF representation of documents and is generally easier to interpret than
LDA.
Steps for Applying NMF for Topic Identification:
1. Preprocessing the Data:
o As with LDA, the articles need to be cleaned (removing stopwords, punctuation, and
applying stemming/lemmatization).
o TF-IDF Vectorization: Convert the corpus into a TF-IDF matrix, which helps capture
the importance of words in each article relative to the entire corpus.
2. Apply NMF:
o Choose the number of topics (like LDA, you need to specify the number of topics in
advance).
o Apply the NMF algorithm, which decomposes the TF-IDF matrix into two non-negative
matrices: one for topics and one for documents. Each topic is represented by a set of
important terms, and each document is represented by the topics it covers.
3. Evaluate the Topics:
o NMF will output the top words for each topic, which can be interpreted to label the
topics.
o You can also examine the document-topic matrix, which will show how much each
article relates to the different topics.
4. Interpretation:
o The top words for each topic will help InfoHive interpret and label the topics. For
example, a topic that includes words like “stock,” “market,” and “economy” might be
labeled “Economy”.
Challenges of NMF:

--- page 13 ---
• Choosing the Number of Topics: Like LDA, selecting the correct number of topics can be
challenging, especially for a large corpus.
•   Sensitivity to Initialization: NMF results can sometimes vary depending on the initialization of
the factorization, leading to less stable results.
Use Case for InfoHive:
• Objective: If InfoHive has some predefined ideas of what topics they want to track (e.g.,
Politics, Economy, Sports), or if they prefer clear, intuitive topic labels, NMF is a great
technique.
• Example: InfoHive can use NMF to categorize the past two months’ articles into clearly defined
topics like “Health,” “Politics,” or “Technology.” The results would give InfoHive clear,
interpretable topics that are easy to label.
If InfoHive is looking to discover hidden themes or emerging topics in their articles without knowing

have been prevalent in their content over the past two months.
If InfoHive has a clearer idea of the topics they want to track (for example, predefined categories such
as “Politics,” “Economy,” “Technology,” “Sports”), NMF would be more effective. NMF is easier to
interpret and will give clear topic representations that are more intuitive to label.
