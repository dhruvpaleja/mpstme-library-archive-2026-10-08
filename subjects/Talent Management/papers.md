Vertex Global Solutions, a mid-sized technology consulting firm, has been facing intense
competition in attracting skilled digital talent. Traditional recruitment methods such as job
portals and campus placements were no longer yielding high-quality candidates,
particularly for roles in AI, data analytics, and cybersecurity. Recognizing changing candidate
behavior, the HR department shifted towards social recruiting, leveraging platforms like
LinkedIn, professional online communities, and employee advocacy programs to build
employer branding and engage passive candidates. While applications increased significantly,
HR struggled to assess candidate authenticity and manage the overwhelming volume of
digital responses.
At the same time, the company realized that recruitment alone was insufficient for sustaining
long-term competitiveness. High attrition among young professionals and evolving employee
expectations highlighted the need for a forward-looking talent management strategy. The
leadership team began exploring future trends such as AI-driven talent analytics, remote and
hybrid workforce models, skills-based hiring, continuous learning ecosystems, and diversity-
focused hiring practices to ensure workforce agility and resilience.
The CEO now faces a strategic decision: how can Vertex effectively integrate social recruiting
into a broader, future-ready talent management framework? The organization must determine
how technology, data analytics, employer branding, and employee experience can collectively
strengthen talent acquisition, retention, and long-term sustainability in a rapidly changing
global labor market.
With reference to the above case, answer the following questions:
a. Enumerate how social recruiting contributes to employer branding and candidate
engagement. (5 marks)
b. Examine five future trends of talent management that Vertex Global Solutions must adopt
to ensure company development in the long run. (10 marks)
