Q5) a) - Creep, known also as cold flow, is the permanent deformation (plastic deformation) that increases
with time under constant load or stress
– Composites have better creep resistance than monoliths
– Creep in composites can occur under various circumstances:
Continuous fibers- (possible cases)-
1. The matrix creeps – fibers are elastic; stress is too low to cause damage to fibers
2. The matrix creeps – fibers are elastic; stress is high enough to cause breakdown of fibers
3. Both matrix and fibers creep; no damage to fibers due to very low stress
4. Both matrix and fibers creep; damage to fibers occurs due to high stress
Discontinuous or Short fibers (possible cases)-
1. The matrix creeps; but fibers deform elastically without creeping
2. Both matrix and fibers creep
- Assessment of creep behaviour of composites is important and prerequisite requirement in many
applications like use of Metal matrix composites at high temperatures
-Fatigue is the weakening of a material caused by cyclic loading that results in progressive, brittle and
localized structural damage
– Fatigue phenomenon has three stages; crack initiation, crack propagation and fracture
– With proper choice of matrix and reinforcement, the fatigue resistance of composites can be made better
compare to the monoliths
– Cyclic fatigue behaviour of composites is of great practical significance and is an important consideration
in design engineering
– Brief description of fatigue in MMCs or PMCs or CMCs with supporting graph
(5 Marks + 5 Marks = 10 Marks)
