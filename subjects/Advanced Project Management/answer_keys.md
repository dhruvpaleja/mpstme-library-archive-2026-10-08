BL-4   Analyze how CCPM addresses these challenges and explain the role of buffers in
overcoming them.
Solution CCPM minimizes multitasking by enforcing a single-task focus along the critical
chain, helping resources stay dedicated and reduce switching losses. It ensures timely
resource availability through resource buffers, which serve as alerts before critical
activities. CCPM also introduces project buffers to protect the final deadline and
feeding buffers to shield critical paths from feeder-path delays. Together, these
mechanisms reduce schedule slippage and improve project reliability.

Q 6 B   Analyze how stakeholder influence changes across the program lifecycle and evaluate 5
its impact on decision-making at different stages.
CO-3;
BL-3
Solution   Initiation Phase – High Influence: Stakeholders have strong input in defining goals,
scope, and success criteria.
Planning Phase – Moderate to High Influence: Stakeholders influence program
design, resource allocation, and risk planning.
Execution Phase – Moderate Influence: Influence shifts to feedback, issue resolution,
and change requests.

approve adjustments as needed.

and provide final acceptance.

Solution   Critical Success Factors (CSFs) in the project management life cycle are the essential
elements or activities that must be effectively managed to achieve project objectives
and ensure overall success.
CSFs
• Clear Communication:
✓ Effective, ongoing communication among team members, stakeholders,
and sponsors is crucial throughout all phases.
✓ It ensures everyone understands goals, roles, and changes, reducing
misunderstandings and delays. Poor communication is a common reason
for project failure.
• Stakeholder Engagement:
✓ Identifying and involving key stakeholders from initiation onward is vital.
✓ It includes managing expectations, gathering requirements, and securing
buy-in, which directly impacts project acceptance and support.
• Risk Management:
✓ Proactively identifying, assessing, and mitigating risks across the life cycle
prevents issues from escalating.
✓ It is critical in planning and execution phases but requires continuous
monitoring.
• Resource Allocation:
✓ Efficiently assigning and managing resources (human, financial, material)
ensures projects stay on track.
✓ This includes budgeting, scheduling, and optimizing team skills to avoid
bottlenecks.
• Scope Management and Goal Clarity:
✓ Defining clear, achievable objectives and controlling scope creep is
foundational.
✓ CSFs tie to business or project goals, ensuring deliverables meet
predefined success criteria.
• Leadership and Team Competence:
✓ Strong project leadership, combined with skilled teams, drives execution.
✓ It spans the life cycle, emphasizing motivation, conflict resolution, and
adaptability.

Dinesh, follows a highly participative leadership style and encourages open

BL-4   preferred structured discussions and clarity before contributing. However, Japanese

Dinesh noticed declining participation from the Japanese team and increased
frustration in the German team due to perceived lack of structure. Productivity
dropped, and the Indian team began dominating conversations. Dinesh realized
********** 8 **********
--- page 9 ---
cultural expectations were influencing how teams responded to his leadership
approach and their motivation to participate.
Based on the case, explain how culture impacts leadership and motivation.
Solution   Culture significantly shapes how individuals perceive leadership styles and what
motivates them. Applying the case:
1. Leadership Style Effectiveness Varies Across Cultures
Dinesh’s participative leadership worked well for Indian members but not for
Japanese or German teams.
Application: Japanese members preferred hierarchical leadership and avoided open
disagreement, reducing their engagement.
2. Communication Preferences Influence Motivation
German members expected structured communication and clarity to stay motivated.
Application: Lack of structure demotivated the German team, causing frustration.
3. Cultural Norms Affect Participation
Indian members were culturally comfortable expressing ideas, increasing motivation
and contribution.
Application: Their active involvement overshadowed Japanese members who valued
harmony and indirect communication.
4. Mismatch Creates Demotivation
Leadership that does not align with cultural expectations reduces participation.
Application: Japanese team felt uncomfortable voicing opinions, reducing motivation
and affecting productivity.
5. Need for Culturally Adaptive Leadership
Leaders must adjust their approach to cultural expectations (e.g., more structure for
Germans, more hierarchy for Japanese).
Application: Adapting leadership would increase motivation and team cohesion.
