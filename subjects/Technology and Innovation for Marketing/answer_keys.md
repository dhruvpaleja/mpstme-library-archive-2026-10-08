Q1d. Explain what is a Migration Path and how do you manage the Migration Path to avoid Obsolescence?
 High – tech markets are blessed with fast and significant improvements, which result in inflection points

   Basic Issue: Adopting newest generations of technology → prior investments obsolete

◦   Expectations about pace and magnitude of improvements relative to price declines play a major

anticipation of a new, better and possibly cheaper innovation coming along.
This impacts sales of current products significantly.
 High-tech firms must offer a migration path or a series of upgrades to help the customer’s transition
between generations.

Contingency model
The contingency model of high-tech marketing posits that no single, best strategy exists; rather, marketing
effectiveness depends on aligning strategies with specific environmental, technological, and
organizational factors. It focuses on navigating high uncertainty, rapid innovation, and the need for tight
R&D-marketing integration.
Key Components of the Contingency Approach
• Environmental Turbulence: High-tech markets are often characterized by fast-paced changes, requiring
firms to adopt flexible, adaptive strategies.
•   Strategic Fit: Success is driven by aligning internal capabilities (e.g., R&D, agility) with external market
conditions.
• Market Orientation: Tailoring market orientation and strategies (e.g., differentiation vs. low-cost) based
on market growth and competitive intensity.
• Innovation & Marketing Strategy: Marketing strategy innovativeness (MSI) must be matched with
external managerial relationships and market dynamism, especially in new ventures.
• Stage-Contingent Structure: As high-tech firms grow, they must transition from centralized to more
decentralized and formal decision-making structures

Market orientation is a business strategy prioritizing customer needs and desires to guide product
development, marketing, and overall company operations, aiming for long-term profitability through high
customer satisfaction. It focuses on gathering market intelligence to deliver superior value, often resulting
in increased customer loyalty and a sustainable competitive advantage.

• Customer Focus: Identifying and satisfying the needs of the target market, often through extensive
research.
• Competitor Intelligence: Understanding competitors' strengths and weaknesses to better position

.

Technology consideration process
A technology consideration process is a structured approach to identifying, evaluating, and adopting new
tools to ensure they align with organizational goals, technical requirements, and budget constraints. It
typically involves defining needs, researching options, assessing scalability, analyzing costs, and
conducting trials to ensure user-friendliness and security.
Key components of the technology consideration process include:
• Requirement Identification & Needs Assessment: Defining the specific problem, necessary features,
and user needs to ensure the technology fits organizational goals.
Technology Exploration & Scouting: Scanning the market for available and emerging solutions that meet
the defined criteria, including assessing vendor reliability.
Technical Evaluation & Feasibility: Assessing technical constraints, such as compatibility with existing
systems, integration capabilities (e.g., APIs), and security requirements.

effectiveness depends on aligning strategies with specific environmental, technological, and

R&D-marketing integration.
