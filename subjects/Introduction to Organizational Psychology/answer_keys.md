Brainstorming can overcome the pressures for conformity that dampen creativity
by encouraging any and all alternatives while withholding criticism. In a typical brainstorming
session, a half-dozen to a dozen people sit around a table. The group leader states the problem
in a clear manner so all participants understand. Members then freewheel as many alternatives
as they can in a given length of time. To encourage members to “think the unusual,” no
criticism is allowed, even of the most bizarre suggestions, and all ideas are recorded for later
discussion and analysis. Brainstorming may indeed generate ideas—but not in a very efficient
manner(5 marks)

B. BATNA (Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement) refers to the best option available to a
negotiator if no agreement is reached. It determines the minimum acceptable value for an
agreement; any offer better than one’s BATNA should be accepted. A negotiation is successful
only when an offer is more attractive than the BATNA of both parties. Understanding the other
side’s BATNA and their willingness to give up key issues strengthens negotiating power.
(3marks)A freelance designer negotiating a fee has another confirmed job paying ₹50,000 as
her BATNA. She should reject any offer below this amount and accept any offer higher than
₹50,000. If the client has no suitable alternative, the designer’s strong BATNA improves her
position in the negotiation. (2marks)
C. Power refers to a capacity that A has to influence the behavior of B so that B acts in   5
accordance with A’s wishes. Someone can have power but not use it; it is a capacity or
potential. The most important aspect of power is that it is a function of dependence: the greater
B’s dependence on A, the greater A’s power. Dependence is based on the alternatives B
perceives and the importance B places on the alternative(s) that A controls.
(3Marks) Example: If you rely on your parents for financial support during college, they hold
power over you because your dependence on their resources is high; this power decreases once
you become financially independent. Money is often a dependence factor, but it is not the only
way power is created; power varies with the level of dependence and the importance of the
resources controlled.(2 Marks)
2.   Answer the following(Attempt Any 2 Questions)   10
A. Telling (S1): In this leadership style, the leader tells people what to do and how to do   5
it. Selling (S2): This style involves more back-and-forth between leaders and followers.
Leaders "sell" their ideas and message to get group members to buy into the
process. Participating (S3): In this approach, the leader offers less direction and allows
members of the group to take a more active role in coming up with ideas and making
decisions. Delegating (S4): This style is characterized by a less involved, hands-off approach
to leadership. Group members tend to make most of the decisions and take most of the
responsibility for what happens.(1Mark per point). As follower maturity increases the
leadership style moves from telling to delegating and the leader uses lesser direction and
control. (1Mark)

Accelerated Learning: Mentees gain knowledge and skills more quickly through guidance
and practical advice from experienced mentors. Career Advancement: Mentoring can help

Opportunities: Mentors often introduce mentees to their professional networks, providing
valuable connections and opportunities.(Any 3 to be explained 1mark for each point)A
newly recruited employee is assigned a mentor in an organization. With the mentor’s guidance,
the employee learns job-related skills more quickly and understands how to apply classroom
knowledge at work. Regular feedback from the mentor builds the employee’s confidence and
improves performance. The mentor also introduces the employee to colleagues and
professional contacts, supporting career growth and future opportunities.(2 marks for
example)
C. Resistance to change can be managed using manipulation, cooptation, and coercion, but each   5
has advantages and drawbacks. Manipulation involves covert influence, such as twisting facts,
withholding information, or spreading rumors. For example, management may falsely threaten
to close a plant to force employees to accept a pay cut. It is inexpensive and can achieve quick
compliance, but can destroy trust and credibility if discovered.(2.5 Marks) Cooptation
combines manipulation and participation by giving resistance leaders key roles or seeking their
endorsement. For example, assigning team leaders to promote a new attendance system to win
their support. It secures backing cheaply, but others may feel used, and trust can be damaged.
(2.5 Marks)

A. NGT is a structured group decision-making method that limits discussion to enhance   5
independent thinking. Steps: (1) Members write ideas individually, (2) Each presents ideas
without discussion, (3) Group discusses ideas for clarity and evaluation, (4) Members rank
ideas silently; the highest-ranked idea becomes the decision. Points to be explained(5 marks)
B. Distributive bargaining operates under zero-sum conditions, where any gain made by one party   5
is at the expense of the other. Its aim is a win–lose outcome, with emphasis on positions.
Information sharing is low, and relationships are typically short term.(1.5 marks)Integrative
bargaining, on the other hand, operates under non–zero-sum conditions, where gains can be
shared by all parties. Its aim is a win–win outcome, with focus on interests. Information
sharing is high, and it helps in building long-term relationships(1.5 marks).Distributive
Bargaining: Two people haggling over the price of a used bike—whatever one gains, the other
loses.(1 mark)Integrative Bargaining: Two departments in a company negotiating resources
—by collaborating, they find a solution that benefits both (e.g., sharing staff to meet both
teams’ deadlines)(1 mark).

C. Rational persuasion is a power tactic in which a manager uses logical arguments, facts, and   5
evidence to convince employees.(1Mark)Example: A manager presents productivity data and
cost–benefit analysis to justify the introduction of a new performance appraisal
system(1Mark)Inspirational appeals involve influencing employees by appealing to emotions,
shared values, and organizational vision.(1Mark)Example: A leader motivates employees by
linking the new system to personal growth, pride in work, and the organization’s future
success.(1Mark) Impact on employee behavior: Rational persuasion leads to acceptance based
on understanding and credibility, while inspirational appeals create emotional commitment,
higher motivation, and voluntary cooperation.(1Mark)

C. Kurt Lewin’s Three-Step Model explains organizational change through unfreezing,   5
movement, and refreezing. Unfreezing prepares members for change by breaking the status
quo and communicating the need for change. Movement involves introducing new systems and
procedures and helping employees adapt to new behaviors. Refreezing stabilizes the change by
aligning rules and routines so that employees do not revert to old methods. (3 marks)For
example, when a college replaces manual administrative records with a digital system,
management highlights problems in the existing system, provides training for staff, and makes
the new system compulsory. This reduces resistance, improves efficiency, and ensures the
change is sustained. (2 marks)

2.Decision-making failures include domination by the chairperson, lack of critical evaluation,
