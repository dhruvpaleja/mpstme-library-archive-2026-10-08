(i) Newtonian Fluid [2.5 marks]
A Newtonian fluid is one that obeys Newton's law of viscosity: τ = μ (du/dy), where μ is constant at a
given temperature and pressure, independent of the rate of shear.
Examples of Newtonian fluids: Water, Air, Petrol, Glycerin (at moderate shear rates).
Examples of Non-Newtonian fluids: Blood, Toothpaste, Polymer solutions, Paint, Ketchup.

(ii) Surface Tension and Spherical Droplet [2.5 mark]
Surface tension (σ) is the tensile force per unit length acting at a free liquid surface, arising from the
net inward cohesive force on surface molecules.
A water droplet takes a spherical shape because surface tension acts to minimise the surface area. For a
given volume, a sphere has the minimum surface area. The surface tension acts as an elastic membrane
Q1   pulling the surface into the smallest possible area, which is a sphere.

Boundary Layer Separation [2 marks]
Boundary layer separation occurs when the boundary layer detaches from the solid surface. This
happens in regions of adverse pressure gradient (dp/dx > 0), i.e., where pressure increases in the flow
direction.
As the adverse pressure gradient builds up, the fluid near the wall (with low momentum) is
decelerated. At the separation point, the velocity gradient (du/dy) at the wall becomes zero; beyond
this point, reverse flow begins.

Methods to Control Separation [1 mark]
(i) Suction of boundary layer: Removing low-energy fluid through slots in the surface restores
momentum in the near-wall region.
(ii) Streamlining of the body: Giving the body a gradual tapering shape reduces the magnitude of the
adverse pressure gradient, delaying separation.

(ii) Applying the Momentum Theorem [5 marks]
Mass flux in through AB: ḟ_AB = ρ ∫₀^δ u dy [1 mark]
Mass flux out through CD: ḟ_CD = ρ ∫₀^(δ+dδ) (u + ∂u/∂x dx) dy
By continuity, mass entering through BC = ḟ_CD − ḟ_AB
Momentum flux out through CD − momentum flux in through AB = Net momentum change
Momentum out (CD): ρ ∫₀^δ u² dy + d/dx[ρ ∫₀^δ u² dy] dx
Momentum in (AB): ρ ∫₀^δ u² dy
Momentum in through BC (at free stream velocity U): U × (mass in through BC) [1 mark]
Net momentum in x-direction = − d/dx[ρ ∫₀^δ u² dy] dx + U × d/dx[ρ ∫₀^δ u dy] dx [1 mark]
The only external force in x-direction (for zero pressure gradient flat plate) is the wall shear stress:
External force = −τ₀ × dx (opposing flow)
Applying Newton's 2nd law and rearranging:
τ₀ = d/dx[ρ ∫₀^δ u(U − u) dy]
Recognising that momentum thickness θ = ∫₀^δ (u/U)(1 − u/U) dy:
ρ ∫₀^δ u(U − u) dy = ρU² θ
τ₀ = ρU² (dθ/dx) ←← Von Kármán Momentum Integral Equation [2 marks]
This equation relates the wall shear stress to the rate of growth of the momentum thickness along the
plate. It is valid for any velocity profile shape.
