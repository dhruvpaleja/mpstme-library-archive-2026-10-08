Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP) are a set of accounting rules, standards,
and procedures used to prepare, present, and report financial statements. These principles
ensure consistency, transparency, and accuracy in financial reporting. making it easier for
stakeholders (such as investors, regulators, and company management) to analyze and
compare financial data.

Here are some key components of GAAP:

1. Principle of Regularity: Accountants must follow the established rules and
regulations consistently in financial reporting.

2. Principle of Consistency: Financial statements should be prepared using the same
accounting methods from one period to the next to allow for comparison over time.

3. Principle of Sincerity: Accountants should provide an accurate and honest
representation of a company’s financial situation.

4. Principle of Permanence of Methods: The accounting methods used by the
company should be stable and consistent over time to ensure comparability.

5. Principle of Non-Compensation: Financial performance and position should be
presented without expecting compensation between revenues and expenses.

6. Principle of Prudence: Revenues and assets should not be overstated, and liabilities
and expenses should not be understated. Financial statements should be conservative
to avoid misleading the users.

7. Principle of Continuity: The assumption that the company will continue to operate
indefinitely. unless there is evidence to suggest otherwise.

8. Principle of Periodicity: Financial statements should be prepared for a specific time
period, such as a quarter or year.

9. Principle of Full Disclosure: Financial statements should include all necessary
information and notes to ensure users fully understand the company’s financial
situation.

GAAP is primarily used in the United States and is overseen by the Financial Accounting
Standards Board (FASB). It helps companies standardize their accounting practices and
fosters trust in financial statements. However, other countries may follow similar standards,
such as International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS), which is used globally.

As per paragraph 41 of Ind AS 8, errors can arise in respect of the recognition,
measurement, presentation or disclosure of elements of financial statements. Financial
statements do not comply with Ind AS if they contain either material errors or immaterial
errors made intentionally to achieve a particular presentation of an entity’s financial
position, financial performance or cash flows. Potential current period errors discovered
in that period are corrected before the financial statements are approved for issue.
However, material errors are sometimes not discovered until a subsequent period, and
these prior period errors are corrected in the comparative information presented in the
financial statements for that subsequent period.

As per paragraph 40A of Ind AS 1, an entity shall present a third balance sheet as at the
beginning of the preceding period in addition to the minimum comparative financial
statements if, inter alia, it makes a retrospective restatement of items in its financial
statements and the retrospective restatement has a material effect on the information in
the balance sheet at the beginning of the preceding period.
