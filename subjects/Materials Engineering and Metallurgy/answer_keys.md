CO2 BL–3 a   (2 Marks) Definition: Measure of a material's resistance to permanent plastic
deformation (indentation, scratching, or abrasion).
(2 Marks) Brinell Steps: Apply a known load to a hardened steel/carbide ball of
specific diameter for a set time, remove load, and measure the diameter of the
resulting indentation.
CO3 BL–4 b   (2 Marks) Phase: A homogeneous portion of a system that has uniform physical and
chemical characteristics (e.g., Liquid Fe, Solid Ferrite α).
(2 Marks) Phase Diagram: A graphical map showing the equilibrium relationships
between phases as a function of temperature, pressure, and composition (e.g., Iron-
Carbon diagram).
CO4 BL–5 c   (2 Marks) Normalizing: Heating to the Austenite range (A3/Acm) and cooling in still
air. Result is a fine, uniform grain structure and improved mechanical properties.
(2 Marks) Quenching: Rapid cooling (in water/oil/brine) from the Austenite range.
Result is very high hardness and strength due to the formation of brittle Martensite.
CO5 BL–5 d   1 Mark per type-application pair (min 4 pairs): (1) Low Carbon Steel (Structural
beams); (2) Stainless Steel (Cutlery/Chemical tanks); (3) Tool Steel (Cutting
tools/Dies); (4) High Speed Steel (Drill bits/Milling cutters).
CO6 BL–6 e   (2 Marks) Definition: Materials with at least one dimension in the range of 1 to 100
nanometers. Exhibit unique properties due to quantum effects and high surface area.
(2 Marks) Applications (1 Mark each): (1) High-strength composites (e.g., carbon
nanotubes for lightweight structures); (2) Catalysis (high surface area for faster
reactions); (3) Coatings (e.g., scratch-resistant/self-cleaning).

CO2 BL–3 b   (5 Marks) Method 1 (e.g., Ultrasonic Testing - UT): Principle: Uses high-frequency sound   10
waves to propagate through the material. A discontinuity (flaw) reflects the waves back.
Working: Measures the time taken for the reflection to locate and size internal flaws.
(5 Marks) Method 2 (e.g., Magnetic Particle Inspection - MPI): Principle: Magnetizing a
ferromagnetic material. Flaws (surface/near-surface) cause leakage of magnetic flux.
Working: Fine magnetic particles are applied and accumulate at the flux leakage points,
indicating the flaw.
