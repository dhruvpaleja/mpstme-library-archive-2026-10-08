b.   1. Static Determinacy   [5]
A structure is said to be statically determinate when all the unknown reactions
and internal forces can be found only using the equations of equilibrium.
Equilibrium Equations (for 2D structures):
• ∑Fx = 0
• ∑Fy = 0
• ∑M = 0
CO-1   Total available equations = 3
2. Static Indeterminacy
A structure is said to be statically indeterminate when the number of unknowns
exceeds the available equilibrium equations.
In such cases, equilibrium equations are not sufficient.
Condition:
If
Number   of   unknowns   >   Number   of   equilibrium   equations,
then the structure is statically indeterminate.

Q1   Answer briefly:   [20]
a.   A structure is kinematically determinate when its configuration is such that:
•   It has just enough constraints to prevent any independent movement
•   All displacements can be determined uniquely
CO-1   •   It is stable and does not undergo mechanism (collapse due to movement) [5]
A structure is kinematically indeterminate when:
•   It has multiple possible independent displacements (degrees of freedom)
•   Additional compatibility equations are needed to find displacements
b.   Based on Structural Form:   [5]

c.   Stable Structure:   [5]
• A structure that maintains its shape and position under applied loads.
• Has sufficient supports and restraints to prevent movement.
• Does not undergo displacement or collapse.
•   All reactions and internal forces can develop properly.
CO-1
Unstable Structure:
• A structure that cannot maintain its shape under load.
• Has insufficient or improper supports/restraints.
• Undergoes large displacement or collapses (mechanism formation).
• Cannot resist applied loads effectively.
d.   Strain energy is the energy stored in a body due to deformation when it is [5]
subjected to external loads within the elastic limit.
It is recoverable energy → released when the load is removed.
Occurs due to stress and strain in the material.
Depends on:
CO-1   • Load applied
•   Material properties (E)
• Geometry (length, area, moment of inertia)
Used in:
•   Deflection calculations
• Energy methods (e.g., Castigliano’s theorem)
