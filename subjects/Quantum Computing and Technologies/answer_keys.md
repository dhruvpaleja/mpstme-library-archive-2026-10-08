c.   Explain the principle of the Linear Paul Trap with appropriate diagram.
Solution:
(a) The linear Paul trap consists of four conducting rods. Two opposing rods are connected
to one pole of a radiofrequency (rf) voltage source, whereas the remaining two are connected
to the other pole. The axis of symmetry between the rods is the trap axis. (b) With the rods
charged as shown in the figure, the resulting electric force pushes a positive ion to the
negative rods and repels it from the positive ones. (c) Half an rf period later, the polarity of
all rods is reversed, and the direction of the force also reverses. (d) If the polarity changes
fast enough, a heavy ion becomes stuck in a rapid back-and-forth motion. Because the electric
fields are at a minimum at the trap axis, an effective force pushes the ion toward the center,
where it becomes trapped (although it is still free to move along the axis). The string of
radially trapped ions can be confined axially when a positively charged electrode (end cap)
is placed at each end of the rods.
Explanation – 3 marks, Figure – 1 mark.

Classical logic gates and quantum logic gates are fundamental components of their
respective computing systems, but they operate based on different principles. Here
are the key differences:
1. Basic Units of Information
•   Classical Logic Gates: Use bits as the basic unit of information, which can
be either 0 or 1.
• Quantum Logic Gates: Use quantum bits (qubits), which can exist in a
superposition of states. A qubit can be 0, 1, or both simultaneously, thanks
to quantum mechanics.
