Database Management System (DBMS) and Three Levels of Data Abstraction
A Database Management System (DBMS) is a software system designed to store, manage,
retrieve, and organize data in a structured manner. It acts as an interface between users and the
database, ensuring data security, integrity, consistency, and efficient access. A DBMS reduces
data redundancy and allows multiple users to access data simultaneously.
Three Levels of Data Abstraction in DBMS
Data abstraction in DBMS helps hide the complexity of data storage and presents only the
required information to users. The three levels are:
Page 2 of 8
--- page 3 ---
1. Physical Level (Internal Level)
This is the lowest level of abstraction. It describes how data is physically stored in the
system. It includes details such as storage structures, indexing, file organization, and
memory allocation.
It focuses on physically recorded data.
2. Logical Level (Conceptual Level)
This level describes what data is stored in the database and the relationships among the data.
It defines tables, attributes, data types, and constraints.
It represents the database with relational information.
3. View Level (External Level)
This is the highest level of abstraction. It shows only a part of the entire database to
different users according to their needs. It enhances security by restricting access to specific
data.
It presents a part of the full data as a view.

b. Database Management System (DBMS) and its Basic Components   8
A Database Management System (DBMS) is a software system designed to manage, store,
retrieve, and organize data in a structured manner. It acts as an interface between users and the
database, ensuring that data is easily accessible, secure, and efficiently managed. A DBMS
helps in maintaining data integrity, reducing redundancy, and supporting multiple users
simultaneously.
Basic Components of DBMS
Page 2 of 5
--- page 3 ---
1. Data Mining
Data mining refers to the process of extracting useful patterns and meaningful information
from large volumes of data. It helps organizations in decision-making by identifying trends,
relationships, and insights hidden within the database.
2. Data Warehousing
Data warehousing involves collecting and storing large amounts of data from multiple
sources in a centralized repository. It supports reporting, analysis, and strategic planning by
providing consolidated and historical data.
3. Data Dissemination
Data dissemination is the process of distributing or sharing data with users or systems as
required. A DBMS ensures that the right information is delivered to authorized users in an
accurate and timely manner.
4. Backup and Recovery
Backup and recovery mechanisms protect data from loss due to system failure, hardware
issues, or cyber threats. Backup creates copies of data, while recovery restores the database
to its previous consistent state in case of failure.

b. A growing company experiencing declining customer retention despite high sales indicates a   8
gap in relationship management. While new customers are being acquired, existing customers
are not being retained. Implementing Customer Relationship Management (CRM) can
address this issue in the following ways:
1. Adapting to Changing Markets and Consumer Demands
Markets and customer preferences change rapidly. CRM helps track customer behavior,
feedback, and buying patterns. This enables the company to modify products and services
according to evolving expectations.
2. Better Business Processes and Information Systems
CRM integrates sales, marketing, and service departments into one system. This ensures
smooth communication and consistent service, reducing customer dissatisfaction.
3. Need for Data Analytics
CRM systems use data analytics to analyze purchase history, complaints, and preferences. This
helps identify reasons for customer churn and develop targeted retention strategies.
4. Proactive Approach to Meeting Consumer Demands
Instead of reacting after losing customers, CRM enables proactive engagement. Example:
Sending renewal reminders, personalized offers, or follow-up calls before customers switch to
competitors.

CRM helps segment customers based on value, purchase frequency, and preferences. High-
value customers can receive special attention and customized benefits.
