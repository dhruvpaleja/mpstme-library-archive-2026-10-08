Working principle (4 marks)
• A photoresist is a light-sensitive polymer film spun onto a substrate;
patterned illumination through a mask alters its solubility in a developer,
transferring the mask pattern into the resist layer.
•   After exposure, a post-exposure bake can stabilize chemistry, and
development selectively removes the more-soluble tone, leaving a
patterned resist that then guides etching, implantation, or deposition steps.
Positive vs Negative photoresists (5 marks)
•   Positive resists: Exposure causes chain scission or deprotection (often via
photoacid in chemically amplified resists), making exposed regions more

typically enabling thicker films and robust, high-aspect features.

maintain resolution.

microfluidics molds or high-aspect structures).

Principle of Electrostatic Sensors and Actuators 4 marks
Electrostatic devices operate on Coulomb forces between charged conductors.
1. Electrostatic Sensors (Capacitive Sensing)
•   Principle: They function as variable capacitors. The capacitance (C)
between two conductive plates is C=εA/d, where A is the overlap area
and d is the gap. (1 Mark)
• Mechanism: An input stimulus (e.g., acceleration or pressure) causes a
physical displacement (Δd or ΔA), resulting in a measurable change in
capacitance (ΔC). (1 Mark)
2. Electrostatic Actuators
•   Principle: When a voltage (V) is applied between two electrodes, an
attractive electrostatic force (F∝V2/d2) is generated, pulling the movable
structure towards the fixed one. (1 Mark)
•   Limitation: These actuators suffer from pull-in instability, limiting the
stable travel range to about one-third of the initial gap distance. (1 Mark)

The four piezoresistors are connected in a full Wheatstone bridge configuration.
Bridge Circuit Diagram
•   Excitation Voltage (Vin): A stable DC voltage is applied across the bridge
(nodes A and B).
•   Balanced Bridge (Zero Pressure): When no pressure is applied, all four
resistors are ideally equal (R1=R2=R3=R4=R). The bridge is balanced,
and the output voltage Vout (measured between nodes C and D) is ≈0V.
•   Unbalanced Bridge (Applied Pressure): When pressure (P) is applied:
o R1 and R4 might increase to R+ΔR.
o R2 and R3 might decrease to R−ΔR.
o This causes the voltage at node C to differ from the voltage at node
D, making the bridge unbalanced.
•   Output Voltage (Vout): The output voltage is the difference between the
node voltages VC and VD:
Vout=VC−VD=Vin(R3+R4R4−R1+R3R3)
For a full active bridge where R1=R4=R+ΔR and R2=R3=R−ΔR, this simplifies
to: Vout≈VinRΔR
This voltage is directly proportional to the applied pressure.
