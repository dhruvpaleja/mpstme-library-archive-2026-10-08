1. Credit Risk: Possibility that the issuer may default on interest or principal payments.
2. Interest Rate Risk: Rising interest rates can reduce the market value of fixed-rate debentures.
3. Liquidity Risk: Some debentures may be difficult to sell quickly without a price concession.
4. Inflation Risk: Fixed returns may lose purchasing power if inflation rises significantly.
5. Call Risk: Issuer may redeem the debenture before maturity, affecting expected returns.
6. Market Risk: Price fluctuations due to changes in market sentiment or economic conditions.
2. Functions of Commercial Banks in India:

savings, current, and fixed deposits.
2. Providing Loans and Advances: Offer credit facilities such as personal loans, business loans,
overdrafts, and cash credit to individuals and businesses.
3. Credit Creation: Create credit by lending more than the actual cash reserves, thereby supporting
economic activity.
4. Payment and Settlement Services: Facilitate transactions through cheques, demand drafts, NEFT,
RTGS, IMPS, and digital banking.

Structure of Financial Markets:
Financial markets are broadly divided into two segments:
1. Money Market – Deals with short-term funds (less than one year).
o Instruments: Treasury bills, commercial papers, certificates of deposit.
o Participants: Banks, RBI, financial institutions.
2. Capital Market – Deals with long-term funds.
o Subdivided into:
▪ Primary Market: Where new securities are issued.
▪ Secondary Market: Where existing securities are traded.
o Instruments: Shares, debentures, bonds.
o Participants: Investors, companies, stock exchanges.

a) Mobilization of Savings: Channelizes savings from households and institutions into productive
investments.
b) Price Discovery: Helps determine the fair value of financial instruments through demand-supply
dynamics.
c) Liquidity Provision: Enables investors to buy and sell securities easily.
d) Risk Management: Offers instruments like derivatives to hedge against financial risks.
e) Efficient Allocation of Resources: Directs funds to sectors and enterprises with the highest
potential for returns.
f) Facilitates Economic Growth: Supports capital formation and industrial development.

insurance, investment, and asset management. These services facilitate the flow of funds and financial
instruments between individuals, businesses, and governments.

• Customer-Oriented: Tailored to meet the specific financial needs of clients.
• Dynamic: Continuously evolving with technological advancements and regulatory changes.
• Intermediary-Based: Often involve intermediaries like banks, brokers, and financial advisors.
Significance of Financial Services:

b) Capital Formation: Encourage investment in infrastructure, industry, and innovation.
c) Risk Management: Help individuals and businesses manage financial risks through insurance and
hedging instruments.
d) Liquidity Provision: Ensure availability of funds for short-term and long-term needs.
e) Financial Inclusion: Extend services to underserved populations, promoting inclusive development.
f) Efficient Resource Allocation: Direct funds to sectors with high growth potential.

1 a) Under a factoring agreement, the factor assumes the responsibility of collecting payments from
customers, thereby reducing the administrative burden and credit risk of the selling firm. Factoring can
be with or without recourse—meaning, in the former, the firm bears the risk of non-payment, while in the
latter, the factor assumes that risk.
1 b) The role of consumer finance in promoting personal consumption and economic growth is significant.
By providing easy access to credit, consumer finance allows individuals to meet their needs and desires
even without immediate funds. This increases the demand for goods and services, which in turn stimulates
production, employment, and income generation across the economy.
1c ) Operating Lease
• Short-term lease agreement.
• Ownership risks and rewards remain with the lessor.
• Asset is not recorded on the lessee’s balance sheet.
• Lease payments are treated as operating expenses.
• Commonly used for assets with short useful life or frequent upgrades.
Finance Lease (Capital Lease)
• Long-term lease agreement.
• Ownership risks and rewards are transferred to the lessee.
• Asset is recorded on the lessee’s balance sheet.
• Lease payments include interest and principal components.
• Often used when the lessee intends to use the asset for most of its useful life.
2. Futures Contracts: Investors can lock in future gold prices through standardized exchange-traded
contracts, reducing uncertainty in procurement or investment costs.
• Options Contracts: By purchasing call or put options, firms gain the right (but not the obligation)
to buy or sell gold at a predetermined price, offering flexibility and downside protection.
• Forward Contracts: Customized agreements between two parties to buy or sell gold at a future date
for a fixed price, useful for hedging specific exposures.

Q1. Answer Briefly (10 Marks – 2.5 Marks Each)
a. Capital Market
The capital market is a financial market where long-term debt and equity-backed securities are traded. It
consists of the primary market (where securities are issued) and the secondary market (where existing
securities are traded). It helps in capital formation and economic growth.
b. Secondary Market
The secondary market is where already issued securities are bought and sold among investors. It includes stock
exchanges like NSE & BSE and ensures liquidity, price discovery, and market efficiency.
c. Government Securities Market
This market deals with bonds, treasury bills (T-Bills), and dated securities issued by the government to raise
funds. It ensures safe investments with fixed returns and is regulated by RBI.
d. Instruments of the Money Market
The money market includes short-term financial instruments such as:
•   Treasury Bills (T-Bills) – Issued by the government.
• Commercial Paper (CP) – Short-term unsecured corporate debt.
•   Certificates of Deposit (CDs) – Time deposits issued by banks.
•   Call/Notice Money – Short-term interbank loans.

Concept of Mutual Funds
A mutual fund is a professionally managed investment scheme that pools money from multiple investors to
invest in diversified asset classes such as equities, bonds, and money market instruments. It offers
diversification, liquidity, and risk management.
Role of AMFI (Association of Mutual Funds in India)
1. Regulation & Compliance – Ensures mutual funds follow SEBI guidelines.
2. Investor Awareness – Conducts campaigns like "Mutual Funds Sahi Hai" to educate investors.
3. Standardization – Maintains uniform rules on expense ratios, fund disclosures, and risk classification.
4. Dispute Resolution – Addresses investor grievances related to fund management.
AMFI ensures transparency and integrity in the mutual fund industry, promoting investor confidence.
