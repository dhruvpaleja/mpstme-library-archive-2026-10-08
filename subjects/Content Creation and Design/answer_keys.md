Content creation helps a brand become more visible by sharing useful and consistent

strengthens loyalty and encourages long‑term relationships.

Explanation of good UI elements – 2 marks
Comparison and impact of both – 1 mark
Synoptic Answer
Poor UI design usually includes cluttered screens, inconsistent fonts, unclear buttons, and
confusing layouts that make navigation difficult. Colours may be poorly balanced, and
elements might feel overwhelming or misplaced. Good UI design, on the other hand, uses clean
layouts, readable typography, and well‑organised content that guides the user naturally. Buttons
and icons are clearly visible, and spacing helps reduce visual strain. The key contrast lies in
how users experience clarity versus confusion. While poor UI creates frustration, good UI
encourages smooth interaction and stronger engagement.

Synoptic Answer
The 20‑field registration form creates a major UX barrier because it demands excessive effort
before the user receives any value. Asking for unnecessary details like birthdate and landline
adds friction and increases abandonment rates. Customers may feel pressured and lose trust
when forced into long data‑collection steps. A user‑centric alternative is allowing “guest
checkout” or showing shipping costs upfront with only minimal fields required. This reduces
cognitive load and improves conversion by respecting the user’s time.

Explanation of importance – 2 marks

Synoptic Answer
Content distribution is the process of sharing created content across different platforms such as
websites, social media, emails, or apps. It ensures that the right people see the content at the
right time. Effective distribution increases visibility, engagement, and the overall reach of the
message. If content is created but not shared properly, it fails to meet its goals. For example, a
brand launching a new snack may publish a blog on its website but also promote it through
Instagram reels and YouTube shorts to reach younger audiences. This multi‑platform approach
ensures higher awareness and interaction.

Explanation of focus groups – 2 marks
Explanation of competitor analysis – 2 marks
Comparative connection – 1 mark
Synoptic Answer
Focus groups involve bringing together a small group of people to discuss reactions,
preferences, and suggestions about content ideas. This method gives direct, qualitative insights
into audience behaviour and emotional responses. Competitor analysis, on the other hand,
studies what other content creators or brands are producing, how their audience responds, and
which strategies work. It helps creators learn market expectations and identify gaps in content.
While focus groups provide personal feedback, competitor analysis provides strategic
understanding. Using both helps creators build content that is relevant and competitive.

Definition of proximity – 1 mark
Definition of alignment – 1 mark
Explanation of relationship – 2 marks
Relevant example – 1 mark
Synoptic Answer
Proximity refers to placing related elements close together to show their connection, while
alignment ensures that text and graphics line up neatly. Both principles work together to
organise information logically and make layouts easy to read. When items are grouped through
proximity and arranged in straight lines through alignment, the design appears cleaner and
more professional. For example, a business card groups the name, job title, and contact details
together (proximity) and aligns them to the left or centre for neatness (alignment). This
combination helps viewers understand information quickly. Together, proximity and alignment
create visual order and improve readability.

Synoptic Answer-
Canva is suitable for creating a wide variety of designs such as posters, Instagram posts,
presentations, and resumes with its large template library. It is easy to use and better for
general-purpose graphic design. Piktochart, on the other hand, is more specialised for
data‑driven visuals like infographics, charts, and reports. It provides strong tools for converting
complex information into simple visuals. While Canva suits everyday content creators,
Piktochart suits users who need clear data visualisation. Both tools are user‑friendly but serve
different design needs.
