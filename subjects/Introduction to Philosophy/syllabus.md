Course Objective
The course will acquaint students with important conceptual tools in Indian and Western philosophy.
They will be guided to use these philosophical concepts to conduct their own enquiry. It will train
students to think carefully and will inculcate in them the rigour of examining one’s beliefs and actions.
Course Outcomes
After the successful completion of this course, the student will be able to -
1. State and recall philosophical concepts covered in the course
2. Apply the concepts learned, to hypothetical and real situations
3. Formulate an informed response/judgement to a given problem/situation, based on a topic
covered in the course
Detailed Syllabus
Unit   Description   Duration (hrs)
1.   Introduction to Philosophy   05
The nature of philosophical enquiry, branches of Indian and Western
Philosophy, kinds of questions asked by philosophers. Relation of philosophy
with everyday life and making philosophical enquiries into areas such as the
human condition, religion, technology, and   justice. Understanding
philosophy as a transformative critical activity.
2.   Introduction to Epistemology (Indian and Western)   06
Knowledge, justification, sources of knowledge, skepticism
3.   Introduction to Metaphysics (Indian and Western)   06
Being/becoming, physical world, human mind, causation, appearance,
reality
4.   Introduction to Logic (Indian and Western)   06
Anumana; Types of arguments, inference, deductive, inductive and abductive
reasoning, validity, soundness
5.   Philosophy of Language (Indian and Western)   06
The problem of meaning, sphota, meaning, reference and use, meaning and
translation

6.   Ethics and Morality   06
Notions of good, right, duty, obligation, moral dilemmas, justice, human
rights
7.   Contemporary philosophical themes   10
Nationalism,   Multi-culturalism,   Swaraj,   Feminism,   Existentialism,
Modernism and Post-modernism, exclusion and inclusion
Total   45
