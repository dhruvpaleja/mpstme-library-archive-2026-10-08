Good Corporate Governance refers to the system of rules, practices and processes by which a
company is directed and controlled in an ethical and transparent manner. It ensures proper
management, accountability and protection of stakeholder interests. Strong corporate
governance improves company performance, builds investor confidence and prevents corporate
failures.
The elements of Corporate Governance are as follows:
1. Transparency
Transparency means open, accurate and timely disclosure of financial and business information
of the company. This includes financial statements, ownership structure, management decisions
and future plans. Transparency helps shareholders and stakeholders understand the true
position of the company and reduces chances of fraud and misrepresentation.
2. Accountability
Page 4 of 11
--- page 5 ---
Accountability ensures that the board of directors and management are responsible for their
decisions and performance. They must explain how company resources are used and justify
business results. Proper audits, reporting systems and shareholder meetings strengthen
accountability and prevent misuse of authority.
3. Fairness and Equality
Fairness ensures equal treatment of all shareholders, including minority shareholders. All
investors should receive fair returns, equal voting rights and access to information. This
element builds trust among investors and encourages wider participation in capital markets.
4. Independent and Effective Board
A good governance system includes an independent board of directors with non-executive and
independent members. These directors provide objective judgement, monitor management
activities and protect shareholder interests. An effective board improves decision-making and
corporate control.
5. Ethical Behaviour and Integrity
Ethical conduct involves honesty, fairness and moral values in business operations. Companies
should follow codes of conduct, avoid corruption and practice truthful financial reporting.
Ethical behaviour builds corporate reputation and long-term stakeholder trust.
6. Strong Internal Control and Audit System
Good corporate governance requires effective internal controls and audit mechanisms. These
systems safeguard company assets, prevent fraud and ensure accuracy of financial records.
Regular internal and external audits improve financial discipline and transparency.
7. Risk Management
Risk management involves identifying, evaluating and controlling business risks such as
financial losses, legal problems and market changes. Proper risk policies help companies
handle uncertainty and protect long-term stability.
8. Stakeholder Protection
Good governance ensures fair treatment of all stakeholders including employees, customers,
creditors, suppliers and government. It promotes fair wages, quality products, timely payments
and legal compliance, leading to balanced business growth
b. 4 marks for functions and 4 marks for importance   8
The Audit Committee is one of the most important committees of the Board of Directors
formed to ensure proper financial management, transparency, and accountability in a company.
It acts as a link between the Board, management, and auditors. Under the Companies Act,
2013, the Audit Committee is mandatory for listed companies and certain public companies. It
plays a major role in strengthening corporate governance.
Functions of the Audit Committee
Page 5 of 11
--- page 6 ---
1. Oversight of Financial Reporting
The Audit Committee reviews the company’s financial statements before they are presented to
the Board. It ensures that the accounts are accurate, complete, and prepared according to
accounting standards. By carefully examining financial reports, the committee ensures
transparency and protects shareholders from misleading information.
2. Monitoring Internal Controls
The committee evaluates the internal control systems of the company to ensure they are
effective. It identifies weaknesses and suggests improvements to prevent fraud,
mismanagement, or operational inefficiency. Strong internal controls safeguard the company’s
assets and maintain confidence among investors and stakeholders.
3. Supervision of Statutory Auditors
The Audit Committee recommends the appointment, remuneration, and removal of statutory
auditors. It monitors their independence and performance to ensure audits are thorough and
objective. The committee also reviews audit reports and ensures that management addresses
any concerns raised by auditors.
4. Reviewing Internal Audit Reports
The committee examines reports submitted by the internal auditors and ensures that
management takes necessary corrective actions. It coordinates the work of internal and
statutory auditors to avoid duplication and strengthen financial scrutiny. This ensures that the
company’s internal auditing process is effective and reliable.
5. Compliance with Laws and Regulations
The Audit Committee ensures that the company complies with all legal, regulatory, and
statutory requirements, including the Companies Act and SEBI guidelines. It monitors
corporate governance practices and advises the Board about any violations or lapses. This
helps the company maintain ethical and legal operations.
6. Risk Management and Assessment
The committee assesses financial, operational, and strategic risks faced by the company. It
ensures proper mechanisms are in place to manage these risks. By monitoring risk management
frameworks, the committee protects the company from potential losses and enhances long-term
sustainability.
Importance of the Audit Committee
1. Enhances Transparency
The Audit Committee ensures that all financial information and company operations are
transparent. Accurate reporting and clear disclosures increase investor confidence and protect
shareholders from fraud or misleading statements.
2. Strengthens Corporate Governance
By supervising audits, internal controls, and compliance, the committee promotes ethical and
responsible management. It ensures that the Board and management act in the best interests of
shareholders and stakeholders.

3. Improves Accountability
The committee holds management accountable for financial and operational decisions. It
ensures that management follows proper procedures and addresses any lapses, reducing the
chances of errors or misconduct.
4. Protects Shareholders’ Interests
By monitoring financial reporting, audits, and compliance, the Audit Committee safeguards
both majority and minority shareholders. It prevents misuse of funds and ensures that the

5. Assists in Decision-Making

E-Governance refers to the use of information and communication technology (ICT) by
companies and government bodies to improve governance processes, decision-making,
reporting and communication. In the corporate context, E-Governance involves using digital
systems for financial reporting, compliance filing, shareholder communication and internal
controls. It plays a vital role in increasing corporate transparency, efficiency and accountability
in modern business organisations.
1. Digital Disclosure of Financial Information
Page 8 of 11
--- page 9 ---
E-Governance enables companies to publish financial statements, annual reports and regulatory
filings on company websites and government portals. This allows investors, regulators and the
public to easily access accurate and timely information. Easy availability of data reduces
secrecy and promotes openness in corporate operations
2. Online Regulatory Compliance
Through e-platforms such as MCA portal and SEBI online filing systems, companies submit
statutory returns, tax filings and governance reports electronically. This reduces manipulation,
delays and human errors. Digital records also create audit trails, making compliance more
transparent and traceable.
3. Improved Shareholder Communication
E-Governance allows companies to communicate with shareholders through emails, online
voting systems, webinars and digital annual general meetings. Shareholders can easily receive
notices, vote on resolutions and access company information, thereby improving participation
and trust.
4. Reduction in Corruption and Fraud
Digital systems minimize human intervention in reporting and approvals. Automated processes
reduce opportunities for bribery, data manipulation and financial fraud. Every transaction is
recorded electronically, increasing accountability and making it easier to detect irregularities.
5. Faster Decision-Making and Reporting
E-Governance enables real-time data access and faster reporting systems. Management and
board members can review financial performance, compliance status and risk indicators
instantly. This improves transparency in decision-making and allows timely corrective actions.
6. Strengthening Internal Controls
Digital governance systems include audit software, risk management tools and compliance
monitoring platforms. These systems ensure that company policies are followed properly and
deviations are reported immediately. Strong internal controls enhance transparency and prevent
misuse of corporate resources.
7. Public Access to Corporate Information
Many corporate details such as director information, financial filings and company status are
available on government portals. This allows investors, creditors and researchers to verify
company data easily, increasing openness and trust in corporate governance.
8. Enhanced Corporate Accountability
Since actions are digitally recorded and easily traceable, managers and directors become more
responsible for their decisions. E-Governance creates a culture of accountability where
wrongdoing can be quickly identified and corrected.
9. Cost Efficiency and Accuracy
Electronic systems reduce paperwork, storage costs and administrative delays. Automated
calculations and digital documentation reduce errors, improving accuracy and reliability of
corporate information.

b. 4 marks for recommendation and 4 marks for impact   8
The Kumar Mangalam Birla Committee was constituted by the Securities and Exchange Board
of India (SEBI) in 1999 to improve corporate governance standards in listed Indian companies.
The committee was formed in response to growing investor concerns about transparency,
accountability, and protection of shareholders’ rights in Indian corporates. The committee
submitted its report in 2000, which led to the introduction of Clause 49 of the Listing
Agreement, making corporate governance norms mandatory for listed companies. This report
marked a major turning point in the development of corporate governance in India.
Key Recommendations of the Kumar Mangalam Birla Committee
1. Board Composition and Independence
The committee recommended that boards should have an appropriate balance of executive and
non-executive directors, with a significant number of independent directors. If the chairman
was an executive director, at least 50% of the board should consist of independent directors.
This was to ensure objective supervision and reduce concentration of power.
2. Formation of Audit Committee
The report made it mandatory for listed companies to establish an Audit Committee with a
minimum of three directors, mostly independent. The committee was given responsibilities
such as reviewing financial statements, overseeing audits, and ensuring internal control
effectiveness. This strengthened financial transparency and accountability.
3. Disclosure and Transparency Requirements
Companies were required to disclose important information such as Financial performance,
Related party transactions, Risk management policies, Corporate governance reports. These
disclosures improved openness and allowed investors to make informed decisions.
4. Code of Conduct for Directors and Management
The committee recommended that companies adopt a formal code of conduct for board
members and senior executives. This promoted ethical behavior, honesty, and responsible
decision-making.
5. CEO/CFO Certification of Financial Statements
It required the CEO and CFO to certify financial statements, confirming their accuracy and
compliance with accounting standards. This increased responsibility of top management and
reduced financial manipulation.
6. Shareholder Grievance Redressal Mechanism
The committee emphasized the need for a proper system to handle investor complaints and
grievances efficiently. This improved shareholder protection and corporate accountability.
Impact on Corporate Governance Reforms in India
1. Introduction of Clause 49

--- page 11 ---
The Birla Committee Report directly led to the implementation of Clause 49, which made
corporate governance practices mandatory for listed companies. This was India’s first formal
governance framework.
2. Strengthening of Board Independence
The requirement of independent directors improved board oversight and reduced promoter
dominance in Indian companies.
3. Improved Financial Transparency
Audit Committees and disclosure norms significantly improved financial reporting quality and
reduced corporate fraud.
4. Enhanced Investor Confidence
Better governance practices increased trust among domestic and foreign investors, boosting
capital market growth.
5. Basis for Modern Governance Laws
Many provisions of the Companies Act, 2013 and SEBI LODR Regulations, 2015 are based on
Birla Committee recommendations.
The Kumar Mangalam Birla Committee Report was a landmark in Indian corporate
governance history. Its recommendations introduced board independence, audit committees,
transparency, ethical conduct, and financial accountability into Indian corporate practices. By
leading to Clause 49 and later governance reforms, the report significantly strengthened
corporate governance in India, protected shareholders’ interests, and aligned Indian companies
with global standards.
