Q1a. Evolution and Importance of HRM (Module 1) – 5 Marks
Model Answer:
Human Resource Management (HRM) evolved from personnel management, which was administrative
in nature, into a strategic function driving business success.
Earlier, HR focused on payroll and compliance; today, it emphasizes strategic workforce planning,
learning, engagement, and analytics.
The HR department is vital for line managers (guidance on hiring, training, and performance) and staff
managers (policy formulation, conflict resolution).
By integrating business strategy and human capital, HRM ensures that the right people are in the right
jobs at the right time.
Example: Infosys HR’s competency mapping and continuous learning culture make HR astrategic
partner.
Marks: Concept (2) + Application (2) + Example (1)
Q1b. Hard vs Soft HRM – 5 Marks
Model Answer:
• Hard HRM: Focuses on quantitative control, cost minimization, and treating employees as
resources.
• Soft HRM: Focuses on motivation, participation, and development of employees as valuable
assets.
Example: Amazon’s logistics unit uses hard HRM (targets, efficiency metrics), while Google applies
soft HRM (autonomy, innovation culture).
Balanced application of both ensures efficiency and engagement.
Marks: Definitions (2) + Comparison (2) + Example (1)
Q2a. Steps in HR Planning – 5 Marks
Model Answer:
• Forecasting Demand – Identify the number and skills required.
• Assessing Supply – Evaluate existing talent internally and externally.
• Gap Analysis – Identify shortages or surpluses.

• Action Plans – Recruitment, training, redeployment.
• Monitoring – Periodic review and updating.
Example: TCS forecasts manpower annually for digital projects based on client pipeline.

Q2b. Quantitative Techniques in HR Forecasting – 5 Marks
Model Answer:
• Ratio Analysis: Compares number of employees to output (e.g., 1 HR for 80 staff).
• Regression Analysis: Uses historical data to project future needs based on growth or sales
trends.
These help in data-driven manpower decisions.
Example: Infosys uses regression models linking project size to headcount.
Marks: Concepts (2) + Application (2) + Example (1)
Q3a. Recruitment Methods – 5 Marks
Model Answer:
• Internal: Promotions, transfers (builds loyalty, saves cost).
• External: Job portals, campus placements (fresh talent, new ideas).
Balanced recruitment ensures stability and innovation.
Example: Wipro blends internal promotions with Naukri and LinkedIn hiring.
Marks: Explanation (2) + Comparison (2) + Example (1)
Q3b. Selection Process – 5 Marks
Model Answer:
• Screening
• Tests (aptitude, technical)
• Structured Interviews
• Reference Check
• Final Offer
Objectivity is achieved through Behavioral Interviewing, Rating Scales, and ATS tools.
Example: Infosys uses technical panels + HR panels for unbiased evaluation.
Marks: Steps (3) + Tools/Justification (2)
Q4a. Performance Appraisal Challenges – 5 Marks
Model Answer:
Common problems:
• Halo effect: one good trait overshadows others
• Leniency bias: rating everyone high
• Recency bias: judging based on recent events
Solutions include training appraisers, using 360° feedback, and standardized rating scales.
Example: IBM implemented AI-assisted feedback to reduce bias.
Marks: Challenges (2) + Solutions (2) + Example (1)
Q4b. Training Need Analysis (TNA) – 5 Marks
Model Answer:
TNA identifies gaps between current and required performance:
Organizational Analysis: Strategic goals.
Task Analysis: Job skills required.
Person Analysis: Individual performance gaps.
Example: ICICI uses TNA to plan leadership programs. Marks: Steps (3) + Explanation (1) + Example
(1)

5a. Factors Affecting Compensation – 5 Marks
Model Answer:
• Internal: Skill level, seniority, performance.
• External: Market rates, inflation, legal norms.
• Job evaluation ensures fairness.
Example: Infosys uses global pay benchmarking to ensure competitiveness.
Marks: Factors (3) + Explanation (2)
Q5b. Broad Banding – 5 Marks
Model Answer:
Rewards satisfy intrinsic & extrinsic needs.
Recognition, incentives, and promotion increase motivation per Maslow & Herzberg.
Example: Google’s peer-to-peer recognition promotes belonging.
Marks: Concept (2) + Application (2) + Example (1)
Q6a. HR Analytics – 5 Marks
Model Answer:
HR Analytics collects and analyzes HR data to improve decisions.
Applications:
• Recruitment efficiency
•   Attrition prediction
• Training ROI
Example: Deloitte uses predictive analytics to identify high-risk attrition roles.
Marks: Concept (2) + Application (2) + Example (1)
Q6b. Ethical Downsizing – 5 Marks
Model Answer:
Ethical downsizing means reducing workforce with fairness and empathy.
Steps: Transparent communication, voluntary exits, retraining, outplacement.
Example: Nokia retrained employees in new roles during transition.
Marks: Steps (3) + Ethics/Example (2)

a. Analysis of how gap analysis fits into HR Planning:   5
1. Identifying Current Workforce Skills: HR Planning starts with a thorough assessment
of the existing workforce. Gap analysis requires HR to evaluate the current skills,
experience, and qualifications of employees across different roles. This assessment often
includes analyzing performance reviews, skill inventories, and training histories. By
identifying the strengths and limitations of the current workforce, HR gains a clear
picture of the organization’s capabilities.
2. Defining Future Workforce Needs: To align with the organization’s strategic goals,
HR must determine what skills, roles, and competencies will be required in the future.
For example, a company aiming to expand into a new market may need salespeople with
specific market knowledge, or a manufacturing company implementing new
technologies may need employees skilled in automation. HR’s role is to define these
needs accurately and to forecast future workforce requirements based on business
objectives.

3. Assessing the Gap: Once the current and future skill sets are defined, gap analysis
involves comparing the two to identify any discrepancies. This comparison reveals
where the organization is lacking in skills, experience, or workforce numbers, and helps
HR pinpoint specific areas for improvement. For example, if a gap analysis shows that
an IT department lacks cybersecurity expertise, HR can plan to fill this gap by hiring or
training existing employees in cybersecurity.
4. Developing a Strategy to Address Gaps: After identifying skill gaps, HR planning
moves to developing strategies to bridge them. This may include a mix of recruitment,
training, and development initiatives. For roles with high skill gaps, HR might prioritize
external hiring, while smaller gaps could be addressed through upskilling or cross-
training existing employees. The goal is to ensure the organization has the required
talent to meet future demands effectively. For example, an organization might use gap
analysis to create targeted training programs or build internal career development
pathways to address specific deficiencies.
5. Implementing and Monitoring Progress: HR Planning isn’t complete without
implementing and tracking the gap-filling strategies. Monitoring progress ensures that
the organization is on track to closing identified gaps and adapting plans as necessary.
This could involve periodically reassessing the workforce to see if the gap is closing,
evaluating the effectiveness of training programs, and adjusting recruitment efforts. By
regularly revisiting the gap analysis, HR ensures the organization’s workforce remains
aligned with its strategic needs over time.
6. Supporting Strategic Decision-Making: Gap analysis as part of HR Planning provides
data-driven insights that enable leaders to make informed strategic decisions. It helps
HR communicate the importance of workforce development and provides a basis for
budget allocations toward recruitment, training, and development programs. This
alignment ensures that HR efforts directly support the organization’s growth and
competitiveness.

360-Degree Feedback: Contribution to Motivation, Fairness, and Transparency
360-degree feedback involves gathering performance feedback from multiple sources—
supervisors, peers, subordinates, and sometimes even customers. This comprehensive
feedback process contributes to improving employee motivation, ensuring fairness, and
promoting transparency in several ways:
1. Improving Motivation: Employees are more likely to feel motivated when they
receive balanced, constructive feedback from multiple perspectives. Positive
feedback reinforces strengths, while constructive criticism highlights areas for
improvement, offering a clear path for personal growth and career development.
2. Ensuring Fairness: By collecting feedback from various sources, 360-degree
feedback reduces the biases that may arise from relying on just one evaluator (such as
a manager). This holistic approach ensures that employees are assessed based on their
overall performance, not just one person's subjective view.
3. Promoting Transparency: The process encourages open communication about
performance expectations and provides employees with clear insights into how their
work is perceived by others. This transparency helps reduce misunderstandings and
builds trust within the organization.
