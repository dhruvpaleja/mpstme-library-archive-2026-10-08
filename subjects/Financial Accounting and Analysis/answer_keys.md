BL:   The Accrual Concept states that revenues and expenses are recorded in the books of
Und   accounts when they are earned or incurred, not when cash is actually received or paid.
ersta
nd   Example: If rent of ₹20,000 is payable in March but paid in April, it will still be recorded
as an expense in March (the period to which it relates).
Importance: Ensures correct matching of incomes and expenses, and gives a true picture
of financial performance for a particular period.
b. Separate Entity Concept
The Separate Entity Concept means that the business is considered a unit distinct from its
owner(s). The business’s financial records are maintained separately, and the personal
transactions of the owner are not mixed with business transactions.
Example: If the owner withdraws ₹50,000 for personal use, it is recorded as Drawings
and not as a business expense.
Importance: Provides clarity of business performance, ensures proper accountability, and
helps in correct profit measurement.

BL:   a. Accounting Cost Concept
Understand   The Cost Concept states that all assets are recorded in the books at their
original purchase price (historical cost), including expenses incurred to make
them usable. The current market value is not considered for recording
purposes.
Example: If machinery is purchased for ₹5,00,000, it will be recorded at
₹5,00,000 even if its market value later becomes ₹6,50,000.
Importance: Ensures objectivity, reliability, and uniformity in accounting
records.
b. Materiality
The Materiality Concept states that only those items or information which have
a significant impact on business decisions should be recorded and disclosed in
financial statements. Trivial or insignificant items may be ignored or clubbed
together.
Example: Stationery expenses (like pens, paper) can be directly treated as an
expense instead of being shown as an asset, since their impact is immaterial.
Importance: Saves time and effort, avoids unnecessary details, and makes
financial statements more meaningful.

Q-7   a.   [05]
CO: 1   (2.5 Marks Each)
BL:   a. Accounting Cost Concept
Understand   The Cost Concept states that all assets are recorded in the books at their
original purchase price (historical cost), including expenses incurred to make
them usable. The current market value is not considered for recording
purposes.
Example: If machinery is purchased for ₹5,00,000, it will be recorded at
₹5,00,000 even if its market value later becomes ₹6,50,000.
Importance: Ensures objectivity, reliability, and uniformity in accounting
records.
b. Materiality
The Materiality Concept states that only those items or information which have
a significant impact on business decisions should be recorded and disclosed in
financial statements. Trivial or insignificant items may be ignored or clubbed
together.
Example: Stationery expenses (like pens, paper) can be directly treated as an
expense instead of being shown as an asset, since their impact is immaterial.
Importance: Saves time and effort, avoids unnecessary details, and makes
financial statements more meaningful.

BL:   1. Stock Turnover Ratio:
Understand

The Stock Turnover Ratio (also called Inventory Turnover Ratio) measures how
efficiently a company converts its inventory into sales during a period.
It is calculated as:
Stock Turnover Ratio = Cost of Goods Sold ÷ Average Inventory
A higher ratio indicates efficient inventory management and faster sales, while
a lower ratio suggests overstocking or slow-moving goods. It helps assess the
effectiveness of inventory control and sales performance.
2. Debt to Equity Ratio:
The Debt-to-Equity Ratio indicates the proportion of long-term debt to
shareholders’ equity in a company’s capital structure.
It is calculated as:
Debt to Equity Ratio = Long-term Debt ÷ Shareholders’ Equity
A lower ratio implies financial stability and less dependence on borrowed funds,
while a higher ratio shows greater financial risk. It helps assess the firm’s long-
term solvency and financial leverage position.
