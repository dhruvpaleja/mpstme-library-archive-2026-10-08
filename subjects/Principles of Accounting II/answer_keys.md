makes job costing essential for billing and cost control.

2 b. Activity-Based Costing (ABC) offers several strategic advantages over traditional costing methods,
particularly in complex and diversified production environments. Here is a concise overview:
• Enhanced Cost Accuracy: ABC assigns overhead costs more precisely by linking them directly
to specific activities, thus reflecting the actual consumption of resources by products or services.
•   Better Cost Control and Efficiency: By identifying the cost drivers and activities that do not add
value, ABC enables organisations to streamline operations and eliminate wasteful activities,
leading to improved profitability.
• Informed Pricing and Profitability Analysis: ABC provides detailed insights into the true cost of
products or services, aiding managers in setting competitive prices and removing unprofitable
product lines.
• Improved Decision-Making: The detailed activity-based information supports strategic decisions
such as product mix optimisation, process improvements, and resource allocation.
• Supports Cost Management for Diverse Products: ABC adapts easily to varied product lines and
customised services, offering a flexible and comprehensive costing solution.

5 a.
1. Start with Net Income:
Begin analysis from the net income or profit reported on the income statement. This reflects the
company’s profitability, but on an accrual basis.
2. Adjust for Non-Cash Items:
Add back non-cash expenses such as depreciation, amortisation, and stock-based compensation.
These reduce net income but do not affect cash.
3. Analyse Changes in Working Capital:
Examine increases or decreases in current assets and liabilities:
•   Increase in Accounts Receivable and Inventory reduces cash.
•   Increase in Accounts Payable and Accrued Expenses increases cash.
These changes indicate cash tied up in operations or cash released.
4. Compare Operating Cash Flow and Operating Profit:
Verify whether the cash generated from operations matches or exceeds the operating profit. A
significant difference may indicate issues with collection or inventory.
5. Evaluate Sustainability and Liquidity:
Positive and consistent operating cash flow suggests good liquidity and operational health.
Negative cash flow calls for an investigation into operational efficiency or investment needs.
6. Trend Analysis:
Examine cash flow trends over multiple periods to understand growth, operational momentum, or
cash flow issues.

ABC provides more accurate overhead allocation in following manner:
•   Traditional costing allocates overhead based on a single cost driver such as direct labour
hours, which can distort product costs when multiple products consume overhead activities
differently.
•   ABC identifies multiple activities such as machine setups, inspections, material handling,
etc., and assigns costs to these activities (cost pools).
•   Overhead is then traced to products based on their actual use of each activity (cost
driver), resulting in a more precise product cost reflecting resource consumption.
Challenges in implementing ABC:
•   Complexity and cost: Implementation requires detailed data collection and continuous
monitoring of activities and cost drivers, increasing administrative burden.
•   Identification of activities and drivers: It may be difficult to accurately identify relevant
activities and appropriate cost drivers, especially in complex operations.
•   Resistance to change: Employees and management may resist due to the disruption of
existing costing processes and the need for training.

4 b. Importance of Cash Flow Statements to Investors
1.   Liquidity Assessment:
Investors use cash flow statements to evaluate a company’s ability to generate cash from operations
and meet short-term obligations such as debt repayments, salaries, and supplier payments.
2.   Financial Health and Stability:
Cash flow reports show whether a company’s core operations produce sufficient cash, an indicator
of sustainable profitability beyond accounting profits.
3.   Investment and Dividend Decisions:
Positive operating cash flow indicates the company can invest in growth projects or pay dividends,
which is crucial information for shareholders.
********** 7 **********

4.   Identify Cash Management Efficiency:
Cash flow statements reveal how well management handles cash inflows and outflows, helping
investors gauge operational efficiency and risk.
5.   Detect Financial Distress:
Negative or declining cash flows highlight potential liquidity problems, allowing investors to assess
the risk of insolvency earlier than profit reports might show.
6.   Comparative Analysis:
Investors compare cash flow trends over time or across companies to understand relative
performance and financial strength.
7.   Transparency on Non-Cash Profits:
Since profits may include non-cash items, cash flow statements provide a clearer picture of actual
cash generated.
8.   Support Risk Assessment:
Cash flow analysis aids in evaluating the company’s ability to withstand economic downturns or
financial shocks, influencing investment decisions.
