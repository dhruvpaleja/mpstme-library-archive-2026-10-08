A. First impressions influence a person’s personal brand in professional settings. (Any 6   8
points- 6 marks)
•   First impressions are the initial judgments formed about a person within a few seconds
of meeting them.
•   In professional settings, first impressions strongly influence how others perceive a
person’s confidence, professionalism, and competence.
•   Factors such as appearance, body language, communication style, facial expressions,
and behaviour affect these impressions.
•   A positive first impression helps build trust, credibility, and professional relationships.
•   A negative first impression may reduce opportunities and create misunderstandings.
•   First impressions also influence future interactions because people tend to remember
their initial perception.
Example: (2 marks)
•   During a job interview, a candidate who is well-dressed, maintains eye contact, speaks
confidently, and behaves politely is likely to create a strong and positive personal brand.
•   The interviewer may perceive the candidate as professional, capable, and trustworthy.

B. Evolution of Personal Branding (4 stages – 1.5 marks each)   8
1. Early Traditional Period
•   In earlier times, personal branding was not formally recognized as a concept.
•   A person’s reputation was mainly built through:
◦   Family background
◦   Social status
◦   Word-of-mouth communication
◦   Professional conduct
•   Leaders, teachers, artists, and businesspeople gained recognition through their work and
public image.
2. Industrial and Corporate Era
•   During the industrial revolution and corporate growth, professionalism became
important.
•   Employers focused on:
◦   Discipline
◦   Communication skills
◦   Professional appearance
◦   Work ethics
•   Resumes, interviews, and networking started influencing personal image.
•   Individuals began consciously managing their professional reputation.
3. Rise of Marketing and Branding Concepts
•   In the late 20th century, branding concepts used for products and companies started
being applied to individuals.
•   The idea that “people can market themselves like brands” became popular.
•   Tom Peters popularized the term “Personal Branding” through his article “The Brand
Called You” in 1997.
•   People started focusing on:
◦   Unique identity
◦   Competitive advantage
◦   Self-promotion
◦   Career positioning
4. Digital and Social Media Era
•   The internet and social media have completely transformed personal branding.
•   Platforms such as:
◦   LinkedIn
◦   Instagram

B. Evaluation of Positioning Strategies (Any 4 points – 1.5 mark each)   8
1. Premium Pricing Strategy
•   The company positioned the smartphone as a premium product.
•   High pricing created a perception of exclusivity.
•   However, customers may not see enough unique value.
2. Weak Differentiation
•   The product appears similar to competitor brands.
•   Lack of meaningful Points of Difference (PODs).
3. Target Market Positioning
•   Young professionals expect innovation, productivity, and modern features.
•   Positioning may not clearly match customer expectations.
4. Brand Communication Issues
•   Marketing may have failed to communicate unique benefits effectively.
5. Competitive Positioning Problem
•   Competitors may offer similar features at lower prices.
Suggestions (2 marks)
•   Improve differentiation through unique features.
•   Strengthen emotional and functional benefits.
•   Communicate clear value proposition.
Reconsider pricing strategy.
