2. Read the case and answer the following.   15
The Raman family owns and operates “Raman Textiles,” a third-generation family business
specializing in textile manufacturing. Founded by Mr. Vijay Raman in 1970, the company is
currently managed by his two sons, Arjun and Rohit. The third generation, consisting of four
grandchildren aged between 25 and 35, is preparing to take on roles in the business.
Despite steady business growth, the family is experiencing several internal challenges. Arjun
and Rohit disagree about who from the next generation should assume leadership roles. One
grandchild, Nisha, has demonstrated strong managerial ability, but her role remains uncertain
as some family members prefer seniority-based involvement rather than merit-based
selection.
Disagreements have also emerged regarding ownership distribution among third-generation
members, particularly because some are actively involved in the business while others are not.
Family discussions about these matters often become emotional and unstructured, resulting in
unresolved conflicts and growing tension among family members.
As the business continues to expand and more family members become involved, the Raman
family is finding it increasingly difficult to coordinate expectations, responsibilities, and
decisions affecting both the family and the business.
Questions:
a. How can a family constitution address the key issues faced by the Raman family? (5
Marks)
b. What role can independent directors play in this scenario? (5 Marks)
c. Explain the role of a family council in managing family relationships and decision-making
in a family business like Raman Textiles.   (5 Marks)
