Family businesses often face governance challenges due to personal conflicts, resistance
to professionalization, and unclear succession plans. Implementing clear policies,
structured leadership training, and external advisory boards helps businesses navigate
these issues. By addressing these challenges proactively, family enterprises can ensure
stability and long-term success.

constitution.
2. Lack of Professional Management → Solution: Hire experienced professionals

external audits.

workshops.

unbiased advice.

The stewardship theory is a perspective in organizational and family business studies
that emphasizes the role of leaders as stewards- individuals who prioritize the long-term
success of the business and the welfare of stakeholders over personal gain. Unlike
agency theory, which assumes managers act in self-interest and require monitoring,
stewardship theory assumes managers (especially family members) are intrinsically
motivated to act in the best interests of the organization. In family businesses,
stewardship resonates strongly because leaders often see themselves as caretakers of a
legacy, responsible for passing the business to future generations rather than maximizing
short-term profits.
1. Intrinsic Motivation- Leaders are motivated by values, purpose, and commitment
rather than external rewards. Family members often feel emotionally connected to the
business and its legacy.
2. Collective Orientation- Focus on collective goals over individual interests. Decisions
prioritize family harmony, continuity, and shared prosperity.
3. Trust & Commitment- Assumes trust between owners and managers, reducing the
need for strict monitoring. Family ties reinforce trust, lowering governance costs
compared to non-family firms.
4. Long-Term Perspective Emphasis on sustainability and transgenerational success.
Leaders act as caretakers, ensuring the business thrives for future generations.
5. Identity & Values Alignment-Stewardship is rooted in shared identity and values.
Family culture and traditions guide business practices, strengthening cohesion.
6. Governance Structures-Stewardship can evolve through family-dense,
professionalized, or external governance models. Families adapt governance depending
on generational transitions and complexity.

A family constitution is a formal document that defines principles, policies, roles, and
decision-making processes governing the relationship between the family and the business.
• Succession clarity – Establishes objective criteria for leadership selection based on
qualifications and competence.
• Role definition – Clearly defines responsibilities and eligibility for participation in the
business, reducing role ambiguity.
• Ownership guidelines – Provides transparent rules for share distribution, buyouts, and
rights of active and non-active members.
• Conflict resolution mechanism – Sets structured procedures for managing disagreements
and preventing escalation.
• Communication structure – Formalises meetings and decision-making processes to
improve coordination among family members.
