Since beer and pizza are complements, a rise in the price of beer will affect the market for pizza. When the
price of beer increases, people may buy less beer and, consequently, less pizza, as the two goods are often
consumed together.
Effect on the Pizza Market
Let’s analyze the impact on demand, supply, quantity supplied, quantity demanded, and price in the
market for pizza, step by step:
1. Demand for Pizza
Decrease in demand: When the price of beer rises, fewer people will buy beer, and since beer and pizza
are consumed together, this will cause a decrease in demand for pizza.
Demand curve shifts left: This reduction in demand is represented by a leftward shift in the demand
curve for pizza. At every price level, consumers will now demand less pizza because they are buying less
beer
2. Supply for Pizza
No immediate change in supply: The price of beer affects the demand for pizza, but it does not directly
impact the supply of pizza. Suppliers have no reason to change the amount of pizza they are willing to
supply at each price level unless the overall price or cost of pizza production changes.
3. Quantity Demanded and Quantity Supplied
Quantity demanded decreases: Due to the decrease in demand (leftward shift of the demand curve), the
quantity of pizza demanded at the previous equilibrium price will decrease.

Quantity supplied decreases: With the lower demand for pizza, the new equilibrium quantity supplied
will also be lower. However, this change is not due to a shift in the supply curve but rather a movement

4. Price of Pizza
Decrease in price: As demand decreases, there is excess supply at the previous price level, creating
downward pressure on the price of pizza. The market will adjust to a lower equilibrium price where the
new, reduced demand equals the quantity supplied.

B. Income Elasticity of Demand (YED)   12
Income Elasticity of Demand (YED) measures how the quantity demanded of a good responds to changes in
consumers' income. It is calculated as:
Income Elasticity of Demand=% Change in Quantity Demanded/% Change in Income
YED > 0: The good is a normal good (demand increases with higher income).
YED < 0: The good is an inferior good (demand decreases as income increases).
YED > 1: The good is a luxury good (demand increases significantly with income).
Cross Price Elasticity of Demand (XED)
Cross Price Elasticity of Demand (XED) measures how the quantity demanded of one good responds to
changes in the price of another good. It is calculated as:
Cross Price Elasticity of Demand=% Change in Quantity Demanded of Good A/% Change in Price of Good B
· XED > 0: The goods are substitutes (e.g., tea and coffee).
XED < 0: The goods are complements (e.g., bread and butter).
XED = 0: The goods are unrelated.

The problem states that the quantity demanded of a good decreases by 30%, while the total revenue of the
good increases by 15% after a price change.
To determine whether the demand curve is elastic or inelastic, we need to recall the relationship between price
elasticity of demand and total revenue:
Elastic demand (PED > 1): When demand is elastic, an increase in price leads to a more than
proportional decrease in quantity demanded, causing total revenue to decrease.
Inelastic demand (PED < 1): When demand is inelastic, an increase in price leads to a less than
proportional decrease in quantity demanded, causing total revenue to increase.
Interpretation
Since total revenue increases by 15% despite a 30% decrease in quantity demanded, we can conclude
that:
The price increase must have caused the total revenue to rise.
This can only happen if the demand is inelastic because, in the case of inelastic demand, the percentage
increase in price more than offsets the percentage decrease in quantity demanded.
Thus, the demand curve is inelastic in this case.

sourcing.
Arguments for U-shape: coordination, communication costs rise with size, leading to diseconomies.

the kitchen. This would likely increase productivity and lower the average cost per pizza because fixed
costs (like rent and ovens) are spread over a larger output, illustrating economies of scale in the pizzeria’s

•   In the method of breaking up price effect by compensating variation we adjust the
income of the consumer so as to offset the change in satisfaction resulting from the change in

initial level of satisfaction which he was obtaining before the change in price occurred.

2.   Answer the following(Attempt Any 1 Question)   12
A. Price effect: when the price of commodity decreases the quantity demanded increases. The   12
change in quantity due to change in price is called price effect.
•   In the method of breaking up price effect by compensating variation we adjust the
income of the consumer so as to offset the change in satisfaction resulting from the change in
price of a good and bring the consumer back to his original indifference curve, that is, his
initial level of satisfaction which he was obtaining before the change in price occurred.
•   To offset this gain in satisfaction resulting from a fall in price of the good we must take
away from the consumer enough income to force him to come back to his original
indifference curve.
•   This required reduction in income (say, through levying a lump sum tax) to cancel out
the gain in satisfaction or welfare occurred by reduction in price of a good is called
compensating variation in income. This is so called because it compensates (in a negative
way) for the gain in satisfaction resulting from a price reduction of the commodity.
•   Substitution effect means the change in the quantity purchased of a good as a
consequence of a change in its relative price alone, real income or level of satisfaction
remaining constant.
•   It is thus clear that a fall in relative price of a commodity always leads to the increase in
its quantity demanded due to the substitution effect, the consumer’s satisfaction or
indifference curve remaining the same.
•   Thus the substitution effect is always negative. The negative substitution effect implies
that the relative price of a commodity and its quantity demanded change in opposite direction,
that is, the decline in relative price of a commodity always causes increase in its quantity
demanded.
