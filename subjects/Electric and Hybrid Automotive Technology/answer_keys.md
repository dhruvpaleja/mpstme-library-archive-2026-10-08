Q1 (a) — Define Gradeability, Max Speed, Acceleration Performance [5 marks]
• Gradeability: Maximum gradient/slope (% or degrees) a vehicle can climb at a specified speed. 30% grade = 30 m
rise per 100 m horizontal. Depends on motor torque, transmission ratio, vehicle weight. [1.7]
• Maximum Speed: Highest speed on flat road. Limited by motor power and aerodynamic drag (∝ v²). Typical: 120–
180 km/hr for consumer EVs. [1.7]
• Acceleration Performance: Time for 0–100 km/hr. EVs excel — instant peak torque from electric motors. Typical: 3–
8 s consumer, <3 s performance. [1.6]
Ref: Unit 2 — Vehicle Performance Metrics

Q1 (b) — Regenerative Braking Principle & Efficiency [5 marks]
• Principle: During braking, motor becomes generator. Wheels drive motor shaft → motor generates electricity →
charges battery. Conventional: kinetic energy → heat (wasted). [2]
• Efficiency improvement: Recovers 10–25% of total energy. Most effective 80–30 km/hr deceleration. Extends range
15–30% in city driving (frequent stops). Reduces brake wear 2–3×. [2]
• Limitations: Less effective <30 km/hr and when battery full (limited charge acceptance). Friction brakes still needed
for emergency stops. [1]
Ref: Unit 2 — Regenerative Braking Systems
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Q1 (c) — Motor Calculations (80 kW, 250 Nm, 3000 RPM) [5 marks]
• (i) Torque at rated power (80 kW at 9000 RPM): ω = 2π×9000/60 = 942.5 rad/s. T = P/ω = 80000/942.5 = 84.9 Nm.
[1.7]
• (ii) Power at max torque (250 Nm at 3000 RPM): ω = 2π×3000/60 = 314.2 rad/s. P = 250×314.2 = 78.5 kW. [1.7]
• (iii) Wheel radius: Wheel RPM = 9000/10 = 900. ω_wheel = 2π×900/60 = 94.25 rad/s. v = 150 km/hr = 41.67 m/s. r =
v/ω = 41.67/94.25 = 0.442 m ≈ 44 cm diameter. [1.6]
Ref: Unit 3 — Motor Sizing Calculations

Q1 (d) — Impact of Mass Reduction on EV Performance [5 marks]
• 1. Rolling resistance: F_rr = Crr·m·g. 100 kg reduction → 6.5% less rolling resistance → ~3–4% range increase city.
[1.25]
• 2. Acceleration energy: E = ½mv². 100 kg less → 6% less acceleration energy → 5–8% range increase stop-and-go.
[1.25]
• 3. Grading resistance: F_g = m·g·sin(θ). 100 kg less → 6.5% less grading force → 5–10% improvement hilly terrain.
[1.25]

Q2 — EV Classifications and Charging Infrastructure [20 marks]
(a) EV Types (BEV, HEV, PHEV, FCEV) [5 marks]
• BEV: Battery only, 100% electric, 0 emissions. Battery → Motor → Wheels. [1.25]
• HEV: ICE + motor, no plug-in, regen only. ICE + Motor → Wheels. [1.25]
• PHEV: Pluggable hybrid, 20–80 km EV range then ICE. Battery + ICE → Motor → Wheels. [1.25]
• FCEV: Hydrogen fuel cell → electricity → motor. 3–5 min refuel, 400–600 km range. H₂ Tank → Fuel Cell →
Motor → Wheels. [1.25]
(b) Charging Levels [5 marks]
• Level 1: 120 V / 12 A / 1.4 kW / 16–20 hrs. Standard household outlet. [1.7]
• Level 2: 240 V / 32 A / 7 kW / 4–8 hrs. Home/workplace wall box. [1.7]
• DC Fast: 50–350 kW / 20–40 min to 80%. Highway stations (CHAdeMO/CCS connector). [1.6]
(c) CHAdeMO vs CCS [5 marks]
• CHAdeMO: Japanese (Nissan/Mitsubishi), 62.5 kW standard / 400 kW max. Separate DC connector. Dominant in
Japan. [2.5]
• CCS: European/American (VW/BMW/Ford/GM), 50–350 kW. Combined AC+DC connector. Becoming global
standard outside Japan/China. [2.5]
(d) V2G and V2H Technologies [5 marks]
• V2G: Vehicle battery supplies power to grid during peak demand. Benefits: Grid stabilization, revenue for EV owner,
renewable integration. [2.5]
• V2H: EV as home backup power. Challenges: Battery degradation (extra cycles), bidirectional charger cost ($2000+),
grid interconnection standards. [2.5]

Q4 — Power Electronics for Electric Vehicles [20 marks]
(a) Three-Phase VSI [5 marks]
• Structure: 6 IGBTs (T1–T6) + 6 flyback diodes. 3 half-bridges (each: 2 IGBTs for one phase). DC bus in, 3-phase AC
out. [2.5]

• Switching: 120° conduction sequence. 8 states (6 active vectors + 2 zero). PWM generates sinusoidal voltage. 3–20
kHz switching typical. [2.5]
(b) Converter Calculations [5 marks]
• I_out = 60,000/350 = 171.4 A. P_in = 60,000/0.95 = 63,158 W. I_in = 63,158/400 = 157.9 A. P_loss = 63,158 −
60,000 = 3,158 W. [5]
(c) SVPWM vs Sinusoidal PWM [5 marks]
• SVPWM: 15% better DC bus utilization (2/√3 vs 1 for sine PWM). Lower THD. Better efficiency. More complex
(DSP/FPGA required). [3]
• Sine PWM: Simpler, intuitive, good for basic drives. Max modulation index 1.0 vs 1.15 for SVPWM. [2]
(d) SiC/GaN Wide Bandgap Semiconductors [5 marks]
• Advantages: 3× higher switching frequency (200 kHz vs 20 kHz Si), 50% lower losses, 200 °C operation, compact
magnetics. [3]
• Barriers: 3–5× current cost (decreasing annually), limited availability, requires new gate driver designs. [2]

Q5 — Mobile Manipulator Software Architecture [20 marks]
(a) Software Modules [5 marks]
• Modules: Localization (AMCL/EKF), Mapping (SLAM/Costmap), Global planning (A*/Dijkstra), Local planning
(DWA/TEB), Motion control (PID velocity), Manipulation (MoveIt), Sensor processing (filters/fusion). [3]
• Interaction flow: Sensors → Localization+Mapping → Planning → Control → Actuators. [2]
(b) ROS2 Autonomous Navigation Steps [5 marks]
• Steps: (1) Build map (SLAM with LiDAR+odom). (2) Save map. (3) Launch nav stack (map_server, AMCL,

• 1. Node delays >100 ms. Fix: RT executor, reduce queue. 2. Sensor sync. Fix: TimeSynchronizer. 3. TF errors. Fix:

interface libraries. [5]

• Simulation (PyBullet/Gazebo): Fast, safe, cheap. Weak on sensor noise. Good for algorithm dev. [1.7]
• HIL: Real sensors + simulated robot. Good middle ground. [1.7]
• Full robot: Real world, essential for deployment. Expensive/slow. Strategy: 70% sim, 20% HIL, 10% real. [1.6]

Q6 — Sensor Systems & Failure Handling [20 marks]
(a) Sensor Suite Design [5 marks]
• 2D LiDAR (Hokuyo UTM-30LX, 270°, 30 m): Navigation/SLAM. Top center mount. [1.25]
• RGB-D Camera (Intel RealSense D435i): Object detection/grasp. Front mount. [1.25]
• IMU (BNO055): Orientation/acceleration. Central, near CG. [1.25]
• Wheel encoders (2000 PPR): Odometry. Wheel hubs. + 8× Ultrasonic (HC-SR04): Close-range <3 m. Perimeter.
[1.25]
(b) Calibration Procedures [5 marks]
• IMU: Static bias removal (1 min stationary average). Rotate 6 orientations for scale factors. [1.7]
• Camera: Checkerboard 20+ images → cv2.calibrateCamera for intrinsics. ArUco markers for extrinsics (R+T to robot
frame). [1.7]
• Encoders: Measure wheel circumference (push 10 m, count ticks) → ticks/meter. Critical for accurate navigation.
[1.6]

(c) Sensor Fusion (EKF vs Particle Filter) [5 marks]
• EKF: Fuses wheel odom (high freq, drifts) + IMU (orientation) + LiDAR (global correction). Gaussian assumption.
Low computation. Good for well-mapped environments. [2.5]
• Particle Filter: Non-Gaussian, handles multi-modal distributions. Better for kidnapped robot problem. Higher
computation but more robust. [2.5]
(d) Failure Handling Strategies [5 marks]
• LiDAR fail → cameras+sonar only, reduce speed 50%. Odom fail → visual SLAM only, warn operator. [1.7]
• IMU fail → magnetometer backup, dead reckoning. Complete loss → E-stop, audio alarm, manual control. [1.7]
• Implementation: Watchdog timers, timeout detection (>500 ms), redundant sensor paths, graceful degradation. [1.6]
Ref: Unit 7 — Sensor Integration, Calibration, Robustness

Q7 — Series-Parallel HEV Design [20 marks]
(a) Architecture with Planetary Gear [5 marks]
• Components: ICE 70 kW → Planetary gear (carrier), MG1 30 kW (sun, generator), MG2 60 kW (ring, traction
motor), Battery. [2]
• Planetary constraint: ω_carrier = f(ω_sun, ω_ring). ICE speed independent of vehicle speed via MG1 control. CVT
effect. [3]
(b) Powertrain Design [5 marks]
• Specs: 1500 kg, 30 km/L equiv, 150 km/hr max, 40 km EV range, 0–100 in 10 s. [1]
• Selection: Engine 60–70 kW Atkinson cycle (peak BSFC), Motor 50–60 kW PMSM, Generator 30 kW, Battery 8–10
kWh Li-ion (40 km × 200 Wh/km). Combined 130 kW for acceleration. [4]
(c) Mode Management Strategy [5 marks]
• EV mode: P_req <30 kW & SOC >40% → battery only. Engine start: P_req >30 kW or SOC <25%. [2]
• Power split: ICE at optimal BSFC point, MG1 balances, excess charges battery. Series at low speed, parallel at
highway. [2]
• Regen: All decelerations, intensity proportional to brake pedal. Priority: regen → friction brake. Target: 40–45 MPG.
[1]
(d) Simulation (AdVisor/MATLAB) [5 marks]
• Components to model: ICE (fuel map), Motor (efficiency map), Battery (Thevenin), Transmission (gear ratios),
Vehicle (mass, Cd, Crr). [2]
• Process: Step through drive cycle → calculate forces → powertrain control logic → energy flows → SOC update.
[1.5]
• Validation: Compare fuel economy vs published data (±5%), verify operating points on engine BSFC map, validate
thermal behavior. [1.5]
