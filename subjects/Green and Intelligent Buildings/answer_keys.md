Q6
A   1. Reduce   10
• Meaning: Minimizing the generation of waste at the source.
• Objective: To use fewer natural resources and reduce pollution.
• Examples:
o Opting for energy-efficient appliances.
o Avoiding single-use plastics.
o Encouraging sustainable design and responsible consumption.
• Impact: Less resource extraction, lower emissions, reduced landfill load.
2. Reuse
• Meaning: Using products or materials multiple times for the same or new
purposes.
• Objective: Extend product lifespan and delay waste disposal.
• Examples:
o Reusing containers, packaging, or construction materials.
o Refurbishing furniture or electronic items.
• Impact: Reduces demand for new resources and decreases waste generation.
3. Recycle
• Meaning: Processing waste materials into new, usable products.
• Objective: Convert discarded materials into valuable resources.
• Examples:
o Recycling paper, metals, glass, and plastics.
o Using construction and demolition waste in road base or aggregates.
• Impact: Conserves energy, saves raw materials, and lowers environmental
pollution.

Q1   [20]
a)   Total energy consumed in extraction, processing, manufacturing, transport &
installation of building materials.
- Includes direct + indirect energy.
- High embodied energy → higher carbon footprint.
- Materials like aluminium, steel have high values; wood, fly ash blocks are low.
- Influences material selection for sustainability.
- Aim: Reduce embodied energy for overall efficiency.
b)   LEED (USA): Points-based, focuses on energy, water, site, materials, indoor air
quality.
- IGBC (India): Adapted from LEED but localized to Indian climate & materials.
- IGBC gives Platinum–Gold–Silver–Certified levels
- LEED uses global benchmarks; IGBC suits Indian economy.
- Both promote sustainable design but differ in regional relevance.
c)   Ratings (e.g., GRIHA, IGBC, LEED India) encourage reusing developed land.
- Incentives: Property tax rebates, extra FSI, faster clearances.
- Promotes urban renewal without greenfield expansion.
- Improves infrastructure & sustainability of existing sites.
- States like Maharashtra, Telangana, Gujarat provide fiscal benefits for
GRIHA/IGBC-certified buildings.
