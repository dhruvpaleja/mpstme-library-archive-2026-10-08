Lady Bracknell personifies the strict societal norms of the Victorian upper class, particularly in her views
on marriage and class. Her obsession with social status, wealth, and the suitability of a match reveals the
rigid, transactional nature of relationships in her social sphere. She enforces these values, showing how
class, wealth, and social connections are prioritized over genuine affection.
3. The characters of Blanche, Stella, and Stanley challenge or reinforce gender norms of the time.   5
Blanche’s refined femininity and sexual desires contrast with Stanley’s dominance and aggression, while
Stella embodies traditional femininity through her submission to Stanley’s will.
Example: Blanche’s flirtations with Mitch (“I’ve got to be good and keep up the act!”) represent an
idealized femininity, whereas Stanley's brutishness, as shown in his treatment of women, reinforces the
masculine ideal of control. Stella’s return to Stanley after his outbursts reflects societal norms where
women were expected to endure male dominance.

2. Ruskin Bond uses Masterji to explore pride, shame, and self-worth through his emotional conflict.   5
Masterji feels ashamed of his situation but is also proud of his teaching and his impact on students.
Example: Despite his pride in the respect he commands from his students, Masterji's internal struggle is
evident when he grapples with his feelings of worthlessness due to the lack of recognition from the
system.
3. Márquez blends the ordinary with the extraordinary by introducing a winged old man into a mundane   5
coastal village. One example is how the old man’s wings are described in a realistic, almost unimpressive
manner, rather than as something divine. Another example is the townspeople’s casual acceptance of
supernatural events, such as the arrival of the spider-woman, without questioning their logic.

The poem traces the speaker’s awareness of mortality through the symbolic carriage ride. The school,
fields of grain, and setting sun represent childhood, maturity, and old age, suggesting a review of her
completed life. The chill air and thin clothing indicate physical vulnerability and death. The “house” in
the ground symbolizes the grave. In the final stanza, centuries feel shorter than a day, showing that
earthly time has dissolved into eternity. This progression illustrates her movement from mortal life to
timeless existence.
