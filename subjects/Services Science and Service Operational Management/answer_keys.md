Ans:
Customer-Supplier Duality (5M): In service supply chains, the customer is

(supplying the labor), the service (pumping gas) cannot be completed.

Q3   [10]
a). How does the hub-and-spoke model differ from the traditional linear supply
chain model in service management?
Explain with the help of an example.
Ans:
Difference (5M):
A traditional linear supply chain is a sequential flow from supplier to
manufacturer to distributor to customer. In service, this is rare. The hub-and-
spoke model is a centralized model where a central "hub" (e.g., a data center, [10]
call center) provides services and support to multiple decentralized "spokes"
(e.g., retail branches, local offices, remote users). The hub centralizes
resources and expertise, improving efficiency and consistency.
Example (5M - e.g., a bank's call center): A traditional model would
require each branch to have its own customer service representatives to
handle every type of query. In a hub-and-spoke model, the central call
center (hub) handles all complex customer service inquiries (technical
issues, account disputes), while the local bank branches (spokes) handle
********** 4 **********
--- page 5 ---
only simple, in-person transactions. This model allows the bank to leverage
specialized expertise and technology centrally, providing a consistent service
standard across all locations.
b)   Goods-Dominant Logic (GDL) and Service-Dominant Logic (SDL)
represent two contrasting perspectives on value creation and business
strategy:
Goods-Dominant Logic (GDL)
• Focus: Tangible products
• Value Creation: Value is embedded in the product during
manufacturing
• Customer Role: Passive recipient
• Marketing View: Exchange of goods; production-centric
• Example: Selling a smartphone as a finished product
[10]
Service-Dominant Logic (SDL)
• Focus: Intangible services and experiences
• Value Creation: Value is co-created through interaction between
provider and customer
• Customer Role: Active participant in value creation
• Marketing View: Relationship-centric; emphasizes use and
experience
• Example: Fitness coaching where value emerges through ongoing
engagement

• Ordering Cost = No. of orders * Ordering cost per unit
