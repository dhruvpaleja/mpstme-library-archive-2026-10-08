The Classical Gold Standard was an international monetary system that lasted roughly from 1875 to
1914. During this period, major economies, including Great Britain, France, Germany, and the United
States, adhered to a system where their national currencies were directly linked to gold. Each
participating country pegged its currency to a specific amount of gold, and exchange rates between
currencies were determined by their gold content. This system provided fixed exchange rates, creating a
stable environment for international trade and investment .
Key Features of the Classical Gold Standard:
1. Unrestricted Coinage of Gold: Gold was freely coined and could be exchanged for currency.
2. Two-way Convertibility: National currencies were convertible into gold at a fixed rate, ensuring a
stable exchange rate.
3. Free Movement of Gold: Gold could move freely between countries, maintaining balance in
international payments.
4. Price-Specie-Flow Mechanism: Any imbalance in trade between countries would be corrected
through gold flows, adjusting the domestic money supply and restoring equilibrium.
(3 Marks)
Advantages of the Gold Standard
1. Exchange Rate Stability: Since exchange rates were pegged to gold, they remained stable, which
facilitated predictable international trade and investment.
2. Automatic Adjustment Mechanism: Trade imbalances were automatically corrected through the
flow of gold between countries, which influenced price levels and adjusted demand.
3. Inflation Control: Gold's limited supply restricted money supply expansion, which helped control
inflation and promote price stability.
(3 Marks)
Limitations of the Gold Standard

1. Deflationary Pressure: The limited supply of gold could not support the rapid growth of global
trade and investment, causing deflation and constraining economic growth.
2. Lack of Flexibility in Monetary Policy: Countries on the gold standard had limited ability to
implement independent monetary policies since they needed to maintain gold reserves to support
their currency.
3. Vulnerability to Economic Shocks: Economic crises could disrupt the stability of the system. For
instance, World War I led most countries to suspend the gold standard as they faced significant
financial pressures.
4. No Mechanism to Enforce Compliance: Countries could abandon the gold standard if it conflicted
with national interests, as there was no central authority enforcing the system.

The Classical Gold Standard was an international monetary system that lasted roughly from 1875 to
1914. During this period, major economies, including Great Britain, France, Germany, and the United
States, adhered to a system where their national currencies were directly linked to gold. Each
participating country pegged its currency to a specific amount of gold, and exchange rates between
currencies were determined by their gold content. This system provided fixed exchange rates, creating a
stable environment for international trade and investment .
Key Features of the Classical Gold Standard:
1. Unrestricted Coinage of Gold: Gold was freely coined and could be exchanged for currency.
2. Two-way Convertibility: National currencies were convertible into gold at a fixed rate, ensuring a
stable exchange rate.
3. Free Movement of Gold: Gold could move freely between countries, maintaining balance in
international payments.
4. Price-Specie-Flow Mechanism: Any imbalance in trade between countries would be corrected
through gold flows, adjusting the domestic money supply and restoring equilibrium.
(3 Marks)
Advantages of the Gold Standard
1. Exchange Rate Stability: Since exchange rates were pegged to gold, they remained stable, which
facilitated predictable international trade and investment.
2. Automatic Adjustment Mechanism: Trade imbalances were automatically corrected through the
flow of gold between countries, which influenced price levels and adjusted demand.
3. Inflation Control: Gold's limited supply restricted money supply expansion, which helped control
inflation and promote price stability.
(3 Marks)
Limitations of the Gold Standard
Page 4 of 7
--- page 5 ---
1. Deflationary Pressure: The limited supply of gold could not support the rapid growth of global
trade and investment, causing deflation and constraining economic growth.
2. Lack of Flexibility in Monetary Policy: Countries on the gold standard had limited ability to
implement independent monetary policies since they needed to maintain gold reserves to support
their currency.
3. Vulnerability to Economic Shocks: Economic crises could disrupt the stability of the system. For
instance, World War I led most countries to suspend the gold standard as they faced significant
financial pressures.
4. No Mechanism to Enforce Compliance: Countries could abandon the gold standard if it conflicted
with national interests, as there was no central authority enforcing the system.

ADRs simplify access to foreign companies, allowing U.S. investors to diversify their

b. Cross-listing shares offers several advantages for multinational corporations, such as:   5
Expansion of Investor Base: Cross-listing allows a company to tap into a broader range
of investors in foreign markets, potentially increasing demand for its stock. This demand
can enhance the market price of the stock and improve liquidity .
Name Recognition and Capital Access: Listing in a foreign market can increase a
company’s visibility and brand recognition, helping it access equity or debt capital more
easily in that region. This is particularly beneficial for companies from emerging markets
with limited domestic capital .
Enhanced Corporate Governance: Listing in markets with strict regulatory requirements,
like the United States or the United Kingdom, may enhance the company’s corporate
governance. This enhanced transparency often appeals to investors, leading to potentially
higher valuations .
Protection Against Hostile Takeovers: Cross-listing can broaden the company’s investor
base, making it more challenging for a single party to gain control, thereby providing a
layer of defiance against hostile takeovers .
These advantages collectively can lower a firm's cost of capital, improve share valuation,
and strengthen its competitive positioning in global markets.
