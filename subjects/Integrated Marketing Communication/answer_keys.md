Segmenting & Targeting 5 marks for each topic
 Geographic segmentation divides markets by geographic locations such
as nations, states, regions, or cities.
 Demographic segmentation divides markets based on demographic
variables such as gender, age, education, race, and life stage.
 Socioeconomic segmentation divides markets based on socioeconomic
variables such as income, education, and occupation.
 Psychographic segmentation divides markets based on personality
values or lifestyle. VALS is a popular approach to lifestyle segmentation
Segmentation based on the buying situation includes:
 Behavioral segmentation divides a market into groups according to their

service.
 Outlet types segments a market based on the type of store where a
product is sold, such as convenience, supermarket, mass merchandiser,

 Benefit segmentation divides markets on the basis of the specific benefits

product or service.
TARGETING
 Undifferentiated marketing ignores segment differences and offers just
one product or service to the entire market.
 Differentiated marketing involves marketing in a number of segments,

 Concentrated marketing involves selecting, and trying to capture a large
share of, a single segment.

AIDA
Awareness: creating brand awareness or affiliation with your product or
service.
Interest: generating interest in the benefits of your product or service, and
sufficient interest to encourage the buyer to start to research further.
Desire: for your product or service through an 'emotional connection', showing

Action: CTA - Move the buyer to interact with your company and taking the next
step i.e. downloading a brochure, making the phone call, joining your
newsletter, or engaging in live chat, etc.
INNOVATION ADOPTION MODEL
For adopting a new product, at first, the consumer becomes aware of the new
product but does not have information about it. The consumer shows interest and
searches for information about the new product. In the third stage, the consumer
evaluates whether trying the new product is worthwhile. After that, the consumer

bases of segmentation such as lifestyles often prove to be more effective for the marketing of
some products. The reason for this is that one’s lifestyle may better reflect what products
and/or services consumers might need or want. Consider the following examples:
Singles v. families —single males and females clearly have a different lifestyle than those
who are married, and certainly those with families. Singles may be more socially active,
frequenting bars and clubs, maybe attending college and perhaps have more time for sports,
entertainment, etc. If they have just started a career, much of their attention may be focused
on that aspect of their lives. Think for a moment about their purchase behaviors. Sports
equipment and work clothing may account for much of their expenditures. A significant part
of the budget may be allocated for entertainment purposes such as travel, etc. On the other

movies or sports events for bars and clubs. Money will have to be allocated for purchasing of

necessary. Home cooked meals may substitute for eating out or packaged foods designed for

Similar demographics/different lifestyles—is a 35 year old male, employed as an attorney

BUDGET DETERMINATION METHODS
Affordable
•   Money allocated after all other expenses
•   Spend as much as you can afford
Percentage of Sales
•   A specified percentage of sales of the current year, succeeding year, or average of few
years
•   Simple but not logical
Unit of Sales
•   A fixed amount is taken as budget per unit of item sold
Industry Standards
•   Matching budgets with industry average or competition
•   Creates parity with competition but disregards company’s individual needs
Objective-and-task
•   1. Establish objectives 2. Ascertain tasks 3. Price tasks
•   Difficult, but logical
Payout Planning
•   Estimating expenditures, expected returns in terms of sales, and time necessary to
breakeven and earn profits
•   Difficult, but logical
Quantitative Models
•   Computer simulation models using statistical and mathematical tools and techniques
determine budgets
•   Complex, time-consuming, expensive, unreliable
Experimental Methods
•   Test-marketing at different budget levels to determine optimum level
•   Difficult, time-consuming, expensive
