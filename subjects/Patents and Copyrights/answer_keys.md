making, using, or selling the invention without permission.
Governed by: Patents Act, 1970
Duration: 20 years from the date of filing
Examples:
A pharmaceutical company inventing a new drug.

Grant of Patent (India) - A patent is an exclusive legal right granted to an inventor for a
new invention that is novel, useful, and capable of industrial application. In India,
patents are granted under the Patents Act, 1970 by the Controller General of Patents.
Conditions for Grant of Patent - A patent is granted only when the invention satisfies the
following conditions:
Novelty – The invention must be new and not previously known.

Procedure for Grant of Patent
Filing of Application – The inventor files a patent application with complete

Process of Patent Registration in India
In India, patents are granted under the Patents Act, 1970 by the Office of the Controller
General of Patents. The process of patent registration involves the following steps:
1. Filing of Patent Application - The inventor or applicant files a patent application with
the Patent Office. It can be: Provisional application (temporary specification), or
Complete specification (full details of the invention).
2. Publication of Application - The patent application is published in the Official Patent
Journal after 18 months from the date of filing. After publication, the invention becomes
open to public inspection.
3. Request for Examination - The applicant must file a request for examination within
the prescribed time. The Patent Office examines whether the invention satisfies
conditions of patentability such as novelty, inventive step, and industrial application.
4. Examination Report and Objections - The examiner issues a report. If there are
objections, the applicant must respond and make necessary corrections.
5. Opposition (if any) - Any person can file opposition against the grant of patent before
it is granted.

Controller grants the patent and enters it in the Register of Patents. A patent certificate is

examination, opposition, and grant. After registration, the inventor gets exclusive rights
over the invention for 20 years, subject to payment of renewal fees.

Meaning of Infringement of Copyright (India)
Copyright infringement refers to the unauthorized use of a copyrighted work without the
permission of the copyright owner. It occurs when any person violates the exclusive rights
granted to the copyright holder under the Copyright Act, 1957.
Copyright law gives the owner exclusive rights such as reproduction, distribution, adaptation,
communication to the public, and translation of the work. If any person uses the work without
authorization in a manner that violates these rights, it amounts to infringement.
When Use of a Work Amounts to Copyright Infringement A use of a copyrighted work is
considered infringement in the following situations:
1. Unauthorized Reproduction - When a person copies or reproduces a copyrighted work
without permission of the owner. Example: Printing and selling copies of a book without
permission from the author or publisher.
2. Unauthorized Distribution or Sale - Selling, renting, or distributing copyrighted material
without consent. Example: Selling pirated CDs or movies in the market.
3. Communication to the Public - Displaying or broadcasting copyrighted work to the public
without permission. Example: Showing a movie in a public place or uploading it online
without authorization.
4. Unauthorized Adaptation or Translation - Adapting or translating a work without
permission from the copyright owner. Example: Making a movie based on a novel without
taking rights from the author.
5. Use for Commercial Purposes - Using copyrighted work for business or profit without
license. Example: Using copyrighted music in advertisements without permission.
6. Import of Infringing Copies - Importing pirated or unauthorized copies of copyrighted
work into India.
A copyright is infringed when someone, without the permission of the copyright holder, does
any of the above, which only the copyright holder has the exclusive right to do.
The Copyright Act provides for both civil and criminal remedies for

infringements of copyrights. On proving an infringement, the copyright owner is entitled to

The Registrar of Copyrights has the power to prevent the import of infringing copies. On

The owner of Copyright can claim the profit made by the other party through unlawful use of

Compensatory damages - the copyright owner can demand the damages he suffered.

Private or personal use

R.G. Anand v. Delux Films (1978)
Facts of the Case - R.G. Anand, a playwright, wrote a Hindi play titled “Hum
Hindustani”, based on the theme of national integration and communal harmony. He
alleged that the film “New Delhi”, produced by Delux Films, copied the theme and story
of his play without permission. Anand filed a suit for copyright infringement under the
Copyright Act, 1957.
Issue - Whether the film had copied the plaintiff’s play in such a way that it amounted to
copyright infringement.
Page 5 of 9
--- page 6 ---
Judgment - The Supreme Court of India held that there was no copyright infringement.
The Court laid down important principles:
Copyright protects the expression of an idea, not the idea itself.
If the same idea is developed differently, there is no infringement.
To prove infringement, there must be substantial copying of the original work.
The test is whether an average viewer would get an unmistakable impression that the
work is copied.
In this case, although both works had a similar theme (national unity), the story,
treatment, and presentation were different. Hence, no infringement was found.
Legal Principle Established - The case established the “Idea-Expression Dichotomy” in
Indian copyright law — ideas are free for everyone, but the particular expression of an
idea is protected.

Misleading viewers into believing brand endorsement.

Unauthorized commercial use of trademark.

Using brand identity or copyrighted work without permission may harm the reputation of the
owner. Creators and brands have moral rights to protect their work from misuse or distortion.

Digital Rights Management (DRM) refers to technological tools and measures used to
protect digital copyrighted content from unauthorized access, copying, and distribution.
It helps copyright owners control how their digital works are used by others. Under the
Copyright Act, 1957 (India), technological protection measures such as DRM are
recognized for safeguarding digital content like music, movies, e-books, and software.
Purpose of DRM
1. Prevents Unauthorized Copying DRM restricts users from copying or downloading
digital content without permission. Example: Paid music or movies on streaming
platforms cannot be freely downloaded.
2. Controls Access and Usage It allows only authorized users to access the content.
Example: E-books purchased on Kindle can be read only by the buyer’s account.
