2   customer experiences, and enhancing security. IoT devices, from wearable
technology to connected vehicles, are generating unprecedented volumes
of data that financial institutions can leverage for various purposes,
including risk assessment, personalized services, and fraud prevention.
IoT refers to the network of physical devices that communicate with each
other and central systems over the internet. These devices include
wearables (smartwatches, fitness trackers), connected vehicles, smart
homes, and even connected vending machines. The Fintech industry uses
IoT to drive new business models, streamline processes, and enhance
consumer interaction with financial services.
Financial institutions have always dealt with massive amounts of data,
but the arrival of IoT has drastically transformed how this data is used.
Traditional financial systems are built on structured data (e.g., account
balances, transaction records). However, with IoT, they can tap into
unstructured data, such as the behaviours, habits, and preferences of
consumers captured through IoT devices.
In the early stages, the integration of IoT in the fintech industry was
largely limited to back-end automation—such as using connected
devices to track inventory for businesses or automated machinery for
loan evaluations. However, as IoT technology advanced and device data
became more sophisticated, fintech applications expanded to more
customer-facing features.
Applications of IoT in the Fintech Industry
