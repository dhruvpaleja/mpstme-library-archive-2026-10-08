CO 2; B How to classify a channel as flat fading or frequency selective fading.   [5]
SO 1;
BL 1   Explanation 5 Marks
Flat fading-
If the channel has a constant gain and linear phase response over a bandwidth which is greater
than the bandwidth of the transmitted signal, the received signal will undergo flat fading.
Frequency selective fading-
If the channel possesses a constant-gain and linear phase response over a bandwidth that is
smaller than the bandwidth of transmitted signal, then the channel creates frequency selective
fading.

CO 5; D State and explain any five key features of 5G technology.   [5]
SO 1;
BL 1   Features- 5 marks
1. Faster Speeds: 5G offers much faster speeds than 4G, with peak download speeds of up to 20
Gbps and average speeds of 100 Mbps or higher.
2. Lower Latency: 5G has significantly lower latency compared to 4G, with latency as low as 1
millisecond. This allows for near-instantaneous communication between devices, which is critical
for applications like autonomous vehicles and remote surgery.
3. Increased Capacity: 5G has much higher capacity compared to 4G, which means it can
support many more devices and users simultaneously. This is essential for the growing number of
internet-connected devices and the increasing demand for data-intensive applications like video
streaming.

strength and penetration through walls and other obstacles. This enables 5G to support more
devices in rural areas and indoors.

multiple virtual networks on a single physical network. This enables them to offer different levels
of service quality and performance to different types of users and applications.
6. Edge Computing: 5G supports edge computing, which involves processing data closer to the
edge of the network, rather than sending it to a central server. This reduces latency and improves
the performance of applications that require real-time processing, such as virtual reality and
augmented reality.
7. Improved Security: 5G offers improved security features compared to 4G, including enhanced
encryption and stronger authentication protocols. This is critical for protecting sensitive data and
ensuring the security of critical applications.

Role of MRC:
• Maximum Ratio Combining (MRC) is used in SIMO systems to combine signals
from multiple receive antennas to improve signal quality.
•   It assigns weights to each received signal based on the channel gain (amplitude
and phase), giving more importance to stronger signals.
• The signals are phase-aligned and coherently summed using the complex
conjugate of their channel coefficients.
• The output Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) is maximized and equals the sum of
individual branch SNRs.
• MRC provides the best performance among diversity combining techniques by
effectively reducing fading and improving reliability.

User Equipment (UE): This is the device used by the end-user to access the 5G network, such as
a smartphone or tablet.
Radio Access Network (RAN): This is the part of the network that connects the UE to the core
network. It consists of Base Stations (BS) or NodeBs, which are responsible for transmitting and
receiving radio signals to and from the UE.
Data Network (DN): It identifies Service Provider services, Internet access or 3rd party
services.
Core Network (CN): This is the backbone of the 5G network and provides various services such
as call control, session management, and mobility management. It consists of several sub-
components:
Access and Mobility Management Function (AMF): This entity manages the mobility of the
UE, such as handover between different cells or handover between different Radio Access
Technologies (RATs).
Session Management Function (SMF): This entity manages the session between the UE and the
service/application server.
User Plane Function (UPF): This entity is responsible for forwarding the data packets between
the UE and the application server.
Network Slice Selection Function (NSSF): This entity selects the appropriate network slice for
the UE based on its service requirements.
Authentication Server Function (AUSF): This entity is responsible for authenticating the UE
and generating security keys.

• The motivation of CoMP officially described by 3GPP are
–   to improve the coverage of high data rate
–   to improve the cell-edge throughput
–   to increase the system throughput
•   Coordinated multipoint transmission and reception actually refers to a wide range of
techniques that enable dynamic coordination or transmission and reception with multiple
geographically separated eNBs. Its aim is to enhance the overall system performance,
utilise the resources more effectively and improve the end user service quality.
• 4G LTE CoMP, Coordinated Multipoint requires close coordination between a number of
geographically separated eNBs. They dynamically coordinate to provide joint scheduling
and transmissions as well as providing joint processing of the received signals. In this
way a UE at the edge of a cell is able to be served by two or more eNBs to improve
signal’s reception / transmission and increase throughput particularly under cell edge
conditions.
•   In essence, 4G LTE CoMP, Coordinated Multipoint falls into two major categories:
•   Joint processing: Joint processing occurs where there is coordination between multiple
entities - base stations - that are simultaneously transmitting or receiving to or from UEs.
•   Coordinated Scheduling or beam forming: This often referred to as CS/CB
(coordinated scheduling / coordinated beamforming) is a form of coordination where a
UE is transmitting with a single transmission or reception point - base station. However
the communication is made with an exchange of control among several coordinated
entities.
• To achieve either of these modes, highly detailed feedback is required on the channel
properties in a fast manner so that the changes can be made. The other requirement is for
very close coordination between the eNBs to facilitate the combination of data or fast
switching of the cells.
• The techniques used for coordinated multipoint, CoMP are very different for the uplink
and downlink. This results from the fact that the eNBs are in a network, connected to
other eNBs, whereas the handsets or UEs are individual elements.

Requirements:
• To enable M2M communication in LTE, devices must first be provisioned with the
appropriate network credentials and settings.
•   This typically involves the use of a SIM card or other secure authentication mechanism
that enables the device to connect to the LTE network.
• Once connected, M2M devices can communicate with each other using various protocols,
such as MQTT (Message Queuing Telemetry Transport), CoAP (Constrained Application
Protocol) or HTTP (Hypertext Transfer Protocol).
•   These protocols are designed to support lightweight communication and are optimized for
use in low-power, low-bandwidth devices.
M2M and its Architecture
• Machine to Machine (M2M) communication refers to the ability of devices to
communicate with each other without human intervention.
•   In the context of Long-Term Evolution (LTE), M2M communication involves the use of
wireless technology to enable devices to exchange data, commands and other information
in a reliable and efficient manner.
• LTE networks are well-suited for M2M communication because they provide high-speed
data transfer, low latency and high reliability.
• The primary objective of M2M communication is to enable communication among smart
devices.

•   Sensors and communication devices are the endpoints of M2M applications.
•   Devices can connect directly to an operator’s network
•   Devices that connect via gateways are normally outside the operator’s responsibility but

network.
M2M GATEWAY
•   Gateways and routers are the endpoints of the operator’s network
•   There will be a control flow between gateway and operator’s platform
•   Application might lose its relevance since there are implementations of IPv6 for sensor
networks available, that allow an all-IP approach
M2M COMMUNICATION NETWORK
•   It covers the communications between the M2M Gateway(s) and M2M applications
•   E.g. xDSL, LTE, WiMAX, and WLAN
M2M APPLICATION
•   Contains the middleware layer where data goes through various application services and
is used by the specific business-processing engines
•   Applications may either target at end users or at other application providers to offer more
refined building blocks by which they can build more sophisticated M2M solutions and

• A femtocell is a small, low-power cellular base station that is designed for use in
residential or small business environments.
•   Femtocells connect to the existing cellular network through an internet connection.
• They provide wireless coverage for a limited area, typically up to a few hundred square
meters.
• A femtocell is a wireless access point used to enhance indoor cellular connectivity.
•   Unlike other cellular connectivity options, femtocells connect back through the internet to
provide in-home or office cellular connectivity.
•   Femtocells look and operate like routers, and users can place femtocells near their current
network hardware setups.
•   Femtocells are accessible to anyone who wants to purchase one.

Types of Femtocell
Closed Access Femtocell:

*« Aclosed access femtocell is a type of femtocell that is only available to users who are authorized by
the carrier.

+ This type of femtocell is typically used by carriers to provide coverage in areas where the cellular
network is weak or congested, such as in rural areas or in high-density urban environments.

Open Access Femtocell:

+ An open access femtocell is a type of femtocell that is available to any user who has a compatible
cellular device.

+ This type of femtocell is typically used by carriers to provide coverage in areas where the cellular
network is weak or congested, such as in rural areas or in high-density urban environments.

Hybrid Femtocell:

*« Ahybrid femtocell is a type of femtocell that combines the functionality of a femtocell with that of
a Wi-Fi router.

+ This type of femtocell can provide both cellular and Wi-Fi coverage in a single device, and is
typically used in residential environments to provide both voice and data services.

Virtual Femtocell:

+ A virtual femtocell is a type of femtocell that is implemented using software rather than hardware.

+ This type of femtocell is typically used by carriers to provide coverage in areas where the cellular
network is weak or congested, and can be deployed more quickly and easily than traditional
hardware-based femtocells.
