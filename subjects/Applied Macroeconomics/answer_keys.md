Factors Causing Shift in the IS Curve - 4 marks
1. Change in Government Expenditure (G)
Increase in G → Raises aggregate demand → Output increases at every interest rate → IS
shifts rightward.
Decrease in G → Reduces aggregate demand → IS shifts leftward.
This is due to the multiplier effect, which magnifies the initial change in spending.
2. Change in Taxes (T)
Tax cut → Increases disposable income → Boosts consumption → IS shifts rightward.
Tax increase → Reduces consumption → IS shifts leftward.
3. Change in Autonomous Consumption

independent of income → Rightward shift.
If consumer confidence falls → Leftward shift.

adaptive expectations
Under the adaptive expectations framework, the Long-Run Phillips Curve (LRPC) is vertical
because, in the long run, unemployment returns to its natural rate regardless of the rate of
inflation.
According to the expectations-augmented Phillips Curve developed by Milton
Friedman and Edmund Phelps, there is only a short-run trade-off between inflation and
unemployment. In the short run, if policymakers adopt expansionary monetary or fiscal
policy, aggregate demand increases, leading to higher output and lower unemployment.
Initially, workers suffer from money illusion—they mistake higher nominal wages for higher
real wages because expected inflation remains unchanged. Firms respond by increasing
employment, and unemployment falls below its natural rate.
However, under adaptive expectations, workers and firms gradually revise their inflation
expectations based on past inflation. As actual inflation rises, people begin to expect higher
inflation in the future. Workers demand higher nominal wages to restore their real wages,
increasing production costs. The Short-Run Phillips Curve (SRPC) shifts upward as expected
inflation adjusts.
Eventually, unemployment returns to the natural rate (or NAIRU), but at a higher rate of
inflation. Since this process repeats with each attempt to exploit the trade-off, there is no
permanent reduction in unemployment through inflationary policy. Thus, in the long run,
inflation can be higher or lower, but unemployment remains at the natural rate. Graphically,
this implies that the LRPC is vertical at the natural rate of unemployment, showing no long-
run trade-off between inflation and unemployment.

Implication: 2 marks
