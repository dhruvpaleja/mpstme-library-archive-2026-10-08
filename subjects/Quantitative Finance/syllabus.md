Course Outcomes
After completion of the course, students will be able to -
1. Describe the structure and characteristics of financial markets and instruments including
equities, bonds, derivatives, and their relevance in time value of money and portfolio
theory.

derivatives.

techniques such as Value at Risk (VaR), credit scoring (Z-score), and operational risk
frameworks.
4. Evaluate financial instruments using the no-arbitrage principle, risk-neutral pricing, and
mathematical constructs such as martingales, Markov chains, and Brownian motion in
discrete and continuous time frameworks.
5. Construct and test quantitative finance models by identifying assumptions, verifying
model outputs, and generating counter-examples for incorrect financial statements or
invalid pricing strategies.

Course Objective
The aim of the course is to introduce the concepts of statistical treatment of risk-neutral
valuation, arbitrage, options and derivatives. It will provide hands-on implementation of
various risk and pricing models that are used in the industry. The purpose of this course is to
give the students exposure to the practical applications of quantitative finance in the real world.
It will teach the students how to put their theoretical understanding into practice.
Course Outcomes
After completion of the course, students will be able to -
1. Describe the similarities and differences in the main asset classes namely equities, fixed
income securities and commodities,
2. Apply the risk neutral probability measures and the fundamental theorems of asset
pricing in solving problems,
3. Analyze the mathematical statements about financial models and Construct counter-
examples for false statements.
