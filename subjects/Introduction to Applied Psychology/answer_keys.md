A.   5
Clinical Psychology (2.5 marks):
Deals with the study, assessment, diagnosis, and treatment of mental, emotional, and
behavioral disorders using psychotherapy and interventions. Work includes individual, couple,
family, and group therapy in settings like hospitals, clinics, schools, and private practice, often
in collaboration with psychiatrists and social workers.
Counselling Psychology (2.5 marks):
Focuses on personal, social, educational, and career adjustment problems. Provides guidance
and support to promote personal growth, positive mental health, and crisis resolution.
Counselling psychologists work with individuals, families, groups, and communities to foster
well-being.

competitiveness, higher stress level, hate failure, and find it difficult to stop working, even
when they have achieved their goals. Very competitive and self-critical.(1.5Mark)TYPE B-
Tend to exhibit flexibility, low competitiveness, and a relaxed, more laid-back approach to life.

Maslow’s Need Hierarchy Theory states that human needs are arranged in a hierarchy, where
lower-level needs must be satisfied before higher-level needs motivate behavior. The levels are:
Physiological → Safety → Social → Esteem → Self-Actualization.(3 Marks) Example: An
employee needs salary and job security first, then seeks social connections, recognition, and
finally personal growth through creative projects. Unmet lower-level needs can hinder higher-
level motivation. Understanding this helps organizations support employee growth and
productivity.(2 Marks)
2.   Answer the following:(Attempt Any 2 Questions)   10
A.   5
Individual Approaches- An employee can take personal responsibility for reducing stress
levels. Time-management-So an understanding and utilization of basic time-management
principles can help individuals better cope with tensions created by job demands.
Physical exercise-These activities increase lung capacity, lower the resting heart rate, and
provide a mental diversion from work pressures, effectively reducing work-related levels of
stress Relaxation training-Individuals can also teach themselves to reduce tension through
relaxation techniques such as meditation, hypnosis, and deep breathing. Social support
networks-Expanding your social support network provides someone to hear your problems
and offers a more objective perspective on a stressful situation than your own.(1Mark for
listing and 4 marks for explaining all the 4)
B.   5

--- page 3 ---
Job Involvement-Degree to which people identify psychologically with their jobs and
consider their perceived performance levels important to self-worth. For example, a teacher
who feels proud of shaping students’ futures shows high job involvement.(2
Marks)Organizational Commitment-It is the extent to which an employee identifies with a
particular organization and its goals and wishes to remain a member a nurse who stays in the
same hospital for years out of dedication reflects strong commitment.(2Marks) Both have
positive consequence on employee performance and result in lower absenteeism and
turnover(1Mark)
C.   5
Emotions and moods strongly affect workplace processes like negotiation and selection. In
negotiation, pretending to be angry can give one party an advantage, while those who
experience failure often develop negative emotions, leading to poor cooperation in future
interactions. The most effective negotiators remain emotionally detached to ensure fairness and
clarity. (2 Marks)In employee selection, considering emotional intelligence (EI) is crucial for
roles requiring high social interaction, such as sales and recruitment. Teams also benefit from
members with positive moods, as these spread across colleagues and enhance collective
performance. (2 Marks)Analyzing these examples shows how emotions and moods shape both
individual outcomes and overall organizational effectiveness.(1 Mark)
3. Read the case and answer the following:.   10
I. Neha -Age and gender stereotype -A young woman cannot be an assertive leader. Rajesh -
Age stereotype -too old to learn new things. Karan positive halo -one good presentation
results in overlooking average work. Anita negative halo -one bad presentation results in
overlooking other contributions. These errors distort fair assessment and harm objectivity in
evaluations. (5 Marks)
II. Stereotypes, halo, effects lower motivation, reduce trust in management, and create
resentment among employees, weakening team cohesion and productivity. Introduce bias-
awareness training to help managers recognize stereotypes and halo effects. Implement
transparent appraisal systems with measurable criteria to ensure fair recognition of actual
performance. (5 Marks)
