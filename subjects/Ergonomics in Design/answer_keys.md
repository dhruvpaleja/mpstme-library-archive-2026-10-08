a. Expert systems for ergonomic design (05 Marks)
• Computer-based systems that replicate human expertise in ergonomic design.
• Used to optimize workstation layouts, tool designs, and posture analysis.
•   Utilize AI and rule-based reasoning for decision support.
• Enhance safety, comfort, and efficiency in design processes.
• Example: Ergonomic CAD tools suggesting optimal reach zones.
b. Aesthetic expressions in ergonomic design (05 Marks)
• Deals with visual and sensory appeal of products.
• Involves color, shape, texture, and proportion.
• Expresses emotion, comfort, and user satisfaction.
• Contributes to brand identity and acceptance.
• Example: Apple’s minimalist and user-friendly product design.
c. Colour in communication and expression (05 Marks)
• Colours convey messages, moods, and warnings.
• Red = danger; Green = safety; Blue = calmness.
• Helps in organizing information visually.
• Enhances focus and reduces confusion.
• Example: Traffic lights and control panels.
d. PPE applications in different industries (05 Marks)
1. Construction – Helmets, safety shoes.
2. Healthcare – Gloves, face shields.
3. Chemical industry – Goggles, chemical suits.
4. Manufacturing – Ear protection, gloves.

5. Mining – Respirators, protective overalls.
Q2
a. Importance of anthropometric data (10 Marks)
• Ensures design fits human physical characteristics.
• Prevents strain, injury, and discomfort.
• Helps define proper height, reach, and clearance.
• Basis for adjustable furniture, tools, and workstations.
• Example: Adjustable chairs and control panels based on body dimensions.
b. Unity in aesthetic product design (10 Marks)
• Ensures all parts form a harmonious whole.
• Achieved through consistent shapes, materials, and colors.
• Provides visual balance and order.
• Enhances clarity and brand identity.
• Example: iPhone or Tesla products with unified design language.
Q3
a. Applications of ergonomics in automated systems and smart manufacturing (10 Marks)
• Enhances safety between humans and machines.
• Reduces operator fatigue and error.
•   Simplifies control interface and feedback systems.
• Supports efficient workflow and monitoring.
• Example: Collaborative robots with human-friendly interaction zones.
b. Functionality as aesthetics (05 Marks)
• “Form follows function” principle.
• A product’s beauty comes from its usefulness.
• Emphasizes simplicity and efficiency.
• Avoids unnecessary decoration.
• Example: Dyson products—functional yet attractive.
c. Importance of balance and proportion (05 Marks)
• Balance ensures visual and physical stability.
• Proportion creates harmony among elements.
• Both improve comfort and appeal.
• Prevent awkward or bulky designs.
• Example: Symmetrical dashboard design in vehicles.
Q4
a. Effect of colour light on objects (10 Marks)
• Light affects how colours appear on objects.
• Warm light enhances reds/yellows; cool light enhances blues.
• Influences perceived material and mood.
********** 2 **********
--- page 3 ---
• Used in interiors, products, and displays.
• Example: LED lighting in showrooms enhances product finish.
b. Industrial design process & ergonomic contribution (10 Marks)
1. Research: Identify user needs and constraints.
2. Concept: Ensure comfort and function in early sketches.
3. Prototype: Test usability and posture.
4. Evaluation: Gather user feedback for improvement.
5. Production: Maintain ergonomic standards.
• Ergonomics ensures safety, comfort, and performance at each stage.
Q5
a. Colour and user experience (10 Marks)
• Colours affect emotion, focus, and comfort.
• Warm tones energize; cool tones calm users.
• Direct user attention and aid visual hierarchy.
• Influence mood and brand impression.
• Example: Blue and green tones in offices enhance concentration.
b. Man-machine relationship in ergonomic product design (10 Marks)
• Focuses on interaction between user and system.
• Reduces operator fatigue and improves accuracy.
• Enhances productivity through intuitive interfaces.
• Ensures comfort and safety during operation.
• Example: Airplane cockpit designed for optimal control layout.
Q6
a. Shapes and sizes of controls & their ergonomic significance (10 Marks)
• Knobs: precision control for small movements.
• Levers: suitable for large, forceful actions.
• Buttons: quick on/off activation.
• Pedals: hands-free operation.
• Proper size, spacing, and feedback reduce strain and errors.
• Example: Vehicle dashboard with distinct button shapes.
b. Safety considerations in the chemical industry (10 Marks)
• Use of PPE such as gloves, goggles, respirators.
• Adequate ventilation and fume extraction systems.
• Safe handling, labeling, and storage of chemicals.
• Regular safety training and emergency preparedness.
• Continuous monitoring and risk assessments.
Q7
a. Design of major controls in automobiles and machine tools (05 Marks)
********** 3 **********
--- page 4 ---
• Controls must be within reach and easily visible.
•   Differentiated by shape, color, or size.
• Provide tactile/audible feedback.
• Grouped functionally to avoid confusion.
• Example: Steering wheel buttons and gear controls.
b. Ergonomic control design reduces tiredness (05 Marks)
• Minimizes repetitive and awkward movements.
• Reduces required operating force.
• Logical arrangement improves comfort.
• Supports correct posture and hand positions.
• Results in higher productivity and less fatigue.
c. Environmental ergonomics factors (10 Marks)
• Temperature: affects alertness and comfort.
•   Lighting: influences vision and mood.
• Noise: causes stress and hearing issues.
•   Vibration: leads to fatigue and long-term harm.
•   Air quality: essential for health and focus.
