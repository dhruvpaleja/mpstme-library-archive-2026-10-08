CO-1, BL-1   A. Principles include authenticity verification, integrity preservation,   5
reproducibility, chain of custody maintenance, and adherence to
forensic protocols ensuring evidence is not altered.
CO-2, BL-2   B. Video analytics algorithms include motion detection, object   5
tracking, facial recognition, scene change detection, and behavior
analysis. These help identify key frames and events in forensic
video review.
CO-2, BL-3   C. Common methods include Error Level Analysis (ELA), metadata   5
analysis, noise inconsistency detection, edge mismatch detection,
and machine learning-based forgery detection models.
CO-3, BL-4   D. Techniques include analyzing quantization artifacts, codec   5
inconsistencies, frame structure patterns, and statistical deviations
in bitstream to detect multiple compression or transcoding events
Q2   [20]
CO-1, BL-2   A.   Forensic guidelines ensure evidence authenticity, prevent   7
contamination, and maintain legal admissibility. They standardize
procedures for acquisition, enhancement, and analysis of digital
content.
CO-1, BL-3   B. Raster images are pixel-based (JPEG, PNG), while vector images   7
use mathematical paths (SVG, AI). Raster images are common in
evidence and more prone to tampering, making forensic validation
crucial.
CO-2, BL-3   C. Hex editors allow examiners to inspect binary data and file   6
headers directly. Differences in EXIF data or missing signatures
can reveal hidden modifications or file corruption.
Q3   [20]
CO-2, BL-3   A. Artifacts include blocking, blurring, ringing, ghosting, and   7
flickering caused by compression, low bitrate, or motion.
Understanding them aids forensic authenticity checks.

CO-2, BL-4   B.   Lighting, weather, vibrations, and motion can degrade quality and   7
introduce distortions. Awareness helps analysts distinguish natural
issues from tampering.
CO-2, BL-5   C. Process involves bitstream analysis, GOP pattern comparison,   6

Q4   [20]
CO-2, BL-4   A.   Statistical: pixel correlation; Geometric: shape mismatch   10
detection; Frequency domain: DCT/DWT analysis. Combined,
they detect tampering like splicing, cloning, or resampling.
CO-2, BL-5   B.   Pipeline includes data collection, preprocessing, model training   10
(GAN/Autoencoder), face swapping, and post-processing.
Detection uses CNNs, frequency, or behavioral cues.
Q5   [20]
CO-2,3, BL-3   A. Assessment ensures integrity and clarity for investigation using   7
metrics like PSNR, SSIM, and VMAF to identify compression
losses or manipulations.
CO-2,3, BL-5   B. Metadata (EXIF) includes camera details, timestamps, and GPS info.   7
Inconsistencies or absence may suggest manipulation or re-encoding.
CO-2,3, BL-6   C. GANs consist of generator and discriminator networks. The   6
generator synthesizes fake images, while the discriminator
distinguishes real vs fake, improving the model iteratively.
Q6   [20]
CO-1,2,3, BL-3 A. Common impairments: low resolution, lighting variations,   7
compression noise. Enhancement techniques: histogram
equalization, frame interpolation, and de-noising filters.
CO-1,2,3, BL-4 B. Block-based divides image into blocks to find duplicates;   7
keypoint-based uses SIFT/SURF to detect copied regions. Both
reveal cloning in images.
CO-1,2,3, BL-5 C. Techniques: Eye-blink detection, lip-sync mismatch, heartbeat   6
pattern estimation. These reveal unnatural motion in Deepfakes.
Q7   [20]
CO-2,3, BL-3   A. ELA visualizes compression error differences in images.   10
Tampered areas show inconsistent brightness in the ELA map,
helping detect splicing or object removal.
