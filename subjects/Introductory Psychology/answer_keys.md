a.   Briefly explain any two approaches to motivation. (Any 2 )
Instinct Approaches:
1. Instinct approaches to motivation suggest that people and animals are
born preprogrammed with sets of behaviors essential to their survival.   5
2. Those instincts provide the energy that channels behavior in
appropriate directions.
3. Instinct approaches are unable to explain why certain patterns of
behavior and not others have evolved in a given species.
Drive-Reduction Approaches:
1. Drive-reduction approaches to motivation suggest that a lack of some   5
basic biological need produces a drive to push an organism to satisfy
that need.
2. A drive is motivational tension that energizes behavior to fulfill a need,
with primary drives related to biological needs.
3. In secondary drives, prior experience and learning bring about needs
that fulfill no obvious biological requirement.
********** 10 **********
--- page 11 ---
Arousal Approaches:
1. According to arousal approaches to motivation, people try to maintain
a steady level of stimulation and activity.
2. If stimulation and activity levels become uncomfortably high, we try to
reduce them; if they are too low, we will try to increase them by
seeking stimulation.
3. People vary widely in the optimal level of arousal they seek out, with
some looking for especially high levels of arousal.
Incentive Approaches:
1. Incentive approaches to motivation suggest that motivation stems from
the desire to attain external rewards, known as incentives.
2. The desirable properties of external stimuli account for a person’s
motivation, explaining why we may succumb to an incentive even
without internal cues.
3. Internal drives proposed by drive-reduction theory work in tandem
with the external incentives of incentive theory to motivate behaviour.
Cognitive Approaches:
1. Cognitive approaches to motivation suggest that motivation is the
outcome of people’s thoughts, beliefs, expectations, and goals.
2. Cognitive theories of motivation draw a key distinction between
intrinsic and extrinsic motivation, where intrinsic motivation is for
enjoyment and extrinsic motivation is for concrete rewards.
3. We are more apt to persevere and produce higher-quality work when
motivation for a task is intrinsic rather than extrinsic.
