3. BOD & COD

+ BOD: oxygen required by microorganisms to decompose organic matter. Indicates
biodegradable load.

+ COD: oxygen required to oxidize organic matter chemically. Indicates total organic
load (biodegradable + non-biodegradable).

+ Importance:
+ Assess strength of wastewater.
+ Evaluate efficiency of treatment (compare influent vs effluent).

+ COD > BOD > presence of non-biodegradable pollutants.

future demand.

* Typical values: 15-30 years depending on component (intake, treatment plant,
distribution system).

+ Importance:
+ Ensures infrastructure is adequate for population growth.
+ Avoids frequent costly expansions.

+ Balances economy with reliability.

Q3a.

Conventional Water Treatment Processes (Municipal Supply):
1. Screening — removes large floating matter like sticks, leaves, plastics.

2. Aeration — improves oxygen content, removes gases (COz, H2S), oxidizes iron/
manganese.

3. Coagulation & Flocculation — chemicals (alum, ferric salts) added to destabilize
colloids; gentle mixing forms flocs.

4. Sedimentation — flocs settle under gravity, reducing turbidity and suspended
solids.

5. Filtration — rapid sand filters remove fine suspended matter and microorganisms.

6. Disinfection — chlorine or other agents used to kill pathogenic organisms before
distribution.

¢@ Flow Sheet (Sequence)

Raw water > Screening > Aeration > Coagulation/Flocculation > Sedimentation >
Filtration > Disinfection > Clear water reservoir > Distribution system

1. Oxygen sag occurs when wastewater is discharged into a river: dissolved oxygen
(DO) decreases due to microbial decomposition of organic matter.

2. Deoxygenation: microorganisms consume oxygen while oxidizing organic
pollutants, causing DO to fall below saturation.

3. Reaeration: natural absorption of oxygen from the atmosphere replenishes DO,
counteracting deoxygenation.

4. Streeter—Phelps model mathematically describes the balance between
deoxygenation and reaeration, predicting DO levels downstream.

5. Oxygen sag curve: shows initial DO drop, reaching a minimum (critical point), then
gradual recovery as reaeration dominates.

Q7a

Reverse Osmosis (RO): Uses semi-permeable membranes to remove dissolved salts
and impurities; applied in desalination and high-purity water production.

Ultraviolet (UV) disinfection: UV rays damage microbial DNA, preventing
reproduction; effective for pathogen removal without chemicals.

Ozonation: Ozone gas oxidizes organic matter and kills microorganisms; leaves no
residual taste, stronger than chlorine.

Nanotechnology: Nanomaterials (nano-filters, adsorbents) remove heavy metals,
pathogens, and emerging contaminants; highly efficient at trace levels.
