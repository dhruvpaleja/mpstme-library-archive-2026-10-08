a.   1. Chaining:
•   Definition: Chaining is the process of measuring horizontal
distances between points using a chain or tape.
• Purpose: To determine accurate lengths of survey lines.
• Method: Stretch the chain along the line and record
measurements in meters or links.
2. Direct Ranging:
•   Definition: Direct ranging is the process of aligning a straight
survey line between two stations by sighting directly along the
line.
• Method: Place ranging rods at the two end points; an assistant
adjusts intermediate rods so that they are exactly in line with the
two ends.

3. Indirect Ranging:
•   Definition: Indirect ranging is the process of aligning a straight
line when the line of sight is obstructed by obstacles like
buildings, trees, or hills.
• Method: Use offset measurements at accessible points to guide
the line indirectly.
• Use: In difficult or obstructed terrain where direct sighting is

b. Plane Table Surveying:
•   Definition: Plane table surveying is a method of topographic
surveying where the surveyor plots the positions of points
directly on a drawing sheet fixed on a plane table set up in the
field.
• Instrument: Plane table, alidade, drawing sheet, spirit level,
tripod.
Radiation Method:
•   Definition: The radiation method is a technique of plane table
surveying in which lines are drawn from a single station (the
plane table station) to all accessible points in the area.
• Procedure:
1. Set up the plane table over a known station.
2. Orient the table using a back sight or compass.
3. Sight each point to be plotted with the alidade.
4. Draw a line from the station to the point and measure its
distance (or scale it).
5. Repeat for all points visible from the station.
6. Move the table to another station if required and repeat.
Advantages:
• Quick and simple for small areas.   [10]
•   Direct plotting reduces errors.
• Useful in open terrain.
