Sustainability Reporting Frameworks
Sustainability reporting frameworks are standardized systems used to
report non-financial performance indicators, including
environmental impact, social responsibility, and ethical governance.
Some widely used frameworks include:
1. Global Reporting Initiative (GRI)
One of the most widely adopted frameworks that provides
guidelines for reporting sustainability performance in areas
such as environment, labor practices, and human rights.
2. Sustainability Accounting Standards Board (SASB)
Provides industry-specific sustainability standards that help
investors understand financially material sustainability risks.
3. Integrated Reporting Framework (IR)
Combines financial and non-financial information to provide
a comprehensive overview of an organization’s value
creation.
4. Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures
(TCFD)
Focuses on reporting climate-related financial risks and
opportunities.
Importance of Global Sustainability Frameworks
Global sustainability frameworks play a crucial role in sustainable
development because they:
• Promote transparency and accountability in corporate
activities
• Encourage responsible environmental practices
• Improve stakeholder trust and investor confidence
• Help organizations align with international sustainability
goals

Synoptic Answers
Environmental LCA is a systematic analysis of the environmental
impacts of a product or service throughout its life cycle.
It helps companies quantify and understand the environmental
burdens associated with their products or processes, such as
greenhouse gas emissions, resource depletion, and pollution.
Key Steps in Environmental LCA
• Goal and scope definition: Defining the purpose of the LCA
and setting the boundaries of the assessment.
• Life cycle inventory: Gathering data on the energy and
material inputs, as well as the environmental releases,
associated with each stage of the product life cycle.
• Life cycle impact assessment: Evaluating the potential
environmental impacts based on the inventory data.   [10 marks]
•   Interpretation: Drawing conclusions and making
recommendations based on the LCA results.
Benefits of Environmental LCA
• Identifying hotspots: Pinpointing areas of the life cycle with
the highest environmental impacts, allowing for targeted
improvements.
• Decision support: Providing data-driven insights to guide
product design, process optimization, and supply chain
management.
• Transparency   and   communication:   Communicating
environmental performance to stakeholders and consumers.

management, and efforts to reduce carbon footprint. It emphasizes
sustainable practices that protect and preserve the environment.

company's financial performance and economic impact. It includes

Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) is a policy approach that
holds producers accountable for the entire lifecycle of their
products, especially the post-consumer stage. This concept aims to
promote sustainable product design and reduce the environmental
impact of products by shifting the responsibility of waste
management from local governments and consumers to the
producers themselves.
Producer Responsibility: Producers are required to manage the
disposal and recycling of their products once they reach the end of
their useful life. This includes financial and operational
responsibilities. EPR policies incentivize producers to design
products that are easier to recycle, use fewer resources, and have a
longer lifespan.
