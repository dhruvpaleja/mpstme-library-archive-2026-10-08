Examine why two level systems fail to produce lasers.   [4]
3,4
********** 6 **********
--- page 7 ---
Ans. It is not possible to achieve population inversion with a 2-state
(level) system. If the incoming radiation flux is made very large the
probability of stimulated emission and absorption can be made to far
exceed the rate of spontaneous emission. But in 2-state system, the best
we can get is N1 = N2.
Suppose there are only 2 levels, a metastable state, corresponding to energy
hν, above the ground state. The more photons with frequency ν we pump
into the assembly of atoms, the more upward transitions there will be from
the ground state to the metastable state. However, pumping will also
stimulate downward transitions from metastable state to the ground state.
i.e. population and depopulation processes take place simultaneously.
In other words, the incoming flood of photons is just as likely to cause an
atom to drop (stimulated emission) as it is to cause an atom to rise
(absorption). When half the atoms are in each state, the rate of stimulated
emissions will be equal the rate of stimulated absorption, so the assembly
can’t have more than half its atoms in metastable state. In this situation laser
amplification cannot occur, the required criteria for lasing is N2 > N1.

Because of the development of the ‘+’ve space charge region near the n-side
of the junction and ‘-’ve space charge region near the p-side, an electric field

n to p, opposing diffusion current. The electric field E builds up to the point
where net current is zero at equilibrium.

to the relation E= - dV/dx. In the neutral region outside W, a constant
potential is maintained. The potential difference, V0, between the two ends
of the barrier width is known as the contact or junction potential or the built-
in potential barrier, in that it is necessary to the maintenance of the
equilibrium at junction and any electron or hole must surmount this potential
to disturb the equilibrium.

a.   What is an optical fiber? Discuss different types of loss mechanisms that affect
the signal degradation in the optical fibers.
Ans. Definition of the optical fiber.   2 marks
Losses in optical fiber
Losses in optical fiber result from attenuation in the material itself and from
scattering, which causes some light to strike the cladding at less than the critical
angle.
Bending the optical fiber too sharply can also cause losses by causing some of
the light to meet the cladding at less than the critical angle.
Other major types of losses are
Materials loss
• Due to impurities: The material loss is due to the impurities (e.g. Fe, Ni, Co)
present in glass used for making fibers. The following fig. shows attenuation
due to various molecules inside glass as a function of wavelength. It can be
noted that the material loss due to impurities reduces substantially beyond about
1200 nm wavelength.
CO-2; BL-
• Due to OH molecule: In addition, the OH molecule diffuses in the material [10]
1,2
and causes absorption of light. The OH molecule has main absorption peak
somewhere in the deep infra-red wavelength region. However, it shows
substantial loss in the range of 1000 to 2000 nm.
• Due to infra-red absorption: Glass intrinsically is a good infra-red absorber.
As we increase the wavelength the infra-red loss increases rapidly.
• The scattering loss results from the non-uniformity of the refractive index
inside the core of the fiber. The refractive index of an optical fiber has
fluctuation over spatial scales much smaller than the optical wavelength. These
fluctuations act as scattering centers for the light passing through the fiber. The
process is known as Rayleigh scattering. A very tiny fraction of light gets
scattered and therefore contributes to the loss.
• The Rayleigh scattering is a very strong function of the wavelength. The
scattering loss varies as λ-4. This loss therefore rapidly reduces as the

Ans. The formation of color in soap bubble is due to the interference of light
from an extended white light. The films are usually observed by reflected light.
The eyes looking at the thin films receive light waves reflected from top and
bottom surfaces of the thin soap film. The reflected rays are very close to each
CO-2; BL-
other and are in a position to interfere. We know that the condition for   [4]
3,4

2μt cos r = (2n±1)(λ/2)   n = 0, 1,
2, 3, ….

wavelength (λ) and the angle of refraction (r), which is related to the angle of
the incident light on the film.
********** 9 **********
--- page 10 ---
White light consists of several (seven) wave lengths and for specific values of
t and r, waves of only certain wavelength interfere to satisfy the above condition
of constructive interference. Therefore, only those colors are present in the
reflected light. Different colors will satisfy the condition at different angles.
The other wave lengths interfere destructively and hence are absent in the
reflected light. Hence, the film at particular point appears colored.
