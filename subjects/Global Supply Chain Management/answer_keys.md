Supply chain resilience refers to the ability of a supply chain to prepare
for, respond to, and recover from disruptions while maintaining
continuity of operations. Organizations adopt various strategies to
strengthen resilience.
Supplier Diversification: Relying on a single supplier increases risk.

Inventory Buffering (Safety Stock): Maintaining extra inventory helps
absorb shocks caused by delays or sudden demand spikes.
Nearshoring and Regionalization: Shifting production closer to key
markets reduces dependency on long, complex global supply chains.
Digitalization and Real-Time Visibility: Using technologies like IoT, AI,
and blockchain improves visibility across the supply chain, enabling faster
decision-making during disruptions.
Flexible Transportation and Logistics Networks: Developing multiple
transportation options (air, sea, rail, road) ensures continuity when one
mode is disrupted.

CO-1;   occur almost daily. Discuss.   [10]
BL-2   Ans:
********** 6 **********
--- page 7 ---
In global supply chain networks, risk is no longer a rare or exceptional
event—it has become an everyday operational concern. This is because
supply chains today are highly interconnected, geographically dispersed,
and dependent on multiple external factors. As a result, even small
disruptions in transport, resources, or institutional systems can quickly
affect operations.
Frequent transport disruptions, resource availability issues, institutional
and regulatory barriers, market dynamics and demand uncertainty,
Frequency and complexity of disruptions make risk a day-to-day
operational issue. Companies must embed risk awareness into routine
decision-making, ensuring agility and responsiveness. Those that treat risk
proactively as part of operations—not just as an occasional threat—are
better positioned to maintain continuity and competitiveness in a dynamic
global environment.
