Q.4.   [10]
Big Data Analytics in supply chain focuses on analyzing very large,
diverse, and historical datasets from sources like ERP logs, RFID
scans, customer demand patterns, supplier performance databases,
and social media. It helps identify long-term trends, optimize
network design, improve forecasting accuracy, and support strategic
planning.
Real-Time Analytics uses live data streams from IoT sensors, GPS-
tracked vehicles, warehouse scanners, and order systems to make
immediate operational decisions. It enables instant visibility to
shipment status, real-time inventory updates, and alerts on
disruptions or delays.
Difference:
• Big data analytics deals with high-volume, high-variety data
for long-term insights and strategic decisions.
• Real-time analytics deals with fast-moving live data for
immediate responses and short-term operational execution.
Example – Big Data:
Using historical sales, weather, festival trends, and consumer
behavior to forecast demand and plan safety stock.
Example – Real-Time Analytics:
Monitoring GPS and sensor data to reroute a delivery truck due to
real-time traffic congestion or warehouse equipment failure alerts.
Why both are needed:
Big data provides long-term planning accuracy and structural
improvements, while real-time analytics enables agility and rapid
response. Together, they enhance supply chain resilience, service
levels, and cost efficiency.
