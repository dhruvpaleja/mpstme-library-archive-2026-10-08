How do managers identify problems? In the real world, most
problems don’t come with neon signs flashing “problem.” When
her reps started complaining about their computers, it was pretty
clear to Amanda that something needed to be done, but few
problems are that obvious.
Managers also have to be cautious not to confuse problems with
symptoms of the problem. Is a 5% drop in sales a problem?

as poor-quality products, high prices, or bad advertising?

Also, keep in mind that problem identification is subjective. What
one manager considers a problem might not be considered a
problem by another manager.

likely to perform just as poorly as the manager who doesn’t even
recognize a problem and does nothing. As you can see, effectively

3a.   A business cycle is an interval of general expansion followed by recession (sometimes even depression)
followed by recovery and again reaching a certain peak, followed again by the same cycle. It is
characterized by the rise and fall of economic activity, which affects the production of goods and
services. Business Cycles are a fundamental feature of market economies, and they have important
implications for the economy, government, and private sector. Determine key criteria that cause a
business cycle to take place in an economy and how the concept is related to Macro Economics. Justify
with the aid suitable examples. (10 Marks) (BL-Evaluating)
Students are expected to use the references below to evaluate the issue & relate it to appropriate
concepts & provide examples in support.
Factors such as GDP, interest rates, total employment, and consumer spending can help determine
the current economic cycle stage.
• Expansion: The economy is growing, with increased production, lower unemployment, and higher
consumer spending. The peak of this phase is known as the boom.
• Contraction: Growth slows, unemployment rises, and economic output falls.
• Trough: The lowest point of contraction, after which the cycle begins again with an expansion
phase.
