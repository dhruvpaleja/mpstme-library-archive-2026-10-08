(b) Analyse the essentials of a valid pledge and Suresh’s rights [5 Marks]
Essentials of a valid pledge applied to this scenario:
1. Delivery of Goods: Ramesh physically delivered the gold necklace to Suresh.
2. Delivery Must Be as Security: The necklace was delivered solely to secure the loan, not as a gift
or for safe custody.
3. Existence of Debt or Promise: A present debt of ₹1,00,000 exists, partially repaid.
4. Movable Goods Only: The necklace is movable, satisfying the requirement.
5. Ownership: Ramesh, the owner, delivered the necklace. Even if ownership were partial, the pledge
would still be valid.
6. Return of Goods on Fulfilment: Once the debt is repaid in full, Suresh must return the necklace.
Legal Analysis:
•   Suresh, as pledgee, has the right to retain the gold necklace until the debt is fully repaid.
• Since Ramesh has repaid only ₹60,000 out of ₹1,00,000, Suresh is legally justified in retaining the
necklace until the remaining ₹40,000 is paid.
• However, Suresh cannot misuse or sell the necklace unless legally empowered or unless the debt
remains unpaid and contractual remedies allow.
Conclusion:
All essentials of a valid pledge are satisfied. Suresh is legally entitled to retain the gold necklace until
Ramesh repays the remaining ₹40,000. His retention is justified under Section 172 of the Indian Contract
Act, 1872.

1. Introduction
The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 is a modern legislation enacted to protect consumers from
exploitation in the digital and traditional marketplace. It provides legal remedies against defective goods,
deficient services, misleading product representations, and unfair conduct by sellers and e-commerce
platforms. The Act is designed to ensure transparency, accountability, and speedy redressal of consumer
grievances.
2.Meaning / Legal Framework
A consumer under the Act is any person who buys goods or avails services for consideration. The Act
ensures that consumers receive products and services as promised and have the right to seek redressal if
there is any defect, deficiency, or failure in performance. In the present case, Riya has purchased a
smartphone based on certain assurances made in the advertisement, but the product and after-sales service
do not match the promised terms.
3. Legal Analysis
(a) Consumer Rights and Legal Issues (5 Marks)
1. Right to Information
Riya is entitled to receive correct and complete information regarding the product. The claim of “1-year
full replacement guarantee” forms part of the contractual assurance. If this information is false or not
honoured, it violates her right to be properly informed before purchase.
2. Right to Redressal
The Act gives her the right to seek remedy such as refund, replacement, or compensation when goods are
defective or services are not as promised. Since the smartphone is malfunctioning and warranty conditions
are not honoured, she can approach the Consumer Commission for relief.
3. Deficiency in Goods and Services
There is a clear deficiency in service as:
• The product is not functioning properly (defective goods)
• Warranty terms are not fulfilled as promised
•   After-sales service (customer support) fails to provide proper resolution
• This amounts to failure in delivering promised quality and service standards.
4. Liability of Seller and E-commerce Platform
Both the seller and the online platform may be held responsible because:
• The seller provided defective goods
• The platform facilitated the transaction and displayed misleading product assurance
• E-commerce platforms are required to ensure grievance redressal mechanisms
(b) Objectives of the Act and Application (5 Marks)
1. Protection of Consumer Interests
The primary objective of the Act is to protect consumers from exploitation in all forms. In this case, Riya’s
financial and legal interests are protected against defective product delivery and non-fulfilment of
promised service.

The Act strengthens rights such as:

b. Offer (Proposal):
o Defined under Section 2(a) of the Indian Contract Act, 1872.
o   It is an expression of willingness to enter into a contract on specific terms, intending to
get acceptance.
o   If accepted, it results in a binding contract.
o Example: A offers to sell his car to B for ₹2,00,000.
2. Invitation to Offer:
o   It is merely an invitation to others to make an offer.
o   It shows readiness to negotiate or receive offers, but does not intend to create legal
obligations at this stage.
o Acceptance of an invitation does not result in a contract.
o Example: Display of goods with a price tag in a shop window, auction advertisements.
3. Counter Offer:
o   It is a response to an offer, but with different terms or conditions.
o A counter offer rejects the original offer and puts forward a new offer in its place.
o The original offer lapses and cannot be accepted unless renewed.
o Example: A offers to sell his car for ₹2,00,000. B replies, "I’ll buy it for ₹1,80,000"
(counter offer).

• The Commission consists of one Chairperson and six Members as per the Competition Act who
shall be appointed by the Central Government.
• The commission is a quasi-judicial body which gives opinions to statutory authorities and also
deals with other cases. The Chairperson and other Members shall be whole-time Members
• Role of CCI – (5 marks)
• To eliminate practices having adverse effect on competition, promote and sustain
competition, protect the interests of consumers and ensure freedom of trade in the markets
of India.
• To give opinion on competition issues on a reference received from a statutory authority
established under any law and to undertake competition advocacy, create public awareness
and impart training on competition issues.
• The Competition Commission of India takes the following measures to achieve
its objectives:
• Consumer welfare: To make the markets work for the benefit and welfare of consumers.
• Ensure fair and healthy competition in economic activities in the country for faster and
inclusive growth and development of the economy.
• Implement competition policies with an aim to effectuate the most efficient utilization of
economic resources.
• Develop and nurture effective relations and interactions with sectoral regulators to ensure
smooth alignment of sectoral regulatory laws in tandem with the competition law.
•   Effectively carry out competition advocacy and spread the information on benefits of
competition among all stakeholders to establish and nurture competition culture in Indian
economy.
