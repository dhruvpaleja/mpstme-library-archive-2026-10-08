1. Transportation networks determine the speed, reliability, and cost of moving goods within
the supply chain.
2. Efficient networks reduce fuel and labor costs, leading to overall supply chain cost
reductions.
3. They enhance delivery speed, which improves customer satisfaction and retention,

4. Network optimization, like route planning, minimizes travel distance, thus reducing

Answer:
1. Share real-time demand data with vendors for accurate stock replenishment.
2. Establish predefined stock levels to avoid overstocking or understocking.
3. Implement collaborative planning tools for forecasting and order scheduling.
4. Use automated systems for inventory tracking and reporting.

4. Enables flexibility through multimodal transport options, enhancing efficiency.
5. Drives innovation in supply chains through technologies like GPS and IoT in fleet
management.

1. The scope of Logistics and Supply Chain Management (SCM) is broad, encompassing the   10
coordination and optimization of all activities involved in the movement and storage of goods
from the point of origin to the end consumer.
Page 2 of 6
--- page 3 ---
-SCM involves multiple key components, such as procurement, transportation, warehousing,
and distribution, all essential for meeting customer demands efficiently and cost-effectively.
-Enhance operational efficiency, reduce costs, and improve customer satisfaction..
SCM impacts other areas of management in various ways:
1. Marketing.
2. Finance
3. Human Resources
4. Operations
5. Information Technology
2. Compare supply chain performance metrics and their impact on achieving goals   10
1. Cost-related metrics (e.g., cost per order) evaluate financial efficiency.
2. Service metrics (e.g., on-time delivery) impact customer satisfaction.
3. Inventory metrics (e.g., turnover ratio) influence stock availability and capital efficiency.
4. Flexibility metrics (e.g., lead time) measure responsiveness to market changes.
5. Sustainability metrics (e.g., carbon footprint) reflect environmental impact.
3. Key factors include cost-effectiveness, delivery speed, geographic coverage, and   10
environmental impact. An optimized transportation network considers multimodal transport
options, minimizes bottlenecks, and integrates real-time tracking technologies. The goal is to
balance cost and service quality while reducing emissions and maintaining customer
satisfaction.
Students can explain any 2 designs diagrammatically with examples.
4. Inventory management strategies are crucial in balancing stock availability with holding   10
costs, directly affecting a supply chain’s efficiency and customer satisfaction. Two commonly
used methods, Just-in-Time (JIT) and Economic Order Quantity (EOQ), each offer unique
benefits and challenges for supply chain management.
1. Just-in-Time (JIT):
Page 3 of 6
--- page 4 ---
Advantages:
Reduced Inventory Costs: JIT reduces the need for large inventory holdings,
decreasing storage and handling costs.
Increased Flexibility: By producing and receiving goods only as needed,
companies can quickly respond to changing customer demands.
Lower Waste and Obsolescence: JIT minimizes excess stock, reducing the risk of
inventory becoming obsolete.
Limitations:
Dependency on Supplier Reliability: JIT requires a strong, reliable supplier
network as any delay can lead to stockouts and interrupted production.
Increased Ordering Frequency: Frequent reordering may increase transportation
costs and administrative workload.
Vulnerability to Disruptions: JIT systems are vulnerable to supply chain
disruptions, which can halt production if inventory is not available when needed.
2. Economic Order Quantity (EOQ):
Advantages:
Cost Minimization: EOQ aims to balance ordering and holding costs, resulting in
optimal order quantities that minimize overall inventory costs.
Consistent Stock Levels: EOQ helps maintain a steady inventory level, reducing
the likelihood of stockouts.
Predictable Reordering: With EOQ, reordering is systematic and based on
calculated intervals, which simplifies planning.
Limitations:
Assumptions on Demand Stability: EOQ is less effective in handling fluctuating
demand, as it relies on stable demand and lead times.
Inflexibility: EOQ does not easily adapt to changes in demand or supply chain
dynamics, potentially leading to overstocking or understocking.
Page 4 of 6
--- page 5 ---
Higher Holding Costs: EOQ typically requires more inventory on hand compared
to JIT, leading to increased holding costs.
5. Safety inventory, or safety stock, is essential in managing demand variability and supply   10
chain uncertainties, ensuring that companies can maintain service levels even during demand
surges or supply delays.
1. Importance of Safety Inventory in Uncertainty Management:
Mitigating Demand Variability: Safety stock acts as a buffer against unpredictable
demand, preventing stockouts and helping companies meet customer expectations.
Supply Chain Disruptions: In cases of supplier delays or transportation issues, safety
inventory provides a cushion to maintain consistent operations, reducing the risk of lost
sales and maintaining customer satisfaction.
2. Key Factors Influencing Safety Stock Levels:
Demand Variability: Higher demand volatility requires more safety stock to cover
unexpected spikes in orders.
Lead Time Uncertainty: If lead times are unpredictable, more safety inventory is
necessary to account for delays.
Service Level Targets: The desired service level, or the probability of not facing a
stockout, directly impacts safety stock requirements. Higher service levels require more
safety inventory.
Replenishment Frequency: Less frequent replenishments require more safety stock as
the time between deliveries increases the risk of running out of stock.
3. Impact on Service Levels and Costs:
Service Levels: Adequate safety stock enhances service levels, reducing stockouts and
improving customer satisfaction. However, excess safety stock does not necessarily
lead to proportional improvements in service levels.
Inventory Holding Costs: Safety stock increases holding costs, as more inventory is stored
to cover uncertainty. Companies must analyze the trade-off between holding costs and the
benefit of higher service levels to determine the optimal safety stock
