(A) Price Rigidity under the Kinked Demand Curve Model (4 Marks)
Page 4 of 8
--- page 5 ---
In an oligopolistic market, firms are interdependent and expect asymmetric reactions from
rivals when they change prices. The kinked demand curve model explains price rigidity
through the following behavioural assumptions:
If a firm raises its price above the prevailing market price, rival firms are unlikely to follow
the increase, causing a significant loss of market share. Therefore, the demand curve above
the current price is relatively elastic.
If a firm reduces its price, rival firms are expected to match the price cut to protect their
market share. Hence, the demand curve below the prevailing price is relatively inelastic.
This creates a “kink” in the demand curve at the prevailing price. The corresponding marginal
revenue (MR) curve becomes discontinuous, exhibiting a vertical gap below the kink. As long
as the marginal cost (MC) curve fluctuates within this discontinuous segment of MR, the
equilibrium price and output remain unchanged.
Thus, even if costs change moderately, firms do not alter prices, resulting in price rigidity in
oligopolistic markets.
Diagram: Kinked demand curve with more elastic segment above the prevailing price and less
elastic segment below; discontinuous MR curve with vertical gap; MC intersecting MR
within the gap.) (2 Marks)

5.   1. Pricing Strategy (4 Marks)   12
Arvion Biocare is practicing international price discrimination (dumping), a form of
third-degree price discrimination across national markets.
Reasons:
Same drug sold at ₹200 in India and ₹120 in African markets.
Markets are geographically separated.
Domestic demand is relatively inelastic; African demand is highly elastic.
Price differences are based on elasticity and competitive conditions.
Thus, the firm charges a higher price in the less elastic domestic market and a lower price in
the more elastic foreign market.
2. Factors Justifying and Enabling the Pricing Decision (4 Marks)
The firm can adopt this strategy because:
Different elasticities: African markets are more price-sensitive; domestic market is less
elastic.
Market separation: No resale/arbitrage between African and Indian markets.
Brand power domestically: Strong recognition and limited substitutes.
Cost recovery motive: Higher domestic prices help recover R&D expenses.
Excess capacity utilisation: Lower export price helps increase total output.
These conditions make third-degree discrimination feasible.

Short-run effects:
Consumers benefit from lower prices.
