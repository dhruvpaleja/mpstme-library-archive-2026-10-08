Q1(a)   Edge Computing is a distributed computing paradigm in which data processing and   5
computation are performed close to the data source (such as IoT devices, sensors, or local
servers) instead of relying entirely on centralized cloud data centers. This approach
minimizes the distance that data must travel, thereby reducing latency and improving
response time. Overview: Edge computing supports real-time applications, reduces
bandwidth usage by filtering data locally, enhances data privacy by keeping sensitive data
near the source, and improves reliability as it can function even with limited internet
connectivity. It is widely used in IoT, healthcare, autonomous vehicles, and smart cities.
Q1(b)   Edge AI refers to the deployment of artificial intelligence models directly on edge devices.   5
Integration with Machine Learning and AI involves training models using large datasets in
centralized cloud environments and then deploying these trained models to edge devices for
inference. This integration enables real-time decision-making without constant cloud
communication. It reduces latency, improves efficiency, ensures data privacy, and allows
offline operation. For example, a smart camera uses AI models to detect objects locally
without sending video data to the cloud.
Q1(c)   Multi-Access Edge Computing (MEC) is a network architecture that extends cloud   5
computing capabilities to the edge of mobile networks, closer to end users. It is typically
deployed within telecom infrastructure such as base stations. Objectives of MEC include:
(1) achieving ultra-low latency for real-time applications, (2) improving bandwidth
efficiency by local processing, (3) enabling context-aware services, (4) enhancing Quality
of Service (QoS) and Quality of Experience (QoE), and (5) supporting emerging
technologies like 5G, IoT, and augmented reality.

Q1(d)   Video processing in Edge AI involves capturing, analyzing, and interpreting video data   5
directly on edge devices such as smart cameras or local servers. AI algorithms like object
detection, facial recognition, and motion tracking are applied locally. This allows real-time
insights without sending large video data to the cloud. Benefits include reduced latency,
lower bandwidth consumption, enhanced privacy, and faster decision-making. Applications
include surveillance systems, traffic monitoring, retail analytics, and industrial safety
systems.
Q2(a)   Edge Computing significantly improves system performance in three key ways. Latency   10
Reduction: By processing data closer to the source, the time required to send data to distant
cloud servers is eliminated, enabling real-time responses. Bandwidth Optimization: Only
relevant or processed data is sent to the cloud, reducing network congestion and saving
bandwidth. Data Privacy: Sensitive data is processed locally on edge devices, minimizing
exposure during transmission and enhancing security. This is especially important in
healthcare and financial systems.
Q2(b)   Log analysis is the process of collecting, examining, and interpreting log data generated by   10
systems and applications. It is crucial for monitoring system performance, identifying
errors, and troubleshooting issues. By analyzing logs, administrators can detect anomalies,
security breaches, and performance bottlenecks. It also helps in root cause analysis,
ensuring system reliability, and improving overall system efficiency. In edge environments,
log analysis enables proactive maintenance and quick issue resolution.
Q3(a)   On-device processing refers to performing computations directly on edge devices such as   10
smartphones, IoT devices, or embedded systems instead of relying on cloud servers. This
approach reduces latency, enables real-time decision-making, and allows devices to
function even without internet connectivity. It also enhances data privacy since sensitive
data remains on the device. Examples include voice assistants, facial recognition on
smartphones, and industrial sensors performing local analytics.
Q3(b) RAN (Radio Access Network) is a part of a telecommunications system that connects user   10
devices to the core network through base stations. The MEC platform is integrated within
the RAN to provide computing and storage resources at the network edge. This
combination enables ultra-low latency services, real-time data processing, and efficient
network utilization. It is essential for applications like autonomous vehicles, AR/VR, and
smart city solutions.

Q4(a)   Integration of MEC with 4G LTE and 5G networks allows edge computing capabilities to   10
be embedded within telecom infrastructure. In 4G LTE, MEC enhances service delivery by
reducing latency and improving bandwidth efficiency. In 5G, MEC plays a critical role by
supporting ultra-reliable low-latency communication (URLLC), network slicing, and
massive IoT deployments. This integration enables advanced applications such as smart
healthcare, autonomous driving, and immersive media experiences.
Q4(b)   Real-time video analytics refers to the immediate processing and analysis of video streams   10
as they are captured. Its importance lies in enabling instant decision-making and rapid
response. For example, in traffic management, cameras analyze live video feeds to detect
congestion or accidents and adjust traffic signals accordingly. This improves road safety,
reduces congestion, and enhances efficiency. It is also widely used in surveillance, retail,
and industrial monitoring.
Q5(a)   Activity detection in Edge Computing involves identifying and recognizing human or   10
object actions from video or sensor data using AI models deployed on edge devices.
Techniques such as motion detection, pattern recognition, and deep learning are used to
classify activities like walking, running, or suspicious behavior. Performing this at the edge
ensures real-time response, reduced latency, and enhanced privacy. Applications include
surveillance, healthcare monitoring, and smart homes.
Q5(b)   Case Studies: In Video Surveillance, edge AI is used to detect intrusions, recognize faces,   10
and monitor activities in real time without relying on cloud processing, improving security
and response time. In Smart Cities, edge AI is used for traffic management, waste
management, energy optimization, and public safety. For example, smart traffic systems
analyze vehicle flow and adjust signals dynamically, reducing congestion and improving
urban mobility.
Q6(a)   Edge Computing offers several advantages: Speed: Data is processed locally, resulting in   10
faster response times.
Reliability: Systems can operate even with limited or no internet connectivity. Security:
Sensitive data is processed locally, reducing exposure to cyber threats.
Scalability: Distributed architecture allows easy addition of new devices without
overloading central systems.
These advantages make edge computing suitable for real-time and mission-critical
applications.
Q6(b) MEC use cases include: Smart Cities enabling intelligent traffic systems, smart lighting,   10
and public safety solutions;
Industrial IoT supporting automation, predictive maintenance, and real-time monitoring in
manufacturing;
Telecommunications improving network performance, enabling low-latency services, and
supporting applications like AR/VR and gaming.
MEC enhances efficiency and user experience across these domains.

Q7(a)   Anomaly detection in Edge AI involves identifying unusual patterns or behaviors in data   10
that deviate from normal conditions using AI and machine learning models.
These anomalies may indicate faults, security threats, or system failures.
Real-time alerts notify administrators immediately when anomalies are detected, enabling
quick action.
Incident response involves analyzing the issue, mitigating its impact, and restoring normal
operation.
Together, they ensure system reliability, security, and continuous monitoring.
Q7(b)   Edge AI in Traffic Management uses sensors and cameras to collect real-time data on   10
vehicle movement and road conditions.
AI models analyze this data locally to control traffic signals, detect congestion, identify
accidents, and optimize traffic flow.
This reduces travel time, improves road safety, and enhances urban mobility. It is a key
