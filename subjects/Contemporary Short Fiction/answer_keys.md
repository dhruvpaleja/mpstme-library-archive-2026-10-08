universal.

loop.

********** 2 **********
--- page 3 ---
•   First-person: Offers direct access to the narrator’s
emotions, thoughts, and biases — creates intimacy
and subjectivity.
• Third-person: Allows a broader, more objective view
of multiple characters, offering psychological depth
or distance depending on omniscience.
2. Reliability and Bias:
•   First-person: Can be unreliable; readers must
interpret truth through the narrator’s limited
perception (e.g., Strawberry Spring).
• Third-person: Often perceived as more trustworthy or
balanced, though limited-omniscient narrators can
also shape interpretation subtly.
3. Emotional Engagement:
•   First-person: Builds strong empathy; readers feel
events through the narrator.
• Third-person: Creates reflective distance; readers
observe rather than directly experience.
4. Scope and Perspective:
•   First-person: Restricts the narrative world to what the
narrator sees or knows — ideal for internal conflict or
psychological depth.
• Third-person: Expands the scope — useful for
complex plots, multiple perspectives, or social
commentary.
5. Reader’s Role:
•   First-person: Reader becomes an interpreter, piecing
together truth behind the narrator’s words.
• Third-person: Reader becomes an observer, guided by
the narrator’s chosen focus and tone.
6. Overall Impact:
•   First-person: Subjective, immersive, confessional —
blurs truth and emotion.
• Third-person: Broader, controlled, interpretive —
clarifies patterns, motives, and themes.
