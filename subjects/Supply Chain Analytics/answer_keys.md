ion of RFID:

RFID (Radio Frequency Identification) is a technology that uses radio waves to automatically identify and
track objects, products, or assets.

Each item is fitted with a small electronic device called an RFID tag, which contains a microchip and an
antenna. The tag transmits data (like product ID, location, or batch number) to an RFID reader, which sends

the information to a computer system for processing.

Uses of RFID in Supply Chain Management:

RFID plays a vital role in modern supply chain operations by providing real-time vi

ity and automation

across various stages—procurement, warehousing, transportation, and retail.

1. Improved Inventory Tracking

+ RFID enables real-time tracking of products as they move through the supply chain.

* Each item can be scanned automatically without direct line-of-sight (unlike barcodes).

* This allows companies to know exactly what stock, their quantity, and location at any

given time.

2. Error Reduction

* Manual data entry and barcode scanning are prone to human errors.

* RFID automates identification and data capture, minimizing mistakes in shipping, receiving, and order
picking.

+ It ensures accurate order fulfillment and reduces stock discrepancies.

3. Enhanced Operational Efficiency
* Faster scanning of multiple tags simultaneously speeds up inventory audits and warehouse operations.
* RFID supports automated replenishment, triggering reorders when stock reaches a threshold.

+ It reduces labor costs, shrinkage, and out-of-stock situations, improving overall productivity.
