- Course as printed: Biomechanics (code 702ME0E044)

````text
Program: B Tech (Mechanical Engineering)   Semester: V
Course: Biomechanics   Code: 702ME0E044
Teaching Scheme   Evaluation Scheme
Lecture   Practical   Tutorial   Internal Continuous   Term End
(Hours   (Hours   (Hours   Credit   Assessment (ICA)   Examinations (TEE)
per week)   per week)   per week)   (Marks - 50)   (Marks- 100)
2   0   1   3   Marks Scaled to 50   Marks Scaled to 50
Pre-requisite: Engineering Mechanics
Course Objective
This course focusses on the application of classical mechanics to various biological problems by
combining the fields of biology and physiology. This course is concerned with the application of
principles of mechanics to the conceptualization, design, development and analysis of equipment
and systems as applied to human body.
Course Outcomes
After completion of the course, students will be able to -
1. Correlate mechanics, biomechanics and kinesiology and differentiate between their uses in the
analysis of human movement
2. Relate the reference positions, planes, and axes associated with the human body
3. Evaluate the skeletal considerations for movement and the different mechanics involved in
bones
Detailed Syllabus
Unit   Description   Duration
1.   Basic Terminology   05
Biomechanics, kinesiology, anatomy, functional anatomy
linear motion, angular motion, kinematics, kinetics, statics and dynamics.
2.   Kinematic Concepts for Analyzing Human Motion   05
Linear, angular and general motion, anatomical reference position, planes
and axes, joint movement terminology, spatial reference systems, analyzing
human movement and tools for measuring kinematic quantities.
3.   Applications of Statics to Biomechanics   10
Skeletal joints, skeletal muscles, mechanics of the elbow, mechanics of the
shoulder, mechanics of the spinal column, mechanics of the hip, mechanics
of the knee and mechanics of the ankle.
4.   Skeletal Considerations for Movement   10
Bone tissue function, composition of bone tissue, macroscopic structure of
bone, bone formation, strength and stiffness of bone, loads applied to bone,
stress fractures, articular cartilage, fibrocartilage and ligaments.
Total   30
````
