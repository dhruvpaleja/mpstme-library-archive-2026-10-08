b   The Factor of Safety (FoS) is a design criterion used in engineering to ensure that a structure
or component can safely withstand loads greater than the expected working load.
Purpose of Factor of Safety
To prevent failure due to unexpected stresses or loading conditions.
To account for uncertainties in material properties, loading, and working conditions.
To ensure reliability and durability of components during service life.
Conditions Requiring Higher Factor of Safety
A higher FoS (typically 4 to 6 or more) is chosen when:
********** 4 **********
--- page 5 ---
1. Uncertain loading conditions: When actual loads are unpredictable (e.g., wind loads,
earthquake forces).
2. Critical applications: Where failure could result in loss of life or significant damage
(e.g., aircraft parts, pressure vessels, cranes).
3. Poor maintenance or inspection: When regular checks are not feasible.
4. Use of brittle materials: Such as cast iron, which have low ductility and fail suddenly.
5. Inaccurate manufacturing or testing: When there is uncertainty in quality control.
Conditions Requiring Lower Factor of Safety
A lower FoS (typically 1.5 to 3) is acceptable when:
1. Loads are well-known and predictable: Such as in laboratory-tested equipment or
controlled environments.
2. Non-critical components: Where failure does not cause serious harm (e.g., household
appliances).
3. High material cost or weight constraints: Such as in aerospace or automotive
industries, where efficiency is crucial.
4. Ductile materials used: Because they provide visible warning before failure.

A heat engine operates on the basic principle of conversion of heat energy into mechanical

• A hot source that supplies heat energy.

Bernoulli’s Theorem states that:
For an incompressible, non-viscous fluid flowing in a steady and continuous streamline, the
total energy per unit weight (sum of pressure energy, kinetic energy, and potential energy)
remains constant throughout the flow.
Explanation of Bernoulli's theorem with definition 4 marks
Assumptions of Bernoulli's theorem 3 marks
• The fluid is ideal (non-viscous) — no friction losses.
• The flow is steady — velocity at a point does not change with time.
• The flow is incompressible — density remains constant.
• The flow is along a streamline — energy is conserved along that path.
• There is no external energy added or lost (e.g., no pump or turbine between sections).
• The gravitational field is uniform.
