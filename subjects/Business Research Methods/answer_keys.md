• Moderating Variable (1 mark): Organizational support
•   Relationship explanation (1 mark): IVs influence DV; moderation alters strength
b. Problem Statement & Hypotheses [5 marks]
• Problem Statement (2 marks):
To analyze factors affecting teacher satisfaction in online tutoring platforms.
• Hypotheses (3 marks):
• H1: Incentives positively influence teacher satisfaction
• H2: Work flexibility positively impacts teacher satisfaction
Q.2.   a. Purpose of Study
• Type (2 marks): Causal Research
•   Justification (3 marks):
The objective is to determine the cause of decline in subscription renewals, i.e., whether it is
due to pricing dissatisfaction or competitive offerings. This requires establishing a cause–
effect relationship, making causal research the most appropriate purpose.

• Current Unit of Analysis (2 marks):
Individual subscribers (each user’s renewal behavior is studied)

Q.3.   a.
Comparison of Sampling Methods
•   Probability Sampling (2 marks):
• Every element has a known and equal chance of selection
•   Results are more representative and generalizable
• Examples: Simple random sampling, stratified sampling
•   Non-probability Sampling (2 marks):
•   Selection is based on convenience or judgment
•   Faster and cost-effective but less representative
• Examples: Convenience sampling, purposive sampling
Recommendation (1 mark):
• For time and budget constraints → Non-probability (convenience sampling)
• For high accuracy and generalization → Probability sampling
b.
Qualitative Methods
• Method 1 explanation – 2 marks
• Method 2 explanation – 2 marks
• Example – 1 mark
• Focus Groups: Group discussion to understand opinions
• Case Study: In-depth analysis of a single unit
(Example: Studying app usage behavior)
Q.4.
Identifying contradictions/inconsistencies (4 marks):
• Compare findings across studies (1 mark):
Check if results differ (e.g., some studies show security as a major factor, others show
convenience)
• Examine methodology differences (1 mark):
Differences in sample size, data collection methods, or analysis techniques
• Analyze context and sample (1 mark):
Variations across regions, demographics, or time periods
• Check definitions and variables (1 mark):
Different studies may define constructs (e.g., “trust”) differently

Importance of recognizing gaps (6 marks):
• Avoids duplication (2 marks):
Prevents repeating already established findings
•   Identifies research gaps (2 marks):
Highlights unexplored areas or conflicting results needing clarification
•   Contributes new insights (2 marks):
Enables development of new models, variables, or context-specific findings
Q.5.
Reliability (Consistency) Methods (6 marks):
•   Test-retest reliability (3 marks):
Measures stability over time by administering the same instrument twice
Example: Same survey given after two weeks gives similar results
•   Internal consistency (Cronbach’s Alpha) (3 marks):
Measures consistency among items in a scale
Example: Multiple questions measuring satisfaction give consistent responses
Validity (4 marks):
•   Definition (2 marks):
Validity refers to the extent to which an instrument measures what it is intended to
measure
• Example (2 marks):
A questionnaire designed to measure “customer satisfaction” should actually capture
satisfaction and not unrelated aspects like price alone
Q.6.
Measurement Error:
The difference between the actual value and the observed response due to flaws in
questionnaire design or respondent bias.
1. Leading/Loaded Questions (3 marks)
•   Issue (1 mark): Question suggests a preferred response
• Example (1 mark):
“Don’t you agree that our brand is highly trustworthy?”
• Improved (1 mark):
“How would you rate the trustworthiness of the brand on a scale of 1–5?”
2. Double-Barreled Questions (3 marks)
•   Issue (1 mark): Measures two constructs in one question
• Example (1 mark):
“Is the brand reliable and affordable?”
• Improved (1 mark):
• “How reliable do you find the brand?”
********** 3 **********
--- page 4 ---
• “How affordable do you find the brand?”
3. Ambiguous/Vague Questions (3 marks)
•   Issue (1 mark): Lack of clarity or undefined terms
• Example (1 mark):
“Do you think the brand performs well?”
• Improved (1 mark):
“How satisfied are you with the brand’s product quality on a scale of 1–5?”
Q.7.
a. Technique (2 marks):
• One-Way ANOVA
•   Justification: Used to compare mean satisfaction levels across more than two groups
b. Hypotheses (2 marks):
•   H₀: Customer satisfaction is the same across all service channels
•   H₁: At least one channel has a different level of satisfaction
c. Role of ANOVA (3 marks):
• Determines whether differences in satisfaction across channels are statistically significant
• Helps identify if service channel influences customer experience
•   Supports data-driven decision-making
d. Managerial Action (3 marks):
• Conduct post hoc analysis to identify which channels differ
• Improve low-performing channels or invest more in high-performing ones

a. Explain reliability and validity in qualitative research.
Reliability and validity have a slightly different meaning in qualitative
research in comparison to quantitative research. Reliability in qualitative
data analysis includes category and inter-judge reliability. Category
reliability “depends on the analyst’s ability to formulate categories and [5
present definitions of the categories so they will agree on which items of
a certain population belong in a category and which do not”. Thus, marks]
category reliability relates to the extent to which category definitions can
be used to classify the qualitative data. Well-defined categories will lead
to higher category reliability.
Validity refers to the extent to which the research results (1) accurately
represent the collected data (internal validity) and (2) can be generalized
or transferred to other contexts or settings (external validity). Two
methods that have been developed to achieve validity in qualitative
research are:
Supporting generalizations by counts of events. This can address
common concerns about the reporting of qualitative data: that anecdote
supporting the researcher’s theory have been selected, or that too much
attention has been paid to a small number of events, at the expense of
more common ones. Ensuring representativeness of cases and the
inclusion of deviant cases (cases that may contradict your theory). The
selection of deviant cases provides a strong test of your theory.
