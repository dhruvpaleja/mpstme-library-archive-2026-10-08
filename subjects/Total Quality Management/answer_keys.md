a) Discussion about differences in manufacturing and service organizations with reference to
their respective dimensions of quality. --- 05 M
Manufacturing and service organizations differ significantly in how quality is defined and evaluated,
particularly when viewed through their respective dimensions of quality. In manufacturing
organizations, quality is associated with tangible products and is assessed through objective and
measurable dimensions such as performance, features, reliability, conformance, durability,
serviceability, aesthetics, and perceived quality. Performance refers to the primary operating
characteristics of a product, while features add additional value in the form of extra attributes.
Reliability and durability focus on the product’s consistency and lifespan under stated conditions,
whereas conformance ensures that the product meets predefined standards. Serviceability reflects the
ease of repair, and aesthetics along with perceived quality influence customer appeal through look,
feel, and brand image. These dimensions allow manufacturing organizations to emphasize
standardization, inspection, and defect prevention to ensure consistent quality.
In contrast, service organizations deal with intangible outputs, and their quality is evaluated through
dimensions that are largely customer-centric and perception based. These include time, timeliness,
completeness, courtesy, consistency, accessibility, convenience, accuracy, and responsiveness. Time
and timeliness focus on how quickly and reliably services are delivered, while completeness ensures
that all aspects of the service are fulfilled. Courtesy and responsiveness highlight the behaviour and
attitude of service personnel, which directly influence customer satisfaction. Consistency ensures
uniform service delivery across different customers and occasions, whereas accessibility and
convenience determine how easily a customer can obtain the service. Accuracy reflects doing the
service right the first time, which is critical since errors cannot be easily corrected after delivery. These
dimensions indicate that service quality depends heavily on human interaction, communication, and
real-time performance.
Overall, the key difference lies in the nature and measurement of quality. Manufacturing quality
dimensions are technical, measurable, and product-oriented, whereas service quality dimensions are
experiential, subjective, and process-oriented. While manufacturing organizations can evaluate and
control quality before the product reaches the customer, service organizations must ensure quality
during the service encounter itself due to simultaneous production and consumption. Thus,

b) Explanation about the differences between TQM and Six Sigma. --- 05 M
TQM is a broad, culture-oriented approach that emphasizes continuous improvement, customer
satisfaction, and the involvement of all employees across the organization. It focuses on long-term
development of a quality culture through leadership commitment, teamwork, and process
improvement. In contrast, Six Sigma is a focused, data-driven methodology that aims at reducing
defects and process variation using structured techniques such as DMAIC (Define, Measure, Analyze,
Improve, Control). While TQM promotes incremental improvements across all functions, Six Sigma
concentrates on specific projects with clearly defined goals and measurable results.
From an implementation perspective, TQM relies on managerial principles and qualitative tools,
encouraging participation at all levels without rigid role definitions. Six Sigma, however, adopts a
highly structured approach with specialized roles like Green Belts and Black Belts, and uses advanced
statistical tools for decision-making. This makes Six Sigma more precise and result-oriented, whereas
TQM provides flexibility and fosters a participative environment. Additionally, TQM does not always
enforce strict performance metrics, while Six Sigma sets quantifiable targets, often striving for near-
zero defects, which enhances accountability and clarity in outcomes.
In terms of effectiveness, TQM is particularly strong in developing a sustainable quality culture,
improving employee engagement, and ensuring continuous improvement over time. However, its
impact may be slower and less measurable. Six Sigma, on the other hand, is highly effective in
achieving rapid and measurable improvements, especially in processes where data can be analysed
rigorously. It significantly reduces defects and enhances efficiency but may require substantial
investment in training and expertise. Therefore, while TQM builds the foundation for quality, Six
Sigma acts as a powerful analytical tool, and their combined use can lead to comprehensive and
sustained excellence in quality management.
c) Explanation about Poke-Yoke method of error prevention. –- 05 M
Poka-Yoke, introduced by Shigeo Shingo, is a quality management technique used to prevent errors
or make them immediately detectable during a process. The main objective is to design systems in
such a way that mistakes are either impossible to occur or are instantly identified and corrected, thereby
improving quality and reducing defects. Poka-Yoke works on the principle that human errors are
inevitable, but defects can be prevented by modifying the process or design. It is widely used in
manufacturing as well as service industries to ensure consistency and reliability. There are generally
two approaches: prevention-based (eliminates the possibility of error) and detection-based (identifies
errors before they lead to defects).
In manufacturing, a common example is a USB drive or SIM card design, which can be inserted only
in one direction, preventing incorrect usage. Another example is a fixture in assembly lines that allows
parts to fit only in the correct orientation. In everyday life, an automatic washing machine that does
not start when the door is open is also a form of Poka-Yoke. In service industries, online forms that do
not allow submission until all required fields are filled correctly help prevent errors. Similarly, ATMs
that prompt users to take their card before dispensing cash reduce the chance of forgetting the card.
Thus, Poka-Yoke is an effective technique in quality management that ensures error prevention,
improved efficiency, and customer satisfaction by embedding quality directly into the process rather
than relying on inspection.
d) Comparison between chance causes and assignable causes of variation in quality. –- 05 M
Chance causes: Some Stable pattern of variation or a constant cause system is inherent in any particular
scheme of production and inspection. This pattern results from many minor causes that behave in a

be prevented or eliminated under any circumstance. Such type of variation has got to be allowed within

Assignable causes: The second type of variation attributed to any production process is due to non-

and so on. These causes can be identified and eliminated and are to be discovered in a production
process before it goes wrong that is before the production becomes defective.

The commercial bank is facing quality problems that Crosby’s philosophy would classify as

Explanation about zero defects in banking services and outlining a step-by-step practical
improvement plan. --- 07 M
According to Crosby, quality means meeting clearly defined requirements, and zero defects means that
there should be no acceptable level of errors built into the process. In a banking service, zero defects
does not mean that people never make mistakes, but it does mean that the system should be designed
so that errors are prevented, detected early, and not passed on to customers. So, the ideal standard for
the bank should be correct transactions, accurate billing, timely service, and smooth coordination
across all departments from the first attempt.
The first step in the improvement plan is for top management to make a clear commitment that quality
is a priority and that customer service defects will not be tolerated. After that, the bank should form a
cross-functional quality improvement team with representatives from reception, operations, credit, IT,
billing, and customer service. This team should identify the main defect points in the service process
and set measurable quality standards for waiting time, transaction accuracy, complaint resolution time,
and billing correctness. Next, the bank should measure the current level of poor quality by collecting

strongly emphasizes the cost of nonconformance, so the bank should calculate how much money is

Once management sees the real cost of poor quality, it becomes easier to support corrective action.
The bank should then carry out intensive staff training on the new software system and on standard

a) Discussion about Job characteristics theory of employee empowerment. --- 05 M
The Job characteristics theory explains how specific features of a job influence employee motivation,
satisfaction, and ultimately empowerment. According to this theory, five core job dimensions—skill
variety, task identity, task significance, autonomy, and feedback create meaningful work experiences
that enhance an employee’s sense of responsibility and control. For instance, when an employee is
given autonomy in decision making and receives regular feedback, they feel more empowered to take
initiative and improve performance. A practical example can be seen in a manufacturing unit where
workers are allowed to manage their own work schedules and monitor quality, rather than strictly
following supervisor instructions. This not only increases motivation but also builds ownership and
accountability, which are key aspects of employee empowerment. Thus, the Job characteristics theory
emphasizes job design and work structure, ensuring that roles are meaningful and engaging
Discussion about Acquired needs theory of employee empowerment. --- 05 M
The Acquired needs theory developed focuses on three learned needs- need for achievement, need for
affiliation, and need for power that drive individual behavior at work. Employee empowerment can be
enhanced by aligning job roles and responsibilities with these dominant needs. For example,
employees with a high need for achievement can be empowered by assigning challenging tasks with
clear goals and measurable outcomes, while those with a high need for power can be given leadership
roles or decision-making authority. Similarly, employees with a strong need for affiliation can be
empowered through teamwork and collaborative tasks. By understanding and addressing these needs,
organizations can create an environment where employees feel valued, motivated, and capable of
contributing effectively. Thus, the Acquired needs theory emphasizes individual differences and
personal motivation.

b) Explanation of Deming’s 14 principles of total quality management (TQM) – 10 M
1) Create constancy of purpose for improving products and services: Plan for quality in the long
term. Don't just do the same things better – find better things to do.
2) Adopt the new philosophy: Embrace quality throughout the organization Create your quality
vision and implement it.
3) Cease dependence on inspection to achieve quality: Inspections are costly and unreliable and they
don't improve quality; they merely find a lack of quality.
4) End the practice of awarding business on price alone; instead, minimize total cost by working with
a single supplier: Look at suppliers as your partners in quality. Encourage them to spend time
improving their own quality – they shouldn't compete for your business based on price alone.
5) Improve constantly and forever every process for planning, production and service: to improve
quality and productivity, and thus constantly decrease costs.
6) Institute training on the job: Build a foundation of common knowledge Allow workers to
understand their roles in the "big picture".
7) Adopt and institute leadership: Don't simply supervise – provide support and resources so that each
staff member can do his or her best. Be a coach instead of a policeman.
8) Drive out fear: Allow people to perform at their best by ensuring that they're not afraid to express
ideas or concerns. Make workers feel valued and encourage them to look for better ways to do
things.
9) Break down barriers between staff areas: People in research, design, sales, and production must
work as a team, to foresee problems of production and in use that may be encountered with the
product or service.
10) Eliminate slogans, exhortations and targets for the workforce: Let people know exactly what you
want – don't make them guess. Don't let words and nice-sounding phrases replace effective
leadership.
11) Eliminate numerical quotas for the workforce and numerical goals for management: Look at how
the process is carried out, not just numerical targets. Measure the process rather than the people
behind the process.
12) Remove barriers that rob people of pride of workmanship and eliminate the annual rating or merit
system: This means, inter alia, abolishment of the annual or merit rating and of management by
objective.
13) Institute a vigorous program of education and self-improvement for everyone: Improve the current
skills of workers. Encourage people to learn new skills to prepare for future changes and
challenges.
14) Put everybody in the company to work accomplishing the transformation: The transformation is
everybody's job. Improve your overall organization by having each person take a step toward
quality.

b) Explanation about the procedure of a double sampling plan with example. --- 07 M
Single Sampling Plan calls for decision on acceptance or rejection of a lot on the basis of evidence of
one sample from the lot. Double Sampling Plan involves the possibility of putting off the decision on
the lot until a second sample has been taken. A lot may be accepted at once if the first sample is good
enough or rejected at once if the first sample is bad enough. If the first sample is neither good nor bad
enough, the decision is based on the evidence of the first and second samples combined. In general,
Double Sampling Plan involves less inspection than single sampling for any given quality protection.
They also have certain psychological advantages based on the idea of giving a second chance to
doubtful lots. The symbols used in connection with the Double Sampling Plan are as follows: -
N = Number of pieces in the lot.
c1 = Acceptance number for the first sample i.e., the maximum number of defectives that permits the
acceptance of the lot based on the first sample.
c2 = Acceptance number for the two samples combined that is the maximum number of defectives that
will permit the acceptance of the lot on the basis of two samples.
Considering the following example:
The above double sampling plan can be interpreted as follows through the following steps:
Inspect the first sample of 36 items from a lot of 1000 items.
Accept the lot on the basis of the first sample if the sample contains 0 defective.
Reject the lot on the basis of the first sample if the sample contains more than 3 defectives.
Inspect a second sample of 59 items if the first sample contains 1,2 or 3 defectives.
Accept the lot on the basis of the combined sample of 95 items if the combined sample contains 3 or
less defectives.
Reject the lot on the basis of the combined sample if the combined sample contains more than 3
defectives.

c) Explanation about Deming Cycle and its role in quality improvement. --- 05 M
The Deming Cycle, also known as the PDSA (Plan–Do–Study–Act) cycle, was popularized by W.
Edwards Deming as a continuous improvement tool. It provides a systematic method for improving
processes, products, and services through an iterative approach.
The cycle begins with the Plan phase, where problems are identified, objectives are set, and strategies
are developed to improve quality. For example, an organization may plan to reduce defects in a
production process by analysing root causes. The next step is Do, where the planned solution is
implemented on a small scale, such as introducing a new method or process change in a pilot area.
This is followed by the Study phase, where the results are monitored and compared with expected
outcomes to determine whether improvements have been achieved. For instance, defect rates before
and after implementation are analysed. Finally, in the Act phase, successful changes are standardized
and implemented across the organization or corrective actions are taken if results are not satisfactory.
The Deming Cycle is widely used to improve quality continuously by promoting a structured problem-
solving approach. It helps organizations identify inefficiencies, test solutions, and implement
improvements systematically. Because it is cyclical, the process is repeated continuously, leading to
ongoing enhancement of quality, increased efficiency, reduced defects, and better customer
satisfaction. Thus, the PDSA cycle serves as a fundamental tool in quality management for achieving
sustained improvement.
