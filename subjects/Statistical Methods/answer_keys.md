Q7 A In a study of urban wildlife, researchers randomly select several city parks and then count the
number of a specific bird species in each selected park. What is the sampling method being used? 6
Discuss the advantages of this method in ecological research
SOL ANS: The sampling method being used in this study is Cluster Sampling.
In cluster sampling, the population is divided into groups (called clusters)—in this case, city
parks. Instead of sampling individual birds directly from all parks, researchers randomly select
certain parks (clusters) and then collect data from all (or a sample of) birds within those
selected parks.
So here: Population: All birds of the specific species in the city
Clusters: Individual city parks
Sample: Birds found in the randomly selected parks….03 MARKS
Advantages of Cluster Sampling in Ecological Research:
1. Cost-effective and practical:
It is often difficult and expensive to observe wildlife across all parks or the entire city.
Sampling a few parks reduces travel, time, and resource costs.
2. Easier data collection:
Since observations are concentrated within a few selected locations, researchers can
collect data more efficiently.
3. Useful when complete population lists are unavailable:
In ecology, it's hard to list all individual animals, but parks (clusters) are easily
identifiable, making cluster sampling feasible.
4. Good for large, geographically spread populations:
When birds are distributed across a large urban area, cluster sampling allows
researchers to capture variation without needing to cover the entire city.
5. Allows study of environmental influence:
Each park may represent a different habitat condition (e.g., vegetation, pollution level).
Sampling clusters helps in comparing how local environments affect bird
populations……03 MARKS

Dimensionality Reduction:
Dimensionality reduction is a technique used to reduce the number of features (variables) in a
dataset while retaining most of the important information. It helps to simplify models, reduce
computation, and remove noise or redundancy.
Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and How It Achieves Dimensionality Reduction:
• PCA transforms the original correlated variables into a smaller set of uncorrelated variables
called principal components.
• These components are linear combinations of the original variables, ordered so that the first
few components capture the maximum variance in the data.
• By selecting only the top principal components, PCA reduces the number of dimensions while
preserving most of the dataset’s variability.
Example: A dataset with 10 correlated features can be reduced to 2–3 principal components that still
explain most of the data variation.
