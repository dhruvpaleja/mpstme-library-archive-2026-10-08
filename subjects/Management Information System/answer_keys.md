Q 4
( a) Define Supply Chain. Also discuss how Supply Chain Management is different from Logistic Management in brief.
CO2
SCM definition. ( 2 marks)
Difference (8 marks)
Supply Chain Management (SCM) and Logistics Management are closely related but distinct concepts.
Supply Chain Management (SCM):
•   Scope: Encompasses the entire flow of goods, information, and finances from raw materials to the end consumer.
It includes procurement, production, distribution, and even customer service.
•   Focus: Strategic in nature, aiming to optimize overall supply chain performance, enhance collaboration among
stakeholders, and improve efficiency and sustainability.
•   Activities: Involves demand planning, supplier relationship management, inventory control, and coordinating
across multiple partners and channels.
Logistics Management:
•   Scope: A subset of SCM that specifically focuses on the movement and storage of goods. It deals with the physical
transportation and warehousing of products.
•   Focus: Tactical in nature, primarily concerned with the efficient movement and storage of goods to meet
customer demand.
•   Activities: Includes transportation management, warehousing, order fulfillment, inventory management, and last-
mile delivery.
In summary, while logistics management is a critical part of the broader supply chain management framework, SCM
encompasses a wider range of processes and strategic considerations.

Q.6 (a) Explain how IT strategies enables effective management and operations in multinational companies.
8 to 10 points
A global business strategy is a plan that outlines how a company will expand its operations beyond its domestic market. The goal
is to increase revenue, profitability, and market share.
Global business and IT strategies are essential to ensure that companies operate efficiently, remain competitive, and adapt to
the evolving business environment. Here are some key components and considerations for creating effective global business and
IT strategies:
1. Alignment with Business Goals
•   Strategic Fit: Ensure that IT strategies align with the overall business goals, whether that’s growth, cost reduction,
customer engagement, or market expansion.
•   Agility and Innovation: Companies should implement IT strategies that allow for rapid adaptation to market changes,
fostering innovation.
2. Globalization and Localization
•   Scalability: IT systems must be scalable to support global operations across different regions and markets.
•   Localization of Solutions: Ensure that the technology is adaptable to local languages, currencies, and regulatory
requirements.
3. Standardization vs. Flexibility
•   Global Standards: Maintain global standards in infrastructure, security, and processes to ensure consistency across
regions.
•   Flexible Solutions: Allow for regional customization to address specific local market needs and legal requirements.
4. Cloud Computing
•   Global Cloud Solutions: The use of cloud computing enables businesses to scale globally, access data in real-time, and
improve collaboration across international teams.
•   Multi-Cloud Strategies: Many global companies are opting for multi-cloud strategies to avoid vendor lock-in and
enhance security and reliability.
5. Cybersecurity and Data Privacy
•   Security Frameworks: Global businesses must implement robust cybersecurity measures to protect data and
infrastructure across multiple geographies.
•   Compliance with Global Regulations: Data privacy regulations, such as the GDPR in Europe and CCPA in the U.S., require
careful attention when crafting global IT strategies.
6. Global Talent Management
• Remote Workforce: Leverage IT solutions to support remote workforces, ensuring global teams remain productive.
•   Skill Development: Investing in upskilling the workforce on emerging technologies (e.g., AI, machine learning) and
business processes is crucial.
7. Digital Transformation
•   Automation and AI: Global businesses are increasingly adopting automation, AI, and machine learning to streamline
operations and enhance customer experiences.
• ERP and CRM Systems: Implement global enterprise resource planning (ERP) and customer relationship management
(CRM) systems to create unified operations.
8. Data-Driven Decision Making
•   Global Data Integration: Consolidate data from different regions to provide a unified view of the business for better
decision-making.
•   Big Data and Analytics: Use advanced analytics and business intelligence tools to forecast trends, optimize supply
chains, and identify new business opportunities.
9. Supply Chain Integration
•   End-to-End Visibility: Implement IT solutions that offer real-time insights into global supply chains, improving efficiency
and reducing risk.
•   Blockchain: Some global businesses are using blockchain technology to enhance transparency and security in supply
chain management.
10. Sustainability and Green IT
•   Energy Efficiency: Implement energy-efficient IT infrastructure and sustainable business practices to reduce carbon
footprints.

11. Customer Experience
•   Omnichannel Solutions: Develop IT systems that provide a seamless customer experience across different platforms

•   Personalization: Use AI and big data to deliver personalized services and experiences to customers globally.

•   Strategic Alliances: Collaborate with technology partners, startups, and other businesses to foster innovation and

Q 7 (a) Discuss key steps to effectively manage knowledge for Business Intelligence. CO2
7-8 points
1. Identify and Capture Knowledge
Sources of Knowledge: Identify where knowledge resides—internally (employees, documents) and externally
(customers, market trends).
Knowledge Management Systems (KMS): Implement systems to capture, store, and organize both tacit (experiential)
and explicit (documented) knowledge.
2. Organize and Structure Knowledge
Taxonomy and Metadata: Create a clear structure using taxonomies and metadata to facilitate easy retrieval and
navigation.
Data Integration: Integrate data from various sources to provide a comprehensive view, ensuring consistency and
reliability.
3. Analyze and Interpret Data
Analytics Tools: Utilize BI tools (like Tableau, Power BI) to analyze data and visualize insights.
Data Quality Management: Ensure data accuracy, completeness, and consistency to derive meaningful insights.
4. Facilitate Knowledge Sharing
Collaborative Platforms: Encourage collaboration through platforms that allow employees to share insights and best
practices.
Communities of Practice: Establish communities within the organization to foster knowledge exchange around
specific topics or functions.
5. Develop a Culture of Continuous Learning
Training Programs: Invest in training to enhance data literacy and BI tool usage among employees.
Feedback Loops: Create mechanisms for feedback on BI processes to continuously improve and adapt.
6. Leverage Advanced Technologies
Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning: Implement AI/ML to uncover hidden patterns in data and enhance
predictive analytics.
Natural Language Processing (NLP): Use NLP for sentiment analysis and to interpret unstructured data sources.
7. Monitor and Evaluate Performance
KPIs and Metrics: Define key performance indicators (KPIs) to assess the effectiveness of knowledge management
initiatives.
Regular Reviews: Conduct periodic reviews of BI processes and outcomes to identify areas for improvement.
8. Ensure Security and Compliance
Data Governance: Establish policies for data access, usage, and sharing to protect sensitive information.
Regulatory Compliance: Ensure adherence to relevant regulations (e.g., GDPR) when managing and analyzing data.
