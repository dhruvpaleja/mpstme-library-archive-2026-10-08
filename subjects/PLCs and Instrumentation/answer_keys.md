b) Comparison between inductive proximity sensor and capacitive proximity sensor based on
principle of operation, range, type of materials detected, advantages, disadvantages and
applications - 05 M
An inductive proximity sensor and a capacitive proximity sensor differ primarily in their operating
principles and applications. An inductive proximity sensor works on the principle of electromagnetic
induction, where a high-frequency oscillating magnetic field is generated by a coil. When a metallic
object enters this field, eddy currents are induced in the object, resulting in a change in amplitude of
oscillations, which is detected to produce an output signal. In contrast, a capacitive proximity sensor
operates on the principle of change in capacitance caused by the presence of an object. These sensors
are broadly classified into dielectric type and conductive type. In the dielectric type, the object
conductive or non-conductive material alters the dielectric constant between the sensor electrodes,
thereby changing capacitance. In the conductive type, the object itself acts as one of the capacitor
plates, directly influencing the electric field.
In terms of sensing range, inductive sensors have a short range but offer high precision and
repeatability, while capacitive sensors also have a short sensing range, it can even detect objects
through insulating materials like plastic or glass. Regarding material detection, inductive sensors are

limited to metallic objects only, whereas capacitive sensors (dielectric type) can detect both metallic
and non-metallic materials, including liquids, powders, plastics, and granular substances. Inductive
sensors are highly robust, immune to dust, moisture, and environmental variations, and provide stable

hand, are versatile and widely used for level sensing and presence detection; however, they are more
sensitive to environmental conditions such as humidity, temperature, and contamination, which may

extensively used in liquid level measurement, packaging industries, food processing, and detection of
non-metallic materials.

Q4.
a) Comparison between Stain gauge and Piezoelectric transducer. ---10 M
A strain gauge and a piezoelectric transducer differ significantly in their working, materials, and
applications. A strain gauge operates on the principle that the electrical resistance of a conductor
changes when it is stretched or compressed. It is typically made of metallic foil such as constantan or
semiconductor materials bonded to a backing material. When strain is applied, its resistance changes
proportionally, which is measured using a Wheatstone bridge circuit. Since it requires an external
power source for operation, it is a passive transducer. Strain gauges are suitable for both static and
dynamic measurements over a moderate range, commonly used in load cells, stress analysis, and
structural health monitoring. Their advantages include good accuracy, versatility, and capability to
measure static quantities; however, they have limitations such as temperature sensitivity, need for
careful calibration, and relatively low output signal.
In contrast, a piezoelectric transducer works on the piezoelectric effect, where certain materials
generate an electrical charge when subjected to mechanical stress. Common materials used include
quartz, Rochelle salt, and synthetic ceramics like PZT (lead zirconate titanate). These transducers are
active, as they generate their own electrical output without requiring an external power source. They
are highly suitable for dynamic measurements such as vibration, pressure pulses, and acoustic signals,
but are not effective for static measurements because the generated charge dissipates over time.
Piezoelectric transducers offer advantages such as high sensitivity, fast response, and wide frequency
range, making them ideal for high-speed sensing applications. However, their limitations include
inability to measure steady-state signals, susceptibility to temperature variations, and the need for
specialized signal conditioning circuits. Overall, while strain gauges are preferred for static and low-
frequency measurements, piezoelectric transducers excel in dynamic and high-frequency applications.

a) Explanation about constructional details, characteristics and applications of thermistor. -- 05 M
A thermistor is a temperature-sensitive resistor whose resistance varies significantly with temperature.
It works on the principle that the electrical resistance of certain semiconductor materials changes with
temperature. Thermistors are generally made from metal oxides such as manganese, nickel, cobalt, or
copper. They are classified into NTC (Negative Temperature Coefficient) thermistors, where
resistance decreases with an increase in temperature, and PTC (Positive Temperature Coefficient)
thermistors, where resistance increases with temperature. Thermistors are passive transducers, as they
require an external power source to measure the change in resistance. They are widely used for
temperature measurement, control, and compensation in devices like digital thermometers, automotive
systems, and electronic circuits. Their advantages include high sensitivity, low cost, and small size,
while limitations include non-linear characteristics, limited temperature range, and susceptibility to
self-heating errors.
Explanation about constructional details, characteristics and applications of a Hall effect

A Hall effect transducer is used to measure magnetic fields, current, or position based on the Hall
effect, which states that when a current-carrying conductor or semiconductor is placed in a magnetic

commonly used in applications like current sensing, speed detection, proximity sensing, and position
measurement in automotive and industrial systems. Their advantages include non-contact operation,

as low output voltage, sensitivity to temperature variations, and the need for amplification and signal
conditioning.

a) Explanation about the working principle and various applications of an ultrasonic proximity
sensor. --- 05 M
An ultrasonic proximity sensor operates on the principle of sound wave reflection (echo method). It
consists of a transmitter that emits high-frequency ultrasonic waves typically above 20 kHz and a
receiver that detects the reflected waves. When ultrasonic pulses are transmitted, they travel through
the medium usually air, strike an object, and are reflected back to the receiver. The sensor measures the
time of flight of these waves, and based on this time, it determines the distance of the object. If the
object lies within the preset range, the sensor produces an output signal indicating its presence. An
important characteristic of ultrasonic sensors is the dead band, which is the minimum distance from the
sensor within which objects cannot be detected. This occurs because the transmitter continues to vibrate
for a short duration after emitting the pulse, and during this time, the receiver cannot accurately detect
returning echoes. As a result, any object located within this dead band remains undetected.
Ultrasonic proximity sensors are widely used due to their non-contact and versatile sensing capability.
They are commonly applied in industrial automation for object detection, level measurement in tanks
and silos, and conveyor system monitoring. In the automotive field, they are used in parking assistance
systems to detect nearby obstacles. They are also used in robotics for navigation and obstacle avoidance,
and in process industries for liquid level measurement. These sensors can detect objects irrespective of
their color, transparency, or surface finish, which is a major advantage over optical sensors. However,
their performance can be influenced by environmental factors such as temperature, humidity, and air
turbulence, and the presence of a dead band limits their effectiveness at very short distances.
b) Discussion about various features of Soft PLC. --- 05 M
A Soft PLC utilizes a personal computer (PC) as a versatile platform for industrial automation, offering
multiple functional roles. Firstly, the PC acts as an engineering workstation, where control programs
are developed, tested, and modified using standard programming tools, enabling flexibility and ease of
system design. Secondly, it serves as a human-machine interface (HMI), allowing operators to monitor

and control processes through graphical interfaces using software such as Visual Basic, Intellution, and
Wonderware. These tools enhance visualization, data logging, and user interaction.
Soft PLC systems are typically based on operating systems like Windows NT or Windows CE, which
provide a stable environment for automation tasks. The PC can directly utilize its CPU for control
processing, or it may employ co-processors to handle specific tasks, improving performance and
reliability. When configured as a real-time processor, the PC executes control functions similar to a
traditional PLC, ensuring deterministic operation required in industrial processes.
In more advanced configurations, the PC may be assisted by co-processor boards such as ISA or PC/104
modules, which handle   time-critical operations independently, thereby enhancing system
responsiveness. Additionally, the PC can function as a fieldbus gateway, enabling communication
between the control system and distributed I/O devices across industrial networks. This allows
integration with various field devices and supports distributed automation architectures.
Overall, a soft PLC leverages the computational power, flexibility, and connectivity of a PC to provide
a cost-effective and scalable automation solution, while also supporting advanced features like real-
time control, networking, and system integration.

Discussion about the rules for writing PLC ladder logic program. --- 04 M
The vertical lines called rails represents power lines. Where the left rail represents positive lead and the
right rail represents negative lead of the power supply.
1. The horizontal lines are called rungs and are labeled in numerical order from left to right and top to
bottom.
2. The ladder is read (executed) like a book from left to right and from top to bottom.
3. Whenever possible the components (instructions) are labeled (addressed) in numerical order from left
to right and from top to bottom.
4. The components (instructions) are shown in their normal condition, which means they are de-
energized.
5. Input instructions are to be placed on the left side and output instructions on the right side.
6. Input instructions can be placed in series or parallel based on the logic required.
7. Output instructions can be placed in parallel but never in series.
8. The energize output coil and its associated input instruction should have same address.

Ultrasonic and magnetic proximity sensors differ significantly in their working principles and
applications. An ultrasonic proximity sensor operates based on the principle of sound wave reflection.
It emits high-frequency sound waves above human hearing range and measures the time taken for the
echo to return after hitting an object. This allows it to detect the presence and distance of objects
regardless of their color, transparency, or surface finish. In contrast, a magnetic proximity sensor works
on the principle of magnetic field detection. It senses the presence of a magnetic field generated by a
magnet or a magnetized object, typically using devices like reed switches or Hall-effect sensors.In

Explanation of working- 04 M
Absolute Encoder directly generates Coded Data to represent angular positions using sequence of pulses.
No pulse coding is involved in this case. Code pattern on an Absolute Encoder disk is direct binary code
or Gray code. A disc or a plate containing opaque and transparent segments passes between a light source
such as LED and detector to interrupt a light beam. The electronic signals that are generated are then fed
into the controller where position and velocity information is calculated based upon the signals received.
The number of tracks is ‘n’ in this case. The disk is divided into ‘2n’ Sectors. Each partitioned area of the

Reason for Gray coding to be used in an absolute encoder- 03 M
There is a data interpretation problem associated with using binary codes in absolute encoder.
For E.g.-: The transition from 0011 to 0100 requires three bits switching. Here rotation was from angle 2
to angle 3 actually rotation was from angle 3 to angle 4 such errors can be avoided using Gray coding.
If the sensors are not aligned properly manufacturing errors and printing on the disk can result in large
number of errors in binary coding. In Gray coding there is only 1-bit change between consecutive position,
so this coding is preferred in absolute encoder.

voltage, speed, etc. There exists a fixed relationship between input and output, described by a
continuous model in form of a transfer function F. This transfer function can be expressed by a set of
differential equations. If equations are linear, the transfer function may be expressed as Laplace or Z-

their regulation. The time constant of the control system must be at least one order of magnitude
smaller than the smallest time constant of the plant. The principal task of the control system for a

changes and enabling better control of the heater. They are also cost-effective and easy to install

Q7.
a) Description of working of Sequencer output instruction (SQO) used in PLC ladder programing
along with relevant example. – 10 M
SQO (Sequencer Output) —Is an output instruction that uses a file to control various output devices.
Parameters that may be required to be entered in sequencer instructions can be summarized as follows:
File —Is the starting address for the registers in the sequencer file and you must use the indexed file
indicator (#) for this address. The file contains the data that will be transferred to the destination address
when the instruction undergoes a false-to-true transition. Each word in the file represents a position,
starting with position 0 and continuing to the file length.
Mask —Is the bit pattern through which the sequencer instruction moves source data to the destination
address. Recall that in the mask bit pattern, a 1 passes values while a 0 blocks the data flow. You use a
mask register or file name when you want to change the mask pattern under program control. An h is
placed behind the parameter to indicate that the mask is a hexadecimal number or a B to indicate binary
notation. Decimal notation is entered without any indicator.
Destination —Is the address of the output word or file to which the SQO moves the data from its
sequencer file.
Control —Is the address that contains the parameters with control information for the instruction. The
control register stores the status byte of the instruction, the length of the sequencer file, and the
instantaneous position in the file as follows:
- The enable bit (EN; bit 15) is set by a false-to-true rung transition and indicates that the instruction is
enabled. It follows the rung condition.
- The done bit (DN; bit 13) is set after the last word in the sequencer fi le is transferred. On the next false-
to-true transition of the rung with the done bit set, the position pointer is reset to 1.
-The error bit (ER; bit 11) is set when the processor detects a negative position value or a negative or
zero length value.
Length —Is the number of steps of the sequencer file starting at position 1. Position 0 is the start-up
position.
The instruction resets (wraps) to position 1 at each cycle completion. The actual file length will be 1 plus
the file length entered in the instruction.
Position —Indicates the step that is desired to start the sequencer instruction. The position is the word
location or step in the sequencer file from which the instruction moves data. Any value up to the file
length may be entered, but the instruction will always reset to 1 on the true-to-false transition after the
instruction has operated on the last position. Before we start the sequence, we need a starting point at
which the sequencer is in a neutral position. The start position is all zeros, representing this neutral
position; thus, all outputs will be off in position 0.
