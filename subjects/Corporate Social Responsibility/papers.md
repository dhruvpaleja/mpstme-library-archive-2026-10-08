CASE STUDY (Marks: 20]

Migrant labour is an issue of growing importance as a number of factors — including
growth in international trade, rapid population expansion, higher rates of urbanization,
and the widening gap between rich and poor and economic opportunities elsewhere,
either in other parts of their country or other parts of the world. The International Labour
Organisation estimates that there are roughly 96 million migrant workers and _ their
dependents in the world today; some experts predict that the number will double in the
next twenty years.

Often employed in low-skilled, low-paying jobs, migrants tend to be the most vulnerable
class of employees. These workers are often handicapped by inadequate training and
language skills, face discrimination in pay and other employment conditions, lack legal
protection and are sometimes victims of xenophobic harassment. Some are held in virtual
forced labour by having to pay large “deposits” to their employers and/or to surrender
their passports until they have worked for a stipulated amount of time. Some are forced to
pay excessive recruitment fees and to sign contracts stripping them of their essential
labour rights. Female migrant workers can be more susceptible than their male
counterparts to mistreatment, including physical and sexual harassment. In addition,
many migrant workers (particularly those supporting families in their home countries or
regions) seek substantial overtime work to maximize profits, raising concerns about
health and safety and product quality.

Companies operating in our increasingly globalized economy — whether large
transnational companies or smaller suppliers — may find themselves employing migrant
labour and will need to deal with the special challenges and issue surrounding this
segment of the workforce.

Questions for Study:
a) What problems do migrant workers face in globalised economy? (12)

b) What steps should companies take in order to be socially responsible to these
workforces? (8)

The AIDS epidemic today is unparalleled in the challenges it poses to the world, and it is
clearly an issue that no one can address alone. Business is an essential partner in the
response to AIDS. The private sector like the other sectors is not immune from AIDS.
Involvement of the private sector in the response to HIV/AIDS is crucial to the success of
our country's efforts against the epidemic.

1. What is the impact of AIDS on businesses? Do you agree that businesses in the near
future would be actively interested in addressing the issue of AIDS? Justify your answer

2. If business wants to partner with an NGO to address the issue of AIDS around its
factory, discuss what steps should the company take to initiate, manage and sustain its
partnership with the NGO.
