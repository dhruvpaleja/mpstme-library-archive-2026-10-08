E-Commerce- Meaning
E-Commerce refers to all commercial transactions conducted electronically using the internet
and digital technologies.
It includes the complete value chain:
Online product display
Ordering and payment
Digital service delivery
Logistics and fulfillment
After-sales services and customer support
E-Commerce functions through digital interfaces such as websites, mobile apps, online
marketplaces, and social commerce platforms.

i. Characteristics of Business to Government (B2G)
1.   Involvement of Public Sector Organizations
In B2G, government departments, ministries, and public sector units act as buyers, while
private businesses act as sellers.
Page 3 of 9
--- page 4 ---
2.   Strict Regulations and Compliance Requirements
Businesses must follow government rules, legal standards, certifications, and documentation
requirements to participate in procurement.
3.   Tender-Based Procurement System
Government purchases are made through online tenders where businesses submit bids.
Contracts are awarded based on eligibility, pricing, and compliance.
4.   Long Approval and Payment Cycles
Due to multiple levels of verification and authorization, approval and payment processes take
a longer time.
5.   High Level of Transparency and Accountability
Online portals ensure fair competition, public visibility of tenders, and proper record-keeping
to reduce corruption.
6.   Large-Scale and High-Value Projects
B2G transactions often involve infrastructure development, defense supplies, public services,
and IT projects with high value.
( 5 mks)
Examples:
Government e-Marketplace (GeM)
GeM is an online platform developed by the Government of India for procurement of goods
and services by government departments. Businesses register as sellers and offer products like
office equipment, IT services, and consultancy. Government buyers place orders directly
through the platform, ensuring transparency.
e-Tender Portals
e-Tender portals are online systems used by government bodies to invite bids from businesses
for large projects and services. Companies submit their proposals digitally, and contracts are
awarded based on eligibility, pricing, and compliance with regulations.
( 2.5 mks)
ii. Characteristics of Business to Consumers (B2C)
1.   Large Number of Customers
B2C platforms cater to millions of individual consumers, each making independent purchase
decisions.
2.   Smaller Order Quantities
Consumers usually purchase products in small quantities for personal use rather than bulk
buying.
3.   Fixed Pricing
Prices are pre-determined by the seller and displayed on the website. Consumers generally do
not negotiate prices.
Page 4 of 9
--- page 5 ---
4.   Short Buying Cycle
The time between identifying a need and making a purchase is very short, often influenced by
offers, convenience, and availability.
5.   Emphasis on Marketing and Branding
Digital marketing techniques such as advertisements, promotions, discounts, and brand image
strongly influence consumer buying behavior.
6.   Focus on Customer Experience
Website design, product descriptions, ease of navigation, delivery speed, return policies, and
customer support are crucial for customer satisfaction.
7.   Secure Online Payment Systems
B2C platforms rely on secure payment gateways, encryption, and authentication methods to
ensure safe online transactions.
( 5 mks)
Examples:
Amazon
Amazon is a global B2C e-commerce platform where businesses sell products directly to
consumers. It offers a wide range of goods including electronics, clothing, books, and
household items. Customers browse products, compare prices, read reviews, make payments
online, and receive doorstep delivery.
Flipkart
Flipkart is a leading Indian B2C e-commerce platform. It allows consumers to purchase
products such as smartphones, appliances, fashion items, and groceries. The platform focuses
on online payments, easy returns, and customer support to enhance the shopping experience.
Netflix
Netflix is a B2C digital service platform that provides movies, TV shows, and documentaries
directly to consumers through a subscription model. Users pay a fixed monthly fee to access
content online, making it an example of B2C service-based e-commerce.

c.   15
Ethical Concerns in Influencer Marketing (7 marks)
1. Authenticity Issues – Influencers may promote products they don’t genuinely use,
misleading followers.
2. Hidden Sponsorships – Lack of disclosure about paid partnerships creates ethical
concerns.
3. Exploitation of Trust – Followers often trust influencers more than brands, making
manipulation easier.
4. Targeting Vulnerable Audiences – Young consumers may be influenced without critical
evaluation.
5. False Claims – Risk of exaggerated or unverified product benefits.
6. Privacy Concerns – Influencers may share personal data of followers during campaigns.
Page 6 of 9
--- page 7 ---
7. Example – Cases where beauty influencers promoted skincare products without disclosing
sponsorship, leading to backlash.
Challenges of Maintaining Transparency in Targeted Advertising (8 marks)
1. Complex Algorithms – Consumers rarely understand how ads are targeted, reducing
transparency.
2. Data Privacy Issues – Use of personal data without clear consent raises ethical concerns.
3. Opaque Practices – Platforms often hide details of targeting criteria from users.
4. Consumer Distrust – Lack of clarity leads to suspicion about manipulation and
surveillance.
5. Regulatory Pressure – Compliance with GDPR and Indian data protection laws requires
constant monitoring.
6. Balancing Personalization vs. Privacy – Too much personalization feels intrusive; too
little reduces relevance.
7. Cross-platform Tracking – Difficult to explain how data is shared across apps and
websites.
8. Example – Facebook faced criticism for unclear ad targeting practices, sparking debates
on transparency.
