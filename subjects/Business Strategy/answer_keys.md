Q3. Explain Porter’s National Diamond Framework with an example of any company of your choice
from the Indian Manufacturing Industry.
1. FACTOR CONDITIONS—“Home grown” resources/capabilities more important
than natural endowments. (eg. Indian Movie Industry)
2.   RELATED AND SUPPORTING INDUSTRIES—Key role of “industry clusters” (Eg. Bangalore)
3. DEMAND CONDITIONS—Discerning domestic customers drive quality & innovation
(Eg. Switzerland, Germany)
4. STRATEGY, STRUCTURE, RIVALRY. E.g. domestic rivalry drives upgrading. (Eg. Indian
Manufacturing vs Indian IT Industry)
Consistency between Strategy and National Resource Conditions
In globally-competitive industries, firm strategy needs to take account of national conditions:
▪   U.S. textile manufacturers must compete on the basis of advanced process technologies and
focus on high quality, less price-sensitive market segments
▪   In the semiconductor industry, US firms like Intel, AMD, Nvidia and Qualcomm concentrate
mainly upon design of advanced microprocessors, graphic chips, and digital signal processing
chips. Chinese firms concentrate upon fabrication of high volume, less technologically advanced
items such as memory and logic chips, microcontrollers, and analog circuits.
▪   Dispersion of value chain to exploit different national environments (e.g. Nike conducts R&D in
US, components in Korea and Thailand, assembly in Indonesia, China, and India, marketing in
Europe and North America. )
Where to Locate Production?
Location decisions must take account of three sets of factors:
▪   National resource conditions: What are the major resources which the product requires?
Where are these available at low cost? (eg. Software Companies)
▪   Firm-specific advantages: to what extent is the company’s competitive advantage based upon
firm-specific resources and capabilities, and are these transferable? (eg. Walmart vs IKEA)

Q5. You are working for the Italian Coffee company Lavazza which is planning on scaling up its coffee
business in India by launching a range of premium instant coffees. Analyze the Industry Attractiveness
using Porter’s Five Forces of Competition Framework.
Entrants’ threat to industry profitability depends upon the height of barriers to entry.
The principal sources of barriers to entry are:
•   Capital requirements (eg. Boeing + Airbus, smartphone apps)
• Economies of scale (eg. Instagram, Automobile Industry)
•   Absolute cost advantage ( Saudi Aramco, Intel)
•   Product differentiation ( brand recognition and customer loyalty, new entrants need to
spend disproportionately)
•   Access to channels of distribution (eg. HUL, Britannia)
•   Legal and regulatory barriers ( Licence Raj till 1991)
•   Retaliation by incumbent firms ( price cutting, increase A&P, litigation, patents,
copyrights)
Extent of competitive pressure from producers of substitutes depends upon:
•   Buyers’ propensity to substitute
–   Substitution may happen at two levels:
–   Like for like manner (coffee and soft drinks)
–   In terms of function (theatres vs bowling)
•   For certain products like fuel, tobacco etc, demand is inelastic with respect to
price, for other products demand is elastic with price (eg. Bookstores, travel
agencies)
•   Also depends on the price-performance characteristics of substitutes (eg. Air travel vs train travel)

▪   Industry growth

▪ Product differentiation

▪ Corporate stakes
▪   Exit barriers (emotional, government restrictions)

Apple, MS Office)

• Complementor concentration relative to industry (Apple and iOS)
• Complements influence on demand (OTT platforms)
