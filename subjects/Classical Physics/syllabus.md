Pre-requisite: Knowledge of First year engineering Physics and Mathematics

Course Objective
This course aims to introduce students to the concepts of dynamical motion of rigid body and to develop
their skill in applying these concepts to understand and analyze real life phenomena.

Course Outcomes

After completion of the course, students will be able to -

1. Explain the motion of rigid bodies

2. Apply the concepts of rigid body motion to interpret real world phenomena and their applications
3. Solve advanced problems involving the dynamic motion of rigid bodies including celestial objects

Detailed Syllabus

Unit | Description Duration
1. Motion and Coordinate frames, Cartesian polar and spherical coordinate
frames, Displacement; Velocity; Acceleration in this frames, Particles and rigid 10
bodies, Newton’s Law of motion, Coulomb friction damping, Viscous friction
damping, Relative Motion, Motion of rigid bodies
2. Linear and angular Momentum, Impulse-momentum and Work-Energy 10
principles, 3D rigid body motion, 3D rotation, Euler angles
3. Kepler's Laws of Planetary Motion, Satellite orbits, Weather Satellites and
Sensing Systems, Operational polar orbiting and geostationary satellites, weather 10
sensors, Satellite data archives , Ground based sensors
Total 30
Text Books

1. N.C. Rana and PS Joag, Classical Mechanics, McGraw Hill Education, 2017.

Reference Books
1. Herbert Goldstein, Charles P Poole, John Safko, Classical Mechanics, 3' Edition, 2011.
