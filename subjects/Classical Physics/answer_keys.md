c.   Why does a moving bicycle or rolling tyre not fall down, whereas a   [4]
stationary one does?
Ans. A stationary bicycle or tyre falls because it has no mechanism to
correct small tilts—any slight imbalance produces a torque due to
gravity, and it topples. A moving bicycle or rolling tyre, however,
stays upright mainly due to dynamical stability, which comes from
two key effects:
1. Gyroscopic effect of the wheels: When the wheels rotate, they
possess angular momentum, making them resist changes in their

orientation. Thus, if the bicycle starts to tilt, the spinning wheels
generate a gyroscopic torque that tends to oppose the tilt or redirect it.
2. Self-correcting steering: When the bicycle tilts to one side, the front
wheel automatically turns in that direction (due to the geometry of the
fork and steering axis). This causes the bicycle to move its base under
the center of mass, restoring balance.

Classification of orbits by eccentricity:
Based on the value of eccentricity, orbits can be categorized as:
Circular Orbit (e = 0): The simplest and most stable form, where
the distance between the orbiting body and the central mass remains
constant.
Elliptical Orbit (0 < e < 1): The most common type for planets,
moons, and artificial satellites. The central mass lies at one focus
of the ellipse.
Parabolic Trajectory (e = 1): Represents an open trajectory where
an object moves at exactly the escape velocity and never returns.
Hyperbolic Trajectory (e > 1): Indicates an open path where the
object passes near the central body once and escapes permanently,
as seen with some interstellar visitors such as Oumuamua or 3I-
Atlas.

high eccentricity can have differences in speed as they orbit. When
they are closer to their sun or parent body, they move faster.

can affect climate on planets. A planet with a high eccentricity
might experience extreme seasons, making it much hotter or colder

Ans. Torque is the measure of the force that can cause an object to
rotate about an axis. Torque, also known as the moment of force, is
the rotational analog of force. In the same way that a force is
necessary to change a particle or object's state of motion, a torque
is necessary to change a particle or object's state of rotation. In
vector form it is defined as
𝜏⃗= 𝑟⃗ x 𝐹⃗⃗⃗⃗
Where 𝜏⃗ is the torque vector, 𝐹⃗ is the force vector and 𝑟⃗ is the
position vector of the point where the force is applied relative to
the axis of rotation. The direction of the torque is always
perpendicular to the plane in which it is applied, hence, for two
dimensional rotation this can be simplified to
τ = r.F.sin θ
where r is the distance between the axis of rotation and the point at
which the force is applied, F is the magnitude of the force and θ is
the angle between the position vector of the point at which the force
is applied (relative to the axis of rotation) and the direction in which
the force is applied (relative to the axis of rotation) and the direction
in which the force is applied.

a.   Write short notes on geostationary, medium and low earth orbits.   [10]
What is the role of transfer orbits?
Ans. Geostationary orbits: A geostationary orbit is a specific type
of circular orbit around Earth, positioned approximately 35,786
kilometers (22,236 miles) above the equator. In this orbit, a satellite
travels at the same rotational speed as the Earth, allowing it to
appear stationary from the perspective of an observer on the
ground. This synchronized motion is essential for various
applications, particularly in communication technology, where
satellites relay television, radio broadcasts, and data across the
globe.
3 marks
Medium Earth orbits: Medium Earth orbit (MEO) covers a wide
range of altitudes anywhere between LEO – usually over the top of
the Van Allen belts – and GEO. Like LEO, satellites in MEO do
not need to follow specific paths around Earth and the orbit is used
by a variety of satellites for many different purposes.
MEO is very commonly used by navigation satellites, like the
European Galileo system (pictured). Galileo provides navigation
services across the world, helping with everything from tracking
large jumbo jets to giving directions on your smartphone. Galileo
consists of a constellation of satellites that provide simultaneous
coverage over large areas of the world.
3 marks
Low earth orbits: Low Earth Orbit (LEO) is an orbital region
around Earth, typically ranging from 160 to 2,000 kilometers in
altitude, characterized by high speeds and short orbital periods of
roughly 90 to 120 minutes. Due to their proximity to Earth, LEO
satellites are ideal for imaging, telecommunications. The
International Space Station is located in low earth orbit.
3 marks
Transfer orbits are a special kind of orbit used to get from one orbit
to another. When satellites are launched from Earth by rockets like
Ariane 6, they are not always placed directly into their final orbit.
Instead, they are often put into an initial transfer orbit where, using
energy from onboard motors, the satellite or spacecraft can move
from one orbit to another.
