Additivity of charges
If a system contains two point charges q1 and q2 , the total charge of the system is obtained
simply by adding algebraically q1 and q2 , i.e., charges add up like real numbers or they are
scalars like the mass of a body.
If a system contains n charges q1 , q2 , q3 , …, qn , then the total charge of the system is q1 +
q2 + q3 + … + qn .
Charge is Quantized
q = ± ne, Here n = 1, 2, 3 and e = 1.6 × 10–19 coulomb.
Quantization of charge
Experimentally it is established that all free charges are integral multiples of a basic unit of
charge denoted by e.
Thus charge q on a body is always given by q = ne where n is any integer, positive or negative.
This basic unit of charge is the charge that an electron or proton carries.
By convention, the charge on an electron is taken to be negative; therefore charge on an electron
is written as –e and that on a proton as +e.
The fact that electric charge is always an integral multiple of e is termed as quantization of
charge
Charge is conserved
It is found that the total charge of the isolated system is always conserved.
It is not possible to create or destroy net charge carried by any isolated system.

charge
