n> and a coin of serial number s is created by the user.

The problem is that we want the bank to sign the serial number s by encrypting it
with <d, n>* but we do not want the bank to know s.
Example of Asymmetric Cryptography:
If Person A wants to send a message securely to Person B:
Person A encrypts the message using Person B’s Public Key.

To use e-cash, a user needs to install a special electronic wallet in his computer.
Firstly, the user creates a coin of the appropriate value by the wallet software.
By using the blind signature method, the coin is signed by the bank without
knowing its serial number.
Furthermore, the corresponding bank account is debited accordingly.
When the user decides to purchase something on the internet with e-cash, the
signed e-cash coin(s) is/are passed to the merchant wallet software.
The merchant then deposits the coins.
As it is possible that an e-cash coin may be further duplicated, the bank needs a
mechanism to make sure that multiple spending cannot occur.
To do this, the bank uses a database to store the serial numbers of all deposited
coins.
When a coin is deposited in the bank, the bank checks whether its serial number
is already in the database.
If not, the deposit is approved and the appropriate bank account is credited
accordingly, otherwise the deposit is rejected.
To keep the database at a reasonable size, each coin has a validity period. Outdated
coins are deleted from the database.
Therefore, each user has to deposit an e-cash coin to the bank within its validity
period, otherwise the coin will become useless.
After verifying that the coin is not outdated, is valid, and has not been deposited
before, the merchant will be notified and its account will be credited accordingly.
Finally the merchant can ship the goods or provide the service accordingly.
Note that e-cash ensures that the bank does not know the original owner of the
coin, i.e. it supports anonymous payment.
Of course, the bank knows the payee because he needs to make deposits in his
account.

CO-3,   B   What is a web service? How does a web service work?   10
SO-6   Companies are using the Internet to connect specific software applications at one
BL-2   organization directly to software applications at other organizations.
Web services is defined as software systems that support interoperable machine-
to-machine interaction over a network.
In other words, a Web service is a set of software and technologies that allow
computers to use the Web to interact with each other directly, without human
operators directing the specific interactions.
A general name for the ways programs interconnect with each other is application
program interface (API). When the interaction is done over the Web, the
techniques are called Web APIs.
Working:
A key element of the Web services approach is that programmers can write
software that accesses these units of business application logic without knowing
the details of how each unit is implemented.
Web services can be mixed and matched with other Web services to execute a
complex business transaction. Thus, Web services allow programs written in
different languages on different platforms to communicate with each other and
accomplish transaction processing and other business tasks.
The common format of this machine-to-machine communication was originally
HTML; however, most Web services implementations now use XML.
The first Web services were nothing more than sources of information.
The first widely used approach to Web services was Simple Object Access
Protocol (SOAP), which is a message-passing protocol that defines how to send
marked-up data from one software application to another across a network.
REST and RESTful Design
Representational State Transfer (REST), describes the way the Web uses
networking architecture to identify and locate Web pages and the elements
(graphics, audio clips, and so on) that make up those Web pages.
Web services that are built on the REST model are said to use RESTful design and
are sometimes called RESTful applications.
A RESTful application transfers structured information from one Web location to
another.
This structured information can be any type of media, but it is most often an XML-
tagged data set.
RESTful applications can also transfer HTML- or XHTML-tagged data. The Web
service is made available at a specific and can be accessed by any other computer
that has a Web browser function.

Companies can become more profitable than the average performer in their

One way of achieving cost and price advantages is through strategic positioning.
Strategic positioning means that a company is doing things differently from its
competitors, in a way that delivers a unique type of value to its customers [Porter
2001].
Porter suggests six fundamental principles that companies need to follow in order
to establish and maintain a distinctive strategic position:
1. A company must start with the right goal: superior long-term return on
investment. Economic value is created when customers are willing to purchase a
product or service that exceeds the cost of producing it.
2. A company's strategy must enable it to deliver a value proposition, or set of

To use e-cash, a user needs to install a special electronic wallet in his computer.

knowing its serial number.

signed e-cash coin(s) is/are passed to the merchant wallet software.

coins.
When a coin is deposited in the bank, the bank checks whether its serial number

accordingly, otherwise the deposit is rejected.

coins are deleted from the database.

period, otherwise the coin will become useless.
After verifying that the coin is not outdated, is valid, and has not been deposited
before, the merchant will be notified and its account will be credited accordingly.
Finally the merchant can ship the goods or provide the service accordingly.

coin, i.e. it supports anonymous payment.

SO-6   Companies are using the Internet to connect specific software applications at one
BL-2   organization directly to software applications at other organizations.
Web services is defined as software systems that support interoperable machine-
to-machine interaction over a network.
In other words, a Web service is a set of software and technologies that allow
computers to use the Web to interact with each other directly, without human
operators directing the specific interactions.
A general name for the ways programs interconnect with each other is application
program interface (API). When the interaction is done over the Web, the
techniques are called Web APIs.
Working:
A key element of the Web services approach is that programmers can write
software that accesses these units of business application logic without knowing
the details of how each unit is implemented.
Web services can be mixed and matched with other Web services to execute a
complex business transaction. Thus, Web services allow programs written in
different languages on different platforms to communicate with each other and
accomplish transaction processing and other business tasks.
The common format of this machine-to-machine communication was originally
HTML; however, most Web services implementations now use XML.
The first Web services were nothing more than sources of information.
The first widely used approach to Web services was Simple Object Access
Protocol (SOAP), which is a message-passing protocol that defines how to send
marked-up data from one software application to another across a network.
REST and RESTful Design
Representational State Transfer (REST), describes the way the Web uses
networking architecture to identify and locate Web pages and the elements
(graphics, audio clips, and so on) that make up those Web pages.
Web services that are built on the REST model are said to use RESTful design and
are sometimes called RESTful applications.
A RESTful application transfers structured information from one Web location to
another.
This structured information can be any type of media, but it is most often an XML-
tagged data set.
RESTful applications can also transfer HTML- or XHTML-tagged data. The Web
service is made available at a specific and can be accessed by any other computer
that has a Web browser function.

1. A company must start with the right goal: superior long-term return on
investment. Economic value is created when customers are willing to purchase a
product or service that exceeds the cost of producing it.
2. A company's strategy must enable it to deliver a value proposition, or set of
benefits, different from those that its competitors offer. Strategy defines a way of
competing that delivers unique value for a particular set of customers.
3. Company strategy needs to be reflected in a distinctive value chain. To establish
a sustainable competitive advantage, a company must perform in ways that are
different from its rivals or perform similar activities in different ways.
4. Robust company strategies involve trade-offs. A company may have to abandon
or forgo some product features, services, or activities in order to be unique at
others
5. Company strategy defines how all the elements of what a company does fit
together. A strategy involves making choices throughout the value chain that are
interdependent; all a company's activities must be mutually reinforcing.
6. Company strategy involves continuity of direction. A company must define a
distinctive value proposition to which it will abide even if that means forgoing

The theory of competitive strategy:
The structural attractiveness of a firm is determined by five underlying forces of
competition [Porter 1997]:
1) The bargaining power of customers;
2) The bargaining power of suppliers;
3) The barriers to entry for new competitors;
4) The threat of new substitute products or services;

The Resource based review:
According to this theory of economic development, innovation is the source of value
creation [Schumpeter 1939].
Several sources of innovation are identified including the introduction of new goods or
new production methods, the creation of new markets, the discovery of new supply
sources, and the reorganization of industries.
Innovation emphasizes the importance of technology and considers novel combinations
of resources as the foundations of new products and production methods.

According to this view, a firm can build a strategic position by picking the right

Transaction Cost Economics:
Transaction cost economics attempt to explain firms' choices between internalizing and

According to transaction cost theory [Williamson 1975], exchanges with external firms

transactions.
These include search costs to find the right trading partner, as well as negotiation,
monitoring, settlement, and various after sales services associated with the exchange.
Transaction cost economics theory provides a theoretical framework for discussion of

hierarchy).
The central question addressed by transaction cost economics is why firms internalize
transactions that might otherwise be conducted in markets. Thus, the two key issues

Which activities should a firm keep within its boundaries, and which activities should

In which way should a firm manage its relationship with its customers, suppliers and

The theory of competitive strategy:
The structural attractiveness of a firm is determined by five underlying forces of
competition [Porter 1997]:
1) The bargaining power of customers;
2) The bargaining power of suppliers;
3) The barriers to entry for new competitors;
4) The threat of new substitute products or services;
5) The competition among existing firms in the industry.
In combination, these forces determine how the economic value created by any product,
service technology or way of competing is divided between companies in an industry as
well as customers, suppliers, distributors, substitutes, and potential new entrants [Porter
1997].
The Resource based review:
According to this theory of economic development, innovation is the source of value
creation [Schumpeter 1939].
Several sources of innovation are identified including the introduction of new goods or
new production methods, the creation of new markets, the discovery of new supply
sources, and the reorganization of industries.
Innovation emphasizes the importance of technology and considers novel combinations
of resources as the foundations of new products and production methods.
These, in turn, lead to the transformation of markets and industries, and hence to
economic development.
********** 7 **********
--- page 8 ---
Barney's resource-based view (RBV), regards a firm as a collection of resources and
capabilities [Barney 1991; Amit 2001].
The resource-based view looks at available resources first to see how a position in the
business environment can be acquired with them.
According to this view, a firm can build a strategic position by picking the right
resources and building competencies that are unique and difficult to imitate.
Transaction Cost Economics:
Transaction cost economics attempt to explain firms' choices between internalizing and
buying goods and services from the market.
According to transaction cost theory [Williamson 1975], exchanges with external firms
entail a variety of coordination costs associated with various aspects of inter-firm
transactions.
These include search costs to find the right trading partner, as well as negotiation,
monitoring, settlement, and various after sales services associated with the exchange.
Transaction cost economics theory provides a theoretical framework for discussion of
market structures, and addresses the question why necessary resources are obtained
either through the market (external procurement) or through internal production (the
hierarchy).
The central question addressed by transaction cost economics is why firms internalize
transactions that might otherwise be conducted in markets. Thus, the two key issues
concerning firms are:
Which activities should a firm keep within its boundaries, and which activities should
it outsource?
In which way should a firm manage its relationship with its customers, suppliers and
other business partners?
