a. Any 8 points for 1 mark each   8
Meaning:
Advances from customers refer to payments received by a company before the delivery of
goods or rendering of services, especially in case of customized or made-to-order products.
These are shown as current liabilities in the Balance Sheet until the order is fulfilled.
It Serves as a Source of Short-Term Finance
1. Pre-financing of Production:
The advance amount can be used to purchase raw materials, pay wages, and meet
manufacturing expenses without arranging external finance.
2. Working Capital Support:
It reduces the need for bank overdrafts or short-term borrowings, thereby supporting day-to-
day operational liquidity.
3. Cost-Free or Low-Cost Finance:
Generally, no interest is payable on customer advances, making it an economical source of
finance.
4. Risk Sharing:
The customer shares part of the financial risk by committing funds before production begins.
Advantages (Company’s Perspective)
1. No Interest Cost:
Unlike bank loans or trade credit, advances usually do not carry explicit interest cost.
2. Improved Liquidity Position:
Immediate inflow of cash strengthens working capital and reduces cash flow pressure.
3. Reduced Dependence on External Borrowings:
Lowers financial leverage and improves solvency ratios.
4. Assured Demand:
Receipt of advance confirms the order, reducing uncertainty of sales.
5. Better Production Planning:
Helps in scheduling production efficiently based on confirmed orders.
Limitations (Company’s Perspective)
1. Obligation to Deliver:
Creates a binding liability. Failure to deliver on time may lead to refund claims, penalties, or
loss of reputation.
2. Restricted Use:
Funds are tied to specific orders and cannot always be freely used for other purposes.
3. Customer Pressure:
Customers who pay advances may demand priority production or price concessions.
