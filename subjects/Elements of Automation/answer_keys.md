c) Comparison between Micro (Compact) PLCs and Modular PLCs based on structure, number
of processors, network connections, I/Os, capabilities and examples. - 05 M
Micro (Compact) PLC has all essential components such as the CPU, power supply, and input/output
interfaces housed within a single compact unit. It generally operates with a single processor and has
limited I/O capacity, making it suitable for small-scale or standalone automation tasks. Its network
connectivity is minimal, often restricted to basic communication ports, and its capabilities are confined
to simple control functions like timing, counting, and basic logic operations. Typical examples include
Siemens LOGO, Allen-Bradley MicroLogix, and Omron CP1E.
In contrast, a Modular PLC consists of separate, interchangeable modules for the CPU, power supply,
and I/O, allowing for greater flexibility and scalability. It may include multiple processors and supports
advanced networking such as Ethernet, Profibus, or Modbus. Modular PLCs can handle a large number
of I/Os, enabling control of complex industrial processes. They are capable of advanced data handling,
motion control, and process automation tasks. Examples include Siemens S7-300, Bosch-Rexroth L25,
Allen-Bradley ControlLogix, and Mitsubishi Q Series.

Discussion about applications of a SCARA robot. – 02 M
A SCARA (Selective Compliance Assembly Robot Arm) robot is widely used in industrial automation
for tasks that require high speed, precision, and repeatability within a horizontal plane. Its design
allows selective compliance in the X–Y direction while maintaining rigidity in the Z-axis, making it
ideal for assembly, pick-and-place, and material handling operations. SCARA robots are extensively
applied in electronic component assembly, such as inserting parts on printed circuit boards, packaging
and labeling, screw driving, machine loading and unloading, and small part transfer in manufacturing
lines. In the pharmaceutical and food industries, they are used for sorting, filling, and packaging
processes that demand accuracy and hygiene.

Explanation about the components used in configuration of an automation system. – 04 M
An automation system consists of following elements.
Process- Complex assembly of phenomenon that refers to some manufacturing sequence. It utilizes
the resources to produce certain product. Many variables may be involved in such a process, some of
which have to be controlled. The variable which is required to be regulated or maintained constant is
called as controlled variable.
Sensors (Feedback Element)- It is the eye of the system. It Produces output that represents the status
of the controlled variable. The output of the sensor is called as feedback. Examples- RTD, Bellows,
Thermistor etc.
Error Detector - Compares set point (reference signal) with the feedback signal. Produces error signal
for the controller.
Error = Set point – feedback signal.
Examples of devices used as error detector are— OP-AMP based differential amplifier for analog
signals and comparison softwares for digital signals.
Controller - The controller is the brain of the system. It receives error signal and develops output
called as control signal that causes the controlled variable to become equal to the set point value.
Examples of controller used in automation are PLC, Microprocessor and OP-AMP based controller.
Actuators - Actuation involves a physical acting on the process, such as the ejection of a work piece
from a conveyor system initiated by a sensor. Actuators are usually electrical, mechanical, fluid power
based. They transform the controller outputs into mechanical outputs such as force, angle, and position.
Actuators can be classified into three general groups.
• Electromagnetic actuators, (AC and DC electrical motors, Stepper motors, Electromagnets)
• Fluid power actuators, (Hydraulics, Pneumatics)
• Unconventional actuators (Piezoelectric, Magnetostrictive, Memory metal)
Final Control elements- The last system element that responds quantitatively to a control signal and
performs the actual control action. Examples of final control element include valves and diaphragms.
The final control elements are operated by the actuator.

Discussion about the operation of each stage in the IoT architectural diagram. – 03 M
Stage 1: Sensor/Actuator Layer: This layer performs following tasks -
• Sensor Deployment: In this stage, a variety of sensors and actuators are deployed in the
physical environment. Sensors are responsible for collecting data from the real world, which
can include environmental conditions (e.g., temperature, humidity), physical parameters (e.g.,
motion, proximity), or other relevant information (e.g., GPS location). Actuators, on the other
hand, can perform actions based on data or commands received, such as controlling motors,
switches, or valves.
• Data Collection: Sensors continuously or periodically collect data and transmit it to the next
stage, which can be an internet gateway or a local data acquisition system.
• Device Management: Managing IoT devices is crucial. Tasks in this stage may include device
registration, authentication, firmware updates, and monitoring to ensure devices are operating
as expected.
Stage 2: Internet Gateway/Data Acquisition System: The tasks performed by this stage are –
• Data Aggregation: This stage involves the aggregation of data from various sensors and
actuators. Data from multiple devices is collected and prepared for further processing.
• Data Transmission: Data is transmitted from the edge sensors and actuators to an internet
gateway or data acquisition system. These systems are responsible for forwarding the data to
the next stage, which may be edge IT or a centralized data center or cloud.
Stage 3: Edge IT: The tasks performed by this stage are –
• Local Data Processing: Edge IT systems are located closer to the data source, and they may
perform local data processing tasks. This can include initial data validation, aggregation, and
preprocessing before transmitting the data to a more centralized location.
• Edge Analytics: In some cases, edge IT systems can perform real-time analytics and decision-
making based on local data, reducing latency and the need to transmit all data to a centralized
location.
Stage 4: Data Center/Cloud: The tasks performed by this stage are –
• Data Storage: Processed data from edge IT or directly from the internet gateway is stored in
data centers or cloud-based storage systems. The choice of storage technology depends on
factors like data volume, access patterns, and retrieval speed.
• Data Processing and Analytics: Data is further processed and analyzed in data centers or cloud
environments. This can involve more advanced analytics, machine learning, and artificial
intelligence to derive insights and predictions from the data.

b) Explanation about the concept of the degrees of freedom for the robotic manipulator with
suitable example. -05 M
The concept of degrees-of-freedom, often abbreviated as DOF, is important for defining the
possible positions and orientations a robot can reach. An object in the physical world can have up to
six degrees of freedom, namely forward/backward, sideways, and up/down as well as rotations around
those axes. These rotations are known as pitch, yaw and roll.
Every robot assumes a position in the real world that can be described by its position (x, y and z) and
orientation (pitch, yaw and roll) along the three major axes of a Cartesian Coordinate system.
Explanation about the concept of the frame of reference for the robotic manipulator with
suitable example. -05 M
Defining all three position axes and orientations might be cumbersome. What level of detail we care
about, where the origin of this coordinate system is, and even what kind of coordinate system we
choose, depends on the specific application. For example, a simple mobile robot would typically
require a representation with respect to a room, a building, or the earth’s coordinate system (given by
the longitude and latitude of each point on the earth), whereas a static manipulator usually has the
origin of its coordinate system at its base. More complicated systems, such as mobile manipulators or
multi-legged robots, make life much easier by defining multiple coordinate systems, e.g. one for each
leg and one that describes the position of the robot in the world frame. These local coordinate systems
are known as Frames of Reference.
Consider following example in it a robot located at the origin of x' , y' and z' might plan its motions in
its own reference frame, which can then be expressed in the coordinate system x, y and z by performing
a translation and a rotation.

b) Discusion about the configuration, features and applications of mobile robots. – 10 M
A mobile robot is a machine controlled by software that use sensors and other technology to identify
its surroundings and move around its environment. Mobile robots function using a combination of
artificial intelligence (AI) and physical robotic elements, such as wheels, tracks and legs. Mobile robots
are becoming increasingly popular across different business sectors. They are used to assist with work
processes and even accomplish tasks that are impossible or dangerous for human workers.
Features of a mobile robot
Each mobile robot will incorporate different features that optimize the system to meet a specific goal
or perform a certain task. However, industrial mobile robot systems, perhaps the most commonly used
today, possess several core features that should always be present. These features are:
•   wireless communication
•   integrated safety
•   fleet simulation software
•   fleet management software
•   integration with the company's supervisory software
Examples of different environment mobile robots include:
•   Polar robots are designed to traverse icy, uneven environments.
•   Aerial robots, also known as unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) or drones, which fly through
the air.
• Land or home robots, or unmanned ground vehicles (UGVs), that navigate on dry land or
within houses.
• Underwater robots, or autonomous underwater vehicles (AUVs), that can direct themselves
and travel through water.
•   Delivery and transportation mobile robots that are designed to move materials and supplies
around a work environment.
There are also two main types of mobile robots: autonomous and non-autonomous, or guided, mobile robots.
The basic functions of a mobile robot include the ability to move and explore, transport payloads, or revenue
producing cargo, and complete complex tasks using an onboard system, like robotic arms. While the industrial
use of mobile robots is popular, especially in warehouses and distribution centres, its functions can also be
applied to the medicine, surgery, personal assistance and security. Ocean and space exploration and navigation
are also amongst the most common uses of mobile robots.

Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS)
Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS) are integrated systems that combine physical processes with
computation, communication, and control. In Industry 4.0, CPS connects machines, sensors,
and software to monitor and control physical operations in real time.
In a CPS, physical components (like machines or production systems) are equipped with
sensors that collect data. This data is transmitted to computational systems where it is
analyzed, and appropriate control actions are sent back to the physical system. Thus, there is
continuous interaction between the physical world and the digital (cyber) world.
CPS enables automation, real-time monitoring, decentralized decision-making, and improved
efficiency in manufacturing systems. It is the foundation of smart factories where machines
can communicate with each other and operate intelligently with minimal human
intervention.

Digital Twins
A Digital Twin is a virtual or digital replica of a physical object, system, or process. In Industry
4.0, it is used to simulate, monitor, and optimize real-world operations.
The digital twin receives real-time data from sensors installed on the physical system and
updates itself continuously. This allows engineers to analyze performance, predict failures,
and test different scenarios without affecting the actual system.
Digital twins help in predictive maintenance, design improvement, process optimization, and
decision-making. By using simulations, industries can reduce risks, save costs, and improve
product quality before implementing changes in the real system.

A meter-in circuit is a type of flow control circuit used in hydraulic or pneumatic systems
where the flow of fluid entering the actuator (cylinder or motor) is controlled. This is
achieved by placing a flow control valve at the inlet side of the actuator.

• When the DCV is actuated, fluid flows from the pump toward the actuator.
• The flow control valve restricts the incoming fluid, thereby controlling the speed of

•   During return stroke, the check valve allows free flow, so the actuator can return
quickly without restriction.
•   Thus, speed control is achieved during extension (or inlet stroke).   [10]

Diagram 3 marks explanation 7 marks
Program scan cycle of PLC includes following steps:-
Input Scan: The PLC starts by scanning all of its input devices. These devices can include
sensors, switches, and other input signals. The PLC reads the state of these devices to
determine the current status of the controlled process.
Program Execution: After scanning the input devices, the PLC executes the user-programmed
logic. The logic is typically written using ladder logic or other programming languages
specific to PLCs. This logic processes the input data and makes decisions based on it.
Diagnostics and Communication in the PLC scan cycle is the step where the PLC checks the
health and status of its system and exchanges data with other devices. During diagnostics,

Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS)
Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS) are integrated systems that combine physical processes with
computation, communication, and control. In Industry 4.0, CPS connects machines, sensors,
and software to monitor and control physical operations in real time.
In a CPS, physical components (like machines or production systems) are equipped with
sensors that collect data. This data is transmitted to computational systems where it is
analyzed, and appropriate control actions are sent back to the physical system. Thus, there is
continuous interaction between the physical world and the digital (cyber) world.
CPS enables automation, real-time monitoring, decentralized decision-making, and improved
efficiency in manufacturing systems. It is the foundation of smart factories where machines
can communicate with each other and operate intelligently with minimal human
intervention.
[5]

Digital Twins
A Digital Twin is a virtual or digital replica of a physical object, system, or process. In Industry
4.0, it is used to simulate, monitor, and optimize real-world operations.
The digital twin receives real-time data from sensors installed on the physical system and
updates itself continuously. This allows engineers to analyze performance, predict failures,
and test different scenarios without affecting the actual system.
Digital twins help in predictive maintenance, design improvement, process optimization, and
decision-making. By using simulations, industries can reduce risks, save costs, and improve
product quality before implementing changes in the real system.

A meter-in circuit is a type of flow control circuit used in hydraulic or pneumatic systems
where the flow of fluid entering the actuator (cylinder or motor) is controlled. This is
achieved by placing a flow control valve at the inlet side of the actuator.
Working Principle
• When the DCV is actuated, fluid flows from the pump toward the actuator.
• The flow control valve restricts the incoming fluid, thereby controlling the speed of
the actuator.
•   During return stroke, the check valve allows free flow, so the actuator can return
quickly without restriction.
•   Thus, speed control is achieved during extension (or inlet stroke).   [10]

a)Explain PLC (Programmable Logic Controller)Program Scan Cycle
Diagram 3 marks explanation 7 marks
Program scan cycle of PLC includes following steps:-
Input Scan: The PLC starts by scanning all of its input devices. These devices can include
sensors, switches, and other input signals. The PLC reads the state of these devices to
determine the current status of the controlled process.
Program Execution: After scanning the input devices, the PLC executes the user-programmed
logic. The logic is typically written using ladder logic or other programming languages
specific to PLCs. This logic processes the input data and makes decisions based on it.
Diagnostics and Communication in the PLC scan cycle is the step where the PLC checks the
health and status of its system and exchanges data with other devices. During diagnostics,
the PLC verifies that the CPU, memory, input/output modules, and power supply are working
correctly and detects any faults or errors in the program or hardware. During
communication, the PLC sends and receives data from external devices such as HMI, SCADA
systems, other PLCs, or computers through industrial networks. This step ensures reliable
operation, allows monitoring of the system, and helps operators control and supervise the
process effectively.
Output Update: Once the program has been executed, the PLC updates the output devices
based on the logic's results. Output devices can include motors, solenoids, valves, and other
actuators. The PLC sets the outputs to the appropriate state to control the process.   [10]

b)Explain Capacitive Proximity Sensor with construction and working with neat sketch.
Sketch 4 marks, explanation 6 marks
Capacitive sensors can detect both metallic and non-metallic objects. They operate based on
changes in capacitance when an object enters the sensor's detection range. Non-metallic
objects like plastic, glass, and liquids can also be detected.   [10]
********** 5 **********
--- page 6 ---
The capacitive proximity sensor consist a high-frequency oscillator along with a sensing
surface formed by two metal electrodes. When an object comes near the sensing surface, it
enters the electrostatic field of the electrodes and changes the capacitance of the oscillator.
As a result, the oscillator circuit starts oscillating and changes the output state of the sensor
when it reaches certain amplitude. As the object moves away from the sensor, the
oscillator’s amplitude decreases, switching the sensor back to its initial state.
A typical sensing range for capacitive proximity sensors is from a few millimeters up to about
1 inch. (or 25 mm), and some sensors have an extended range up to 2 inch. Where capacitive
sensors really excel, however, is in applications where they must detect objects through
some kind of material such as a bag, bin, or box. They can tune out non-metallic containers
and can be tuned or set to detect different levels of liquids or solid materials.

a)Describe Human Machine interface for robotics. Definition 3 marks, Explanation 7 marks
A Human-Machine Interface (HMI) is a user interface or dashboard that connects a person to
a machine, system, or device. While the term can technically be applied to any screen that
allows a user to interact with a device, HMI is most commonly used in the context of an
industrial process.
HMIs are similar in some ways to Graphical User Interfaces (GUI) but they are not
synonymous; GUIs are often leveraged within HMIs for visualization capabilities.
HMIs communicate with Programmable Logic Controllers (PLCs) and input/output sensors to
get and display information for users to view. HMI screens can be used for a single function,
like monitoring and tracking, or for performing more sophisticated operations, like switching
machines off or increasing production speed, depending on how they are implemented.
HMIs are used to optimize an industrial process by digitizing and centralizing data for a
viewer. By leveraging HMI, operators can see important information displayed in graphs,
charts, or digital dashboards, view and manage alarms, and connect with SCADA, ERP, and
MES systems, all through one console.
There are many tools available for HMI programming, depending on the project's type,
scope, and complexity. Visual Studio is a comprehensive IDE (Integrated
Development Environment) that supports multiple languages, frameworks, and platforms for
HMI development. It boasts features such as code editing, debugging, testing, version
control, and deployment tools. Ignition is a web-based HMI platform that allows users to
create, manage, and deploy HMI projects across multiple devices and locations. It supports
OPC UA, MQTT, SQL, and other protocols for data integration. Furthermore, it has a drag-
and-drop interface builder, scripting engine, and reporting module. WinCC is a scalable HMI
software that can run on various operating systems and devices from PCs to embedded
systems. It offers support for multiple communication standards such as TCP/IP, Modbus,
and Profibus. It also has a graphical editor, scripting language, and simulation mode.   [10]

Que 7:-Write a Short note on
a)Hydraulic Pump and Hydraulic motor (2.5 mark each)
Hydraulic pumps convert electrical energy into fluid pressure by using an electric motor to
drive the pump. They are necessary for all hydraulic drives. The fluid pressure is then
delivered by hydraulic fluid to cylinders and actuators and hydraulic motors at the required
pressure level and volume.
Hydraulic motors are powered by pressurized hydraulic fluid and transfer rotational kinetic
energy to mechanical devices. Hydraulic motors, when powered by a mechanical source, can
rotate in the reverse direction, and act as a pump. Hydraulic rotary actuators use pressurized
fluid to rotate mechanical components.
[5]
b) Indexing in motion control. (Explanation 5 marks)
An index will be an angle (rotary) move or a distance (linear) move. The move has a start and
a stop, at which point the machine will perform the next task, like filling a pouch or opening
an elevator door.
Indexing in reference to motion is moving (or being moved) into a new position or location
quickly and easily but also precisely.
Positioning a workpiece at a precise angle or interval of rotation for a machining operation is
called indexing. In addition to that most classic sense of the word, the swapping of one part
for another, or other controlled movements, are also sometimes referred to as indexing,
even if rotation is not the focus.
Indexes are pre-configured moves stored in digital servo drives. They can be either absolute
or relative depending on the preference for the motion application. The move profiles,
including acceleration, velocity, and deceleration, can be individually defined and stored
together in the drive’s internal memory. This spares the controller from having to create a
target move multiple times through a command signal each time it attempts to execute it;
instead, it can simply send a signal that triggers the pre-defined index.   [5]

d) Feedback devices in robots. (Explanation 5 marks)
These are a class of devices used to monitor an operation or process and then verify whether
the operation or process has occurred correctly. Referring to a human body analogy, they act
like sensory organs that collect information and send it to the brain (controller) for decision-
making and control.
In a robotic system, feedback devices are installed to sense important parameters such as
the position, speed, and direction of movement of various links and joints. This information
is continuously transmitted to the controller, allowing it to compare the actual performance
with the desired output and make necessary corrections. This forms a closed-loop control
system, which improves accuracy and reliability.
Common feedback devices include simple limit switches, which detect end positions, and
more advanced position-measuring devices such as encoders, potentiometers, resolvers, and
tachometers. Encoders provide precise digital position data, potentiometers give analog
position signals, resolvers are used for robust angular measurement in harsh environments,
and tachometers measure rotational speed.
Depending on the type of device used, the feedback signal can be digital or analog. Digital
signals are typically more noise-resistant and suitable for modern control systems, while
analog signals provide continuous variation and are useful for fine control. Overall, feedback
devices are essential for ensuring accurate motion control, safety, and efficient operation of
robotic systems.   [5]

Filter: The filter element inside the FRL unit is designed to remove contaminants such as dust, dirt, oil,
water, and other particles from the compressed air. It typically consists of a porous material that allows
air to pass through while capturing and trapping the contaminants. The filter is placed in the airflow

air reaches the pneumatic components downstream.
Regulator: The regulator in the FRL unit is responsible for maintaining a consistent and controlled air
pressure within the pneumatic system. It typically has an adjustable spring-loaded diaphragm
mechanism. The regulator monitors the downstream air pressure and adjusts the valve opening
accordingly to maintain the desired pressure level. By adjusting the regulator's setting, the operator can
control the output pressure to suit the specific requirements of the pneumatic system.

lubricating oil into the compressed air stream. This helps lubricate the moving components within the
pneumatic system, reducing friction and wear. The lubricator usually contains a reservoir for the
lubricating oil and a metering system that allows for precise control of the oil flow rate into the air

An ultrasonic proximity sensor is a non-contact sensing device that detects objects by emitting high-
frequency sound waves and measuring the time taken for the echo to return. It consists of a transmitter
that generates ultrasonic waves and a receiver that detects the reflected waves after they bounce off an
object.
When an object enters the sensor’s detection range, the emitted sound waves reflect off its surface and
return to the receiver. The control circuit processes the time delay between transmission and reception
to determine the object's proximity. If the detected object is within a predefined distance, the sensor
generates an output signal to trigger an action, such as stopping a robotic arm, activating an alarm, or
opening an automatic door. These sensors are widely used in robotics, industrial automation,
automotive parking systems, and object detection applications due to their ability to work in various
environmental conditions and detect objects regardless of color or material composition.

Repeatability measures the variance in position when the robot attempts to reach the same point
multiple times. A highly repeatable robot may still have poor accuracy if it consistently deviates from
the intended target by a fixed offset.
A welding robot must repeatedly reach the same welding spot within micrometer precision. If its
variance is within ±0.1 mm, the repeatability is high.

sequential; they do not work on a part at the same time.
Cooperation: Robot and human work on the same part at the same time, with both in motion.

Explanation of function of filter, regulator and lubricator. – 02 M
Filter: The filter element inside the FRL unit is designed to remove contaminants such as dust, dirt, oil,
water, and other particles from the compressed air. It typically consists of a porous material that allows
air to pass through while capturing and trapping the contaminants. The filter is placed in the airflow
path, and as the air flows through, the contaminants are trapped in the filter element, ensuring cleaner
air reaches the pneumatic components downstream.
Regulator: The regulator in the FRL unit is responsible for maintaining a consistent and controlled air
pressure within the pneumatic system. It typically has an adjustable spring-loaded diaphragm
mechanism. The regulator monitors the downstream air pressure and adjusts the valve opening
accordingly to maintain the desired pressure level. By adjusting the regulator's setting, the operator can
control the output pressure to suit the specific requirements of the pneumatic system.
Lubricator: The lubricator component of the FRL unit is used to introduce a controlled amount of
lubricating oil into the compressed air stream. This helps lubricate the moving components within the

pneumatic system, reducing friction and wear. The lubricator usually contains a reservoir for the
lubricating oil and a metering system that allows for precise control of the oil flow rate into the air
stream. This ensures that the components receive adequate lubrication without excessive oil
consumption.

An ultrasonic proximity sensor is a non-contact sensing device that detects objects by emitting high-
frequency sound waves and measuring the time taken for the echo to return. It consists of a transmitter
that generates ultrasonic waves and a receiver that detects the reflected waves after they bounce off an
object.
When an object enters the sensor’s detection range, the emitted sound waves reflect off its surface and
return to the receiver. The control circuit processes the time delay between transmission and reception
to determine the object's proximity. If the detected object is within a predefined distance, the sensor
generates an output signal to trigger an action, such as stopping a robotic arm, activating an alarm, or
opening an automatic door. These sensors are widely used in robotics, industrial automation,
automotive parking systems, and object detection applications due to their ability to work in various
environmental conditions and detect objects regardless of color or material composition.
b) Explanation about repeatability of a robotic manipulator with relevant example. - 03 M
Repeatability measures the variance in position when the robot attempts to reach the same point
multiple times. A highly repeatable robot may still have poor accuracy if it consistently deviates from
the intended target by a fixed offset.
A welding robot must repeatedly reach the same welding spot within micrometer precision. If its
variance is within ±0.1 mm, the repeatability is high.
Explanation about precision of a robotic manipulator with relevant example. - 03 M
Precision refers to the smallest distinguishable change in position that the robot can execute. It is
limited by the resolution of the encoders and actuators.
If a robotic milling machine needs to engrave fine patterns, precision determines how finely it can
adjust its position to create intricate designs.

position. It is mathematically represented as the RMS error over multiple attempts to reach the same

b) Discusion about the configuration, features and applications of collaboartive robots. – 05 M
A cobot, or collaborative robot, is a robot intended for direct human-robot interaction within a shared
space, or where humans and robots are in close proximity. Cobot applications contrast with traditional
industrial robot applications in which robots are isolated from human contact.
Service robots could be considered to be cobots as they are intended to work alongside humans.
Industrial robots have traditionally worked separately from humans behind fences or other protective
barriers, but cobots remove that separation.
Cobots can have many uses, from information robots in public spaces (an example of service robots),
logistics robots that transport materials within a building, to industrial robots that help automate
unergonomic tasks such as helping people moving heavy parts, or machine feeding or assembly
operations.
Explanation about the four levels of collaboration defined by the International Federation of
Robotics. – 05 M
The IFR ( The international Federation of Robotics) defines four levels of collaboration between
industrial robots and human workers
Coexistence: Human and robot work alongside each other without a fence, but with no shared
workspace.
Sequential Collaboration: Human and robot are active in shared workspace but their motions are
sequential; they do not work on a part at the same time.
Cooperation: Robot and human work on the same part at the same time, with both in motion.
Responsive Collaboration: The robot responds in real-time to movement of the human worker.
