0   4   0   2   Marks Scaled to 50   ---
Pre-requisite: Basic knowledge of machine learning, neural networks, and deep learning
architectures.
Course Objective
The aim of the course is to make students understand the evolution of large language models (LLMs),
their tuning methodologies, innovations in their architectures, and ethical considerations involved in
developing AI systems.
Course Outcomes
After completion of the course, students will be able to –
1. Analyse the evolution and architecture changes of large language models, comparing their
performance and application landscapes
2. Evaluate different fine-tuning methodologies, including their advantages and disadvantages,
and determine their suitability for specific use cases
3. Assess the implications of quantizing LLMs and identify techniques to optimize model
performance while maintaining accuracy
4. Investigate innovative architectures in transformer models and propose new designs that can
optimize training or inference time, considering ethical and sustainable AI practices
Detailed Syllabus

Evaluate the different fine-tuning methodologies.
Case Study 2   Compare the differences, advantages, and disadvantages of each   12
approach.
Compare Constitutional AI with RLHF (Reinforcement Learning with
Case Study 3 Human Feedback). Explain how they differ in terms of methodology   12
and application.
Evaluate the quantization of LLMs. Analyze the available techniques
Case Study 4   for quantization and compare the performance of quantized models   12
with the original models.
Analyze innovative architectures in transformer models that can lead
to savings in training or inference time. Evaluate architectures like MoE
Case Study 5   from Mistral, and compare the performance of new architectures with   12
traditional ones. Propose a new architecture that could lead to further
savings in model performance.
