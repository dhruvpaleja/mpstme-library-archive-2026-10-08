Pre-requisite: Cybersecurity Fundamentals
Course Objective
Cyber threat intelligence helps organization to improve their response and detection capabilities to
deal with increasingly sophisticated advanced persistent threats. This course prepares students in
the tactical, operational, and strategic level cyber threat intelligence skills required to make security
teams better, threat hunting more accurate, incident response more effective, and organizations
more aware of the evolving threat landscape.
Course Outcomes
After completion of the course, the student will be able to -
1. Explain strategic, operational, and tactical cyber threat intelligence application & fundamentals
2. Perform threat modelling.
3. Collect data from various sources and analyse it to produce and disseminate cyber threat
intelligence
Detailed Syllabus
Unit   Description   Duration
1.   Introduction   05
Need for threat modelling and intelligence, Threat actors and their
motivation, technical,   tactical, operational, and strategic-level threat
intelligence, use and benefits of CTI in risk management, incident response
and other areas, traits of good CTI, intelligence cycles (TI life cycle, F3EAD),
TI program
2.   Threat Modelling   06
Introduction, various methods like STRIDE, DREAD PASTA, CVSS, Attack
Trees, Persona Non Grata, hTMM, Quantitative TMM, Trike, VAST
Modelling, and OCTAVE
3.   Threat Intelligence Frameworks and standards   06
Cyber Kill Chain, Diamond Model, Mitre ATT&CK, Pyramid of Pain, Traffic
Light Protocol, STIX, TAXII, OpenCTI, MISP
4.   Collection Sources   04
Open-Source Intelligence (OSINT), Human Intelligence (HUMINT), Cyber
Counterintelligence (CCI), Indicators of Compromise (IoCs), and malware
analysis.
5.   Analysis and Production of Intelligence   05
Logical Fallacies and Cognitive Biases, Common Cyber Threat Intelligence
Informal Fallacies, Statistical Data Analysis, Structured Analysis of
Competing Hypotheses (SACH), pivoting, similarity analysis etc.
6.   Dissemination and Attribution   04

Dissemination of Tactical, Operational and strategic information, heat maps,
runbook, and knowledge base creation, Cyber Threat Intelligence
Report, Types of Attribution, building an Attribution Model, Conducting
Attribution Assessments.

2. Kyle Wilhoit and Joseph Opacki, Operationalizing Threat Intelligence, Packt Publications, 2022.
Reference Books
1. Valentina Palacin, Practical Threat Intelligence and Data-Driven Threat Hunting: A hands-on guide
to threat hunting with the ATT&CK™ Framework and open source tools Packt Publications, 2021.
2. Aaron Roberts, Cyber Threat Intelligence: The No-Nonsense Guide for CISOs and Security Managers,
Apress Publications, 2021.
Laboratory Work
