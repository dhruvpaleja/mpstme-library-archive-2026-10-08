3   0   1   4   Marks Scaled to 50   Marks Scaled to 50
Pre-requisite: Soil Mechanics, Foundation Engineering, Advanced Geotechnical Engineering
Objectives:
1. To introduce the concepts of forensic geotechnical engineering
2. To investigate the foundation failures,
3. To assess the situation-based used of geotechnical instrumentation and monitoring
4. To evaluate the legal implications
Outcomes:
After completion of the course, students would be able to:
1. Identify the geotechnical failures and their causes
2. Analyse the causes of slope failures, trench excavations, dam failures and foundation failures
due to lateral movement of soil and rock masses
3. Assess the situation-based use of different geotechnical instrumentation and monitoring
techniques
4. Evaluate the legal implications of forensic studies
Detailed Syllabus: (per session plan)
Unit   Description   Duration
1   Introduction and overview:   10
Historical development of forensic geotechnical engineering, definition of
forensic engineer, types of damage, typical clients, legal process, examples of
failure, Conditions for failure, factors affecting geotechnical foundation
resistance, structural foundation resistance, applied load, structural actions,
development and testing of credible hypotheses, case study explaining the above
factors
2   Planning for forensic investigation:   05
Site investigation, non-destructive and destructive testing, document search
including project reports, plans, relevant codes, technical documents, case study
3   Forensic investigations for failures by lateral movements:   10
Typical causes for lateral movement of earth leading to slope failures and
landslides, rock falls, stability analysis by probabilistic back analysis approach,
allowable lateral movements for buildings, trench cave-in and dam failures
4   Forensic investigations for foundations:   10
Investigations for settlement of structure, classification of cracking damage,
analyzing causes of settlements, rehabilitation and retrofitting measures,
assessing changes in properties with time, analyzing the failures in expansive
soils, case study
5   Instrumentation and monitoring in geotechnical engineering:   05
Systematic approach to planning monitoring programs using geotechnical
instrumentation, sensing, details of monitoring instruments including pore-
pressure and corner mirror monitoring, general guidelines for use of
instrumentation and modelling strategies, overview of parametric and non-
parametric approaches, technical lags in the field instrumentation
