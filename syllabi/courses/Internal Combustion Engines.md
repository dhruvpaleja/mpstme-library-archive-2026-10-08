Program: B Tech Integrated (Mechanical Engineering)   Semester: IX
Course: Internal Combustion Engines   Code:
Teaching Scheme   Evaluation Scheme
Internal Continuous   Term End
Lecture   Practical   Tutorials   Assessment (ICA)   Examinations (TEE)
Hours per   Hours   Hours per   Credit
week   per week   week   (Marks - 50)   (Marks- 100)
3   0   0   3   Scaled to 50 marks   Scaled to 50 marks
Pre-requisite: Thermal Engineering
Course Objective
Objective of this course is to present a detailed knowledge of Internal Combustion Engine,
alternative fuels, engine testing and modern developments in I C Engines.
Course Outcomes
After completion of the course, the student will be able to -
1. Summaries working and performance of internal combustion engines through
thermodynamic cycles.
2. Analyze combustion phenomena in spark ignition and compression ignition engines and
factors influencing combustion chamber design.
3. Evaluate the performance of internal combustion engines, emission formation
mechanism, importance of alternate fuels and latest developments.
Detailed Syllabus
Unit   Description   Duration
1.   Introduction
Engine nomenclature, four stroke and two stroke engines, spark ignited
and compression ignited engines.
Air Standard Engine Cycles and Their Analysis
Brief revision of air standard cycles and their comparison, p-V and T-S
diagrams and description.   11
Fuel Air Cycles and Actual Cycles
Deviation from air-standard cycles and assumptions made. Effects of losses
due to variation of specific heat, chemical dissociation and number of
moles, comparison between air standard, fuel air cycles and actual cycles.
2.   Combustion in Spark Ignition Engines
Mixture requirements. Simple and modern carburetor. Stages of
combustion in spark ignition engines, effect of engine   variables   on
ignition lag and flame propagation.   06
Abnormal   combustion, knocking, engine   variables effecting knock,
surface ignition, spark ignition engine combustion chamber design
principles, types of spark ignition engine combustion chambers.
3.   Combustion in Compression Ignition Engines
Diesel injection systems. Swirl and squish, stages of combustion in
compression ignition engines, physical and chemical delay, factors
06
affecting delay, diesel knock, methods of controlling diesel knock,
compression ignition engine, combustion chambers, direct injection and
indirect injection chambers.

4.   Alternative Fuels for Internal Combustion Engines
Use of alcohol, vegetable oils, bio-diesel and bio-gas as alternative, fuel   03
blends, comparison with conventional fuels.
5.   Engine Cooling
Need for engine cooling, piston and cylinder temperature distribution,
03
parameters affecting engine heat transfer, air cooling and liquid cooling,
radiators.
6.   Engine Testing and Performance Evaluation
Engine performance parameters and their measurement, measuring
friction power, basic principle of a dynamometer, factors affecting the
06
performance and efficiency of spark ignition and compression ignition
engines, heat balance sheet, performance graphs as a function of engine
speed, numerical examples.
7.   Tailpipe Engine Emissions and Control
Major pollutants from spark ignition and compression ignition engines,
effect of engine variables on tail pipe emissions. Euro I, II and III norms.   04
emission control, three way catalytic converters, exhaust gas recirculation.
8.   Modern Developments in Internal Combustion Engines
Supercharging and turbo charging, petrol injection, multi point fuel
06
injection, common rail direct ignition and gasoline direct engines, stratified
charge engines, rotary engines and dual fuel and multi-fuel engines.
Total   45
