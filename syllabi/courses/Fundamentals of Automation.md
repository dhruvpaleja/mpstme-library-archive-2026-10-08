Pre-requisite: Design Thinking
Course Objective
This course is designed to introduce the fundamentals of automation and its importance in mechanical
engineering industries. This course helps the students to understand the basic concepts of automated
systems, including sensors, actuators, mechanisms, and fluid power elements used in manufacturing and
material handling. Students also develop an understanding of the integration of mechanical systems with
automation components and the role of experimentation and simulation in realizing automated
processes.
Course Outcomes
After completion of the course, students will be able to -
1. Explain fundamentals, applications, and types of automation drives in manufacturing
2. Identify sensors and actuators used in automated mechanical systems.
3. Analyze mechanisms and motion transfer used in mechanical automation systems.
4. Illustrate fluid power components and their role in automation systems.
5. Demonstrate automation concepts through experimentation and simulations.
Detailed Syllabus
Unit   Description   Duration
1   Introduction to Automation   04
Definition of automation, need for automation in industries, evolution of
automation, manual, semi-automatic and automatic systems, levels of
automation, advantages and limitations of automation.
Types and Applications   03
Applications of automation in manufacturing, types of automation drives –
mechanical, electrical, hydraulic, pneumatic, comparison of automation drives.
2   Basic Elements of an Automated System
Basic structure of automated system, input–process–output concept, sensors   05
and their role in automation, classification of sensors – contact and non-contact,
proximity sensors – inductive, capacitive, photoelectric, magnetic.
Principles and working
Basic principles of sensing, actuators used in mechanical automation, linear   03

3   Mechanical Automation and Mechanisms
Role of mechanisms in automation, motion transfer and motion conversion,   04
mechanisms used in automated machines, slider–crank mechanism, cam and
follower mechanism, Geneva mechanism, four-bar linkage.
Use Cases
Applications of mechanisms in assembly, packaging and indexing systems,   03
sequencing of mechanical operations using mechanisms.
